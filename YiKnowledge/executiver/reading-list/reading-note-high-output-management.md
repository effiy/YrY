# Reading Note — High Output Management

- **Author**: Andrew S. Grove
- **Source**: ISBN 978-0679762881 (Vintage, 1995)
- **Date Read**: 2026-08-10
- **Rating**: ★★★★★
- **Tags**: [management, leadership, productivity, okr, performance]

## 1. One-sentence core viewpoint

A manager's output is the output of their organization under their influence — the key to high output is high-leverage activities, effective meetings, and performance management through motivation and task-relevant maturity.

## 2. Key chapter summary

| Chapter | Core Argument | Key Evidence | Quoted Highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Breakfast Factory | Management is a production process; apply operations principles to knowledge work | Uses a breakfast factory analogy to explain production flow, inventory, and quality control | "The output of a manager is the output of the organizational units under his or her supervision or influence." |
| Leverage | Managerial leverage is the measure of output generated per unit of time | High-leverage activities (training, planning, decision-making) multiply team output; low-leverage activities (micromanaging, unnecessary interruptions) drain it | "To increase your output, increase the leverage of your activities." |
| Meetings | Meetings are the medium of managerial work; they are not an interruption | Two types: process-oriented (1on1, staff meetings, ops reviews) and mission-oriented (ad-hoc decision meetings) | "A meeting is nothing less than the medium through which managerial work is performed." |
| Decisions | Decisions should be made at the lowest competent level; peer-group decision-making produces better outcomes | Six-question decision framework: What needs to be decided? When? Who decides? Who consults? Who ratifies? Who informs? | "The ideal decision-making process is free discussion, clear decision, and full support." |
| Planning | Planning is about today's actions to produce tomorrow's results; strategy is the bridge between MTP and daily operations | MTP (Mid-Term Planning) maps strategy to objectives; OKRs align individual output to organizational goals | "Planning is not about what will be delivered tomorrow, but what must be done today to achieve tomorrow's results." |
| Task-Relevant Maturity | Management style should adapt to the employee's task-relevant maturity (TRM) | Low TRM → structured/task-oriented; Medium TRM → individual-oriented; High TRM → delegation/involvement | "The manager's job is to match the management style to the employee's task-relevant maturity." |
| Performance Appraisal | Performance review is the single highest-leverage activity a manager performs | Four-part review: assess performance, deliver the message, handle emotional response, focus on the future | "The fundamental purpose of a performance review is to improve the subordinate's performance." |
| Motivation | Motivation comes from within; the manager's job is to create an environment where it can flourish | Maslow's hierarchy applied to the workplace; training is the highest-leverage motivational tool | "The most important form of motivation is the feeling of achievement." |

## 3. Action implications

### What this team's work can borrow

- **OKR alignment**: Grove's OKR system maps directly to our quarterly OKR process — ensure every individual KR ladders up to a team objective
- **1on1 cadence**: Implement task-relevant maturity assessment to calibrate 1on1 frequency and style per team member
- **Meeting audit**: Classify all recurring meetings as process-oriented or mission-oriented; eliminate those without a clear type
- **Decision authority**: Use the six-question decision framework to clarify decision rights for cross-team initiatives

- [ ] Audit recurring meetings this week: classify each as process-oriented or mission-oriented
- [ ] Add TRM (task-relevant maturity) assessment to 1on1 template
- [ ] Review current OKRs against the "free discussion → clear decision → full support" framework
