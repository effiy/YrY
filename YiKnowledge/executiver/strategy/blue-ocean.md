# Blue Ocean Strategy

> **As a** strategist, **I want to** identify and create uncontested market space, **so that** we compete on new dimensions of value rather than fighting over a shrinking profit pool.

## Definition

Blue Ocean Strategy contrasts two market types:

| | Red Ocean | Blue Ocean |
|---|---|---|
| Market space | Existing, known | Created, unknown |
| Competition | Beat competitors | Make competitors irrelevant |
| Demand | Fight for existing demand | Create and capture new demand |
| Value-cost trade-off | Choose differentiation OR low cost | Pursue both simultaneously (value innovation) |

The core tool is the **Strategy Canvas** — a visual comparison of how competitors invest across key competing factors, plus your new value curve.

## Applicable scenarios

- Entering a crowded, commoditizing market
- Launching a new product category
- Finding growth when existing segments are saturated
- Responding to price wars by changing the basis of competition
- Strategic pivot: when "better, faster, cheaper" isn't working

## Design steps

### Step 1: Map the current strategy canvas

1. Identify 6–8 **key competing factors** that the industry currently competes on
2. Plot your company and top 2–3 competitors on each factor (scale 1–5)
3. This is the "as-is" red ocean map

Example for the CRM industry:

```
Factor              Us   SFDC  HubSpot  Zoho
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Price (low=good)     2     1       3      4
Feature breadth      4     5       3      3
Ease of use          3     2       4      3
Integration depth    4     5       3      2
Enterprise features  4     5       2      1
Analytics/AI         5     3       3      2
```

### Step 2: Apply the ERRC grid

The ERRC grid is how you create a new value curve:

| Action | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **E**liminate | Which factors the industry takes for granted should be eliminated? | Traditional CRM eliminated on-premise deployment |
| **R**educe | Which factors should be reduced well below the industry standard? | Zoho reduced price by simplifying the product |
| **R**aise | Which factors should be raised well above the industry standard? | HubSpot raised ease-of-use and inbound marketing |
| **C**reate | Which factors the industry has never offered should be created? | Notion created "all-in-one workspace" combining docs, wikis, projects |

### Step 3: Draw the new value curve

Plot the new curve after ERRC actions. The new curve should:
- **Diverge** from competitors' curves (not just "a bit better")
- Have a **compelling tagline** that captures the new value proposition
- Be **focused** — not trying to be best at everything

### Step 4: Check for a viable blue ocean

Three tests for a commercially viable blue ocean idea:

| Test | Question | Pass if... |
|---|---|---|
| **Utility** | Is there exceptional buyer utility? | Clear reason for non-customers to switch |
| **Price** | Is the price accessible to the mass of target buyers? | Price point attracts a large market |
| **Cost** | Can you deliver at the target cost and still profit? | Business model works at target price |

### Step 5: Validate with non-customers

Blue oceans often come from understanding **non-customers** — people who could use your category but don't. Three tiers:
1. **Soon-to-be** non-customers: using your product but ready to leave
2. **Refusing** non-customers: consciously chose against your category
3. **Unexplored** non-customers: never considered your category

## Key outputs

- Current strategy canvas (as-is)
- ERRC grid with 2–3 actions per quadrant
- New strategy canvas with divergent value curve
- Compelling tagline (1 sentence)
- Three-tier non-customer analysis
- Utility-Price-Cost viability assessment

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Better" strategy canvas | Your curve is higher on all factors — that's red ocean differentiation | Your curve must diverge: eliminate and create, not just raise |
| Ignoring cost | Value innovation requires both differentiation AND low cost | Run the cost test; can you deliver the new curve profitably? |
| ERRC without customer insight | Guessing what to eliminate/create | Ground in non-customer interviews and jobs-to-be-done |
| Blue ocean as a one-time exercise | First-mover advantage erodes as others copy | Plan for imitation; build barriers (brand, network effects, patents) |
| Creating a blue ocean nobody wants | Novelty ≠ utility | Validate buyer utility with prototypes before full investment |
