<h3 class="wp-block-heading">A startup claims it’s found a drug to make your blood young<strong></strong></h3>



<p><em>—Antonio Regalado</em></p>



<p>I knew I’d officially become a “longevity influencer” when a company called Generation Lab offered me the chance to write about—and even receive—their new rejuvenation treatment.</p>



<p>This wasn’t just any antiaging treatment, either. A company fact sheet says that it “blocks the systemic spread of aging in the bloodstream, reawakens the body’s own repair mechanism and restores health and youth to multiple tissues.”</p>



<p>The approach is based on research by Generation Lab’s irascible scientific founder, Irina Conboy. She found that joining the circulatory systems of old and young mice improved the old animals’ ability to heal from injury.</p>



<p>Conboy now says she has found a combination of two existing drugs that can produce youthful effects without the need for any bodily fluid exchange. But Generation Lab won’t reveal what the drugs are, making the proposition hard to take seriously.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">How to sign up for a virtual power plant—and decide whether you should<strong></strong></h3>



<p>Your thermostat may not look like a power plant. Neither does your electric vehicle, home battery, or HVAC system. But utility and energy companies increasingly want to treat them like one.</p>



<p>That’s the idea behind a <a href="https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/02/07/1087836/how-virtual-power-plants-are-shaping-tomorrows-energy-system/?utm_source=the_download&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=the_download.unpaid.engagement&amp;utm_term=*%7CSUBCLASS%7C*&amp;utm_content=*%7CDATE:m-d-Y%7C*">virtual power plant</a>, or VPP, a collection of household devices (such as smart thermostats, EV chargers, and solar panels) that a utility can control, usually by commanding them to draw less electricity during peak hours. In exchange, participants receive a discount on their energy bills and, in some cases, a signing bonus.</p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">The problem with thinking you’re part Neanderthal</h4>



<p>There’s a theory that many of us have an “inner Neanderthal.” The idea is that <em>Homo sapiens</em> and a cousin species once bred, leaving some people today with a trace of Neanderthal DNA.&nbsp;</p>



<p>This DNA is arguably the 21st century’s most celebrated discovery in human evolution. But in 2024, a pair of French geneticists called into question the theory’s very foundations.&nbsp;</p>



<p>They proposed that what scientists interpret as interbreeding could instead be explained by population structure—the way genes concentrate in smaller, isolated groups.</p>
