<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/28111505/ali-g-who-iz-i-header.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There are many absolutes in life: Water freezes at zero, we&#8217;re gonna die, time moves inexorably forward, and each </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/larry-charles-talks-about-larry-david-sacha-baron-cohen"><span style="font-weight: 400;">new Sacha Baron Cohen film will be met with calls for a boycott.</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> While that&#8217;s just how the universe works, at least one of these feels somehow avoidable.</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In keeping with these universal truths, the latest film from the 54-year-old comedian, who has chosen to dust off his aging chav character for </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ali</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">G:</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Who</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Iz</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">I?</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, is facing an energetic backlash in the U.K. A campaign by </span><a href="https://newscord.org/action/cinemas-refuse-ali-g"><span style="font-weight: 400;">the AI-powered media watchdog NewsCord</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is calling on prospective cinema chains worldwide to block the film rather than screen it to a presumably indifferent populace who likely weren&#8217;t salivating over the prospects of a new </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ali</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">G</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> film to begin with.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><a href="https://variety.com/2026/film/global/ali-g-movie-backlash-campaign-pulled-from-cinemas-1236845851/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">This comes from </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which reports that NewsCord claims to have fired off &#8220;more than 36,000 emails&#8221; to cinema chains worldwide to block </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ali</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">G</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. The film&#8217;s trailer, </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/ali-g-who-iz-i-trailer"><span style="font-weight: 400;">released on Tuesday by Amazon</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, has pissed off some social media users, who have taken exception to, among other things, Cohen&#8217;s use of a dreadlock wig under a Rastafarian hat. NewsCord&#8217;s campaign cites the long-standing condemnation by Black British comedians of Cohen&#8217;s lampooning of white culture appropriating Black culture as &#8220;an alibi for racism,&#8221; and also cites English author Jeanette Winterson, </span><a href="http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/programmes/newsnight/review/2952847.stm"><span style="font-weight: 400;">who told the BBC in 2003</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: &#8220;I feel I&#8217;m looking into the void when I listen to Ali G. I hate this stuff. I don&#8217;t even understand it. I don&#8217;t know what the difference is between him and the [Black] and white minstrels. I don&#8217;t get it.&#8221;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More recent criticisms, </span><a href="https://afuahirsch.substack.com/p/not-my-g?embedding_publication_id=1072407"><span style="font-weight: 400;">such as this one</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from Afua Hirsch, a British broadcaster and author of the 2018 book </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Brit(ish):</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">On</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Race,</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Identity</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">and</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Belonging</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, argue that Cohen&#8217;s brand of racial humor is misaligned with current Black trends. “Black people don’t dress like that anymore. Even white people imitating Black people don’t talk like that anymore,&#8221; she writes. &#8220;Sexualizing Black women and casually mocking working class women is not accepted anymore[&#8230;] Baron Cohen’s so-called ‘humor’ is a crude compound of all these things.”</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is waiting for a comment from Cohen, who definitely saw this development coming. In a post about the trailer for his latest film, </span><a href="https://x.com/SachaBaronCohen/status/2092237375338418610"><span style="font-weight: 400;">he wrote</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, “In an age when a single comedian’s career can be ended by a single un-PC joke, I thought I’d play it safe and bring this guy back.” For NewsCord&#8217;s part, they&#8217;ve managed to score a response from U.K.-based Picturehouse Cinemas, though </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> has noted that the chain, known for its focus on independent and foreign cinema, probably wasn&#8217;t going to bother with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ali G: Who</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Iz</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">I?</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in the first place.</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
