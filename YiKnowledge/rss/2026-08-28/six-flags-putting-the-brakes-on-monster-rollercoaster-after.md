<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/28095232/x2-header.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As the internet, phones that use the internet, and </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/grand-theft-auto-vi-extended-first-look-netflix-rockstar"><span style="font-weight: 400;">newfangled vidya games</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> pull attention from the scores of people who&#8217;d otherwise spend their broiling summer afternoons wandering around their nearest Six Flags, it&#8217;s no surprise that amusement parks would promote their most extreme rides as a draw. However, all promotion for X2, the steel &#8220;4D&#8221; coaster experience at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California, has come to a halt after two life-threatening injuries have renewed safety concerns about the coaster, </span><a href="https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/27/us/six-flags-rollercoaster-brain-injury-invs"><span style="font-weight: 400;">according to a recent CNN report.</span></a></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An investigation by the news service has uncovered two separate cases of X2 riders who landed in surgery for brain hemorrhages &#8220;typically associated with high-speed collisions,&#8221; with one currently in a coma, according to three attending neurosurgeons. &#8220;The severe brain injuries requiring emergent neurosurgical attention were the result of a traumatic rapid acceleration-deceleration event experienced while on the X2 ride,&#8221; they wrote in an email.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These horrific cases are the latest in a series of at least 70 incidents involving X2, according to Six Flags records from 2019 to 2022 obtained by CNN. The coaster, which sends riders screaming through a skydive, a backflip, </span><a href="https://coasterpedia.net/wiki/Raven_turn"><span style="font-weight: 400;">two &#8220;raven turns,&#8221;</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and a twisting front flip at a sustained speed of 76 mph, has reportedly been responsible for more than a dozen hospitalizations, two of which resulted in fatalities. (</span><a href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/six-flags-x2-coaster-closed-brain-injuries-1236683165/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Though </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Hollywood Reporter</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> notes</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> that Six Flags has maintained that of the two riders, both in their twenties, one had a &#8220;preexisting brain abnormality.&#8221;)</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">X2, which has been in operation since 2002, has been closed since July 12. As far as culpability goes, Six Flags maintains that X2&#8217;s routine safety testing meets industry standards and that the ride&#8217;s G-force—clocked at 4Gs—isn&#8217;t sufficient to threaten riders&#8217; internal wiring if riders use X2 &#8220;as instructed.&#8221; The park&#8217;s legal team claims negligence was at the root of one injury. But future riders who have their lives abruptly ruined by their Six Flags experience shouldn&#8217;t worry that their suffering will be in vain; according to CNN, the park has offered the rider currently in a coma and her family a presumably free meal from McDonald&#8217;s.</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
