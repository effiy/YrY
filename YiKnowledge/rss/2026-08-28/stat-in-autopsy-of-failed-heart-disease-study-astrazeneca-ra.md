<p class="wp-block-paragraph">AstraZeneca on Friday detailed the surprise failure of a closely watched heart disease clinical trial, laying out how its experimental drug offered no additional benefits for patients who were largely already on another type of medicine &#x2014; the <a href="https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/27/alnylam-amvuttra-nucresiran-heart-drugs-esc/">latest twist in a multibillion-dollar market</a>.&#xa0;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The study indicated that patients with a progressive condition known as ATTR-CM who received AstraZeneca&#x2019;s drug &#x2014; what&#x2019;s called a silencer &#x2014; saw no improvements if they were already taking another type of drug called a stabilizer, an effect so powerful that it tanked the whole Phase 3 study.&#xa0;Most participants were on a stabilizer at the outset of the trial.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The trial&#x2019;s miss has had implications not just for AstraZeneca and its development partner Ionis Pharmaceuticals, but for other companies that market ATTR-CM drugs, including Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, which sells another silencer, and BridgeBio, the maker of a new stabilizer. Analysts have said the study&#x2019;s outcome bolstered the case that stabilizers, which are oral drugs, were perhaps superior to injectable silencers.&#xa0;</p><p><a href="https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/28/astrazeneca-ionis-wainua-eplontersen-attr-cm-study-failure-bridgebio-alnylam/?utm_campaign=rss">Continue to STAT+ to read the full story&hellip;</a></p>
