<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/28141816/feature-copy.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It’s been </span><a href="https://variety.com/2026/film/features/politics-film-festivals-venice-russia-israel-discourse-1236841779/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">a rough season </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">for organizers of some of the world’s most prestigious film festivals, as many artists are calling for greater political involvement in the face of growing political and humanitarian crises around the globe. The latest event facing pressure to take a stand on something other than artistic quality is the venerable Venice Film Festival, which received </span><a href="https://venice4palestine.org/appello/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">an open letter</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from various high-profile figures this week demanding action on behalf of the Palestinian people.</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pink Floyd’s Roger Waters, a vocal and </span><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/sep/28/roger-waters-accused-of-repeated-antisemitism-in-new-documentary"><span style="font-weight: 400;">sometimes controversial critic</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of the Israeli government’s actions in Gaza, joined Nobel Prize Winning novelist Annie Ernaux (</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Les Années)</span></i> , <span style="font-weight: 400;">directors Matteo Garrone (</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Gommorah</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">) and Isabel Coixet (</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Three Goodbyes</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">), and actors <a href="https://www.avclub.com/alia-shawkat-you-got-older-theater-interview">Alia Shawkat</a> and <a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-brutalist-guy-pearce-best-supporting-character-of-the-year">Guy Pearce</a> among others in calling for what they termed “concrete action in support of Palestine.” Steps called for in the letter include flying the Palestinian flag to honor the two Palestinian films invited to this year’s festival (Ahmed Hassouna’s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Citizen Osama</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and Muayad Alayan’s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Conversation with the Sea)</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, for the festival to cut ties with &#8220;organizations and institutions associated with the Israeli government,&#8221; and &#8220;the introduction of safeguards for professionals who choose to dissociate themselves from such ties.&#8221;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The controversies mounting in advance of Venice’s September 2 start date mirror those faced by both the Berlin and Cannes festivals earlier this year, where conflicts arose over everything from Israel and Russia to right-wing control of media to the rise of A.I.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Neither organizers Venice Biennale nor the Venice Film Festival have yet commented on the letter from the Venice4Palestine collective, but while organizers have always touted the non-political nature of the annual festival, this year’s film roster includes projects certain to generate some controversy about the ongoing conflict in Gaza. In addition to Hassouna and Alayan’s films, the 83rd Venice Film Festival will include screenings of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">NAZA</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the new documentary from </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/no-other-land-years-most-vital-political-documentary-palestine-distribution"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">No Other Land</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, described as an&nbsp; exposé of &#8220;the systems behind Israel’s calculated mass killing of Palestinian civilians in Gaza.&#8221; Ben Hania’s </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-voice-of-hind-rajab-review"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Voice Of Hind Rajab</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a wrenching dramatization of the emergency call received from 5-year-old Palestinian girl after her family’s car was destroyed by Israeli bombing during the 2024 invasion of Gaza City, received a record-breaking 23-minute standing ovation at last year’s festival.&nbsp; </span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
