<p>To hear <a href="https://www.thewrap.com/jerry-seinfeld-curb-your-enthusiasm-finale-reaction/">Jerry Seinfeld</a> tell it, he had a major hand in American Express&#8217; creation of its Centurion Card (widely known as the Black Card) in 1999. </p>



<p>As the story goes, when Seinfeld was a spokesperson for American Express in the 1990s, a member of a commercial&#8217;s crew asked him if he owned an AmEx Black Card. Not knowing what it was but thinking it was about time he did, he called up the company&#8217;s president at the time, Harvey Golub, and said that he wanted one &#8212; but it turned out the coveted card for the ultra-rich and famous was just a rumor at that time.</p>



<p>When it was made a reality, Seinfeld said he was the first person to be gifted one. </p>



<p>The iconic comedian first broke out the story on his Netflix&#8217;s talk series <a href="https://www.thewrap.com/jerry-seinfeld-emerges-victorious-in-comedians-in-cars-getting-coffee-lawsuit/">&#8220;Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee&#8221;</a> with guest John Mulaney in 2018. But in a recent <a href="https://x.com/thirdmetax/status/2093354900109308385">live audience interview with comic and actor Sebastian Maniscalco</a> to launch his &#8220;We Got Company!&#8221; podcast, Seinfeld added another layer to the tale.</p>



<p>“I was on the set, we’re shooting a commercial, and one of the guys on the crew slides up next to me and whispers, &#8216;Do you have the Black Card?&#8217; I go, &#8216;Well, what’s that?&#8217; He goes, &#8216;There’s supposed to be a secret Black Card, and only the Sultan of Brunei and the president of American Express has it. And I thought maybe you had it too.&#8217; </p>



<p>“I go, &#8216;No, I don’t have it. But I’m gonna get it,'&#8221; Seinfeld continued. &#8220;And, the next morning, I called the president of American Express, Harvey Golub at the time, a nice Jewish guy from Brooklyn, and I said, &#8216;What about the Black Card?&#8217; He says, &#8216;It’s just a rumor, it’s never existed.&#8217; I go, &#8216;Let’s do it, let’s make the Black Card.&#8217; He goes, &#8216;Let me look into it.&#8217; And that’s how it happened.”</p>



<p>When Maniscalco asked Seinfeld to confirm that he was indeed the first person to be presented the American Express Black Card, the comic shared a coda:</p>



<p>“I had the first one, and I threw it out,&#8221; Seinfeld said, laughing. &#8220;I don’t know what happened to it. But he gave it to me in this nice padded thing, and it was the first one, and I don’t have it anymore.”</p>



<p>For those hoping to follow in Seinfeld&#8217;s footsteps and also become an AmEx Black Card holder, <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2018/09/11/the-american-express-black-card-may-exist-thanks-to-jerry-seinfeld.html">CNBC previously detailed</a> in its own account that the main thing needed for such access is a &#8220;well-padded bank account.&#8221; Kim Kardashian and Oprah Winfrey, the outlet reported, are also said to be among the card&#8217;s elite holders. </p>
