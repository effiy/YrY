<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Actors spend years building careers around performances that can become deeply associated with their public image, so admitting that something did not work takes a certain amount of confidence. Some have gone much further, happily parodying their biggest roles, making jokes about notorious flops, or appearing in projects that openly poke fun at their own careers. Instead of protecting every part of their filmography, these performers have shown they can laugh when things get awkward. That willingness can make them even more likable, especially when audiences already know the joke. These fifteen actors have all demonstrated that they are perfectly comfortable turning the spotlight back on themselves and having a little fun with their own work.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021732" height="452" width="678" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">George Clooney</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">George Clooney has repeatedly joked about some of his career choices, especially <em>Batman &amp; Robin</em> (1997), openly calling the movie a terrible experience and even apologizing for it while accepting awards years later.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Daniel Radcliffe</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Daniel Radcliffe has never been precious about his post <em>Harry Potter</em> career, happily appearing in deliberately strange projects and joking about some of his more unusual performances, including the bizarre <em>Swiss Army Man</em> (2016).</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021733" height="415" width="739" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Arnold Schwarzenegger</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Arnold Schwarzenegger has spent decades parodying his own action hero image, including appearing as an exaggerated version of himself in <em>Last Action Hero</em> (1993) and repeatedly joking about his famous one liners and accent.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021731" height="407" width="754" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Nicolas Cage</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nicolas Cage has embraced the internet&#8217;s fascination with his most eccentric performances, openly discussing movies such as <em>The Wicker Man</em> (2006) and even recreating the infamous &#8220;Not the bees!&#8221; moment during interviews and appearances.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021734" height="639" width="480" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Ben Affleck</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ben Affleck has been remarkably willing to discuss his career&#8217;s rough patches, including his disappointment with <em>Daredevil</em> (2003), while also joking about the movie during later interviews and appearances.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021736" height="452" width="678" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Matt Damon</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Matt Damon famously appeared in <em>Team America: World Police</em> (2004) as a deliberately ridiculous version of himself, reducing his entire persona to repeatedly shouting his own name in one of the film&#8217;s most memorable jokes.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021737" height="369" width="738" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Samuel L. Jackson</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Samuel L. Jackson has happily leaned into parodying his enormous filmography, appearing as an exaggerated version of himself and allowing movies and shows to joke about his tendency to appear in almost everything.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021738" height="354" width="867" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Hugh Jackman</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Hugh Jackman used <em>Deadpool 2</em> (2018) and later <em>Deadpool &amp; Wolverine</em> (2024) to play with his Wolverine image, embracing jokes about the character, his costume, and his many years associated with the role.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021739" height="452" width="678" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Ryan Reynolds</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ryan Reynolds has made self parody a major part of his public persona, frequently joking about his own movies, acting choices, and superhero career while turning the same humor into a defining part of Deadpool.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021740" height="452" width="678" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Robert Pattinson</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Robert Pattinson has openly joked about some of his earlier performances and the strange experience of becoming associated with <em>Twilight</em> (2008), often poking fun at the franchise and his own image from that period.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021742" height="750" width="564" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Jamie Lee Curtis</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Jamie Lee Curtis has never been afraid to revisit her horror movie past with a sense of humor, including appearing in projects that knowingly play with her status as one of the genre&#8217;s most recognizable stars.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021743" height="1024" width="635" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Michael Keaton</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Michael Keaton has happily revisited his superhero history, particularly through <em>Birdman</em> (2014), a movie that deliberately plays with the idea of an actor struggling with being permanently associated with a superhero role.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021741" height="1024" width="783" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Neil Patrick Harris</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Neil Patrick Harris turned his wholesome <em>Doogie Howser, M.D.</em> image upside down by playing an exaggerated, morally questionable version of himself in the <em>Harold &amp; Kumar</em> movies, making his own celebrity persona part of the joke.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021744" height="453" width="678" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Christopher Mintz Plasse</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Christopher Mintz Plasse has embraced the way audiences associate him with awkward comedy roles, particularly his breakout as McLovin in <em>Superbad</em> (2007), and has repeatedly joked about the character becoming more famous than many of his later roles.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="" class="js-lazy wp-image-1021745" height="414" width="738" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">IMDb</figcaption></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Daniel Craig</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Daniel Craig has shown a surprisingly playful attitude toward his most famous role, happily appearing in sketches and interviews that poke fun at his James Bond image, his serious screen persona, and even the physical demands of playing 007.</p>
