<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/27140743/MixCollage-27-Aug-2026-02-05-PM-7784.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nanny McPhee said that she&#8217;d be around when we need her but do not want her. After this week, it&#8217;s probably fair to say that people both </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">need</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and want a magical British nanny, and Emma Thompson has answered the call. This morning, it was announced that </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nanny McPhee </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">would return—now as a musical—to the London stage. Thompson, who wrote the screenplay for the 2005 film that introduced the character to a younger generation, will also write the musical. Thompson will write the show&#8217;s book and collaborate on the lyrics with the musician Gary Clark of the band Danny Wilson.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I’ve lived with Nanny McPhee for so long that to see her finally tread the boards is supremely exciting. Theater feels like her natural home — magic, music, mischief and a good dollop of danger can all happen right in front of you,&#8221; said Thompson in </span><a href="https://variety.com/2026/legit/global/nanny-mcphee-musical-emma-thompson-london-1236844871/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">a statement to </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. &#8220;I can’t wait to introduce the all-new Nanny McPhee to audiences next summer.&#8221; This represents Thompson&#8217;s first major piece of theater writing, though she&#8217;s written a number of screenplays already, including for the 1995 film </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sense And Sensibility</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and co-write 2016&#8217;s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bridget Jones&#8217;s Baby</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nanny McPhee</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> musical will presumably be </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/nanny-mcphee-1798201409"><span style="font-weight: 400;">based mostly on the 2005 film</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (which we commented at the time positions itself nicely as the anti-</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mary Poppins</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">), though that movie is based on the character from the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nurse Matilda </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">books, which were published from 1964 until 1974. Like </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mary Poppins</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">—another successful book to film to stage adaptation—the story follows Nanny McPhee as she comes to work at a home full of unruly children, though her story is set further back in time with more children and includes de-glamming makeup than you&#8217;re likely to find on a Julie Andrews character. The musical is slated to open in London next summer, and should be a success, perhaps Nanny McPhee can clean up some messes Stateside by the decade&#8217;s end.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
