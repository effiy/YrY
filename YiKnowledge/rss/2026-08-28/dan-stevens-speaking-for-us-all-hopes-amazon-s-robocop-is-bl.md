<p><a href="https://comicbook.com/tv-shows/news/dan-stevens-breaks-silence-on-robocop-casting-and-what-he-wants-to-preserve-from-original-movie-exclusive/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Speaking with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ComicBook</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> about his new steel-plated gig</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, still in early pre-production, Stevens could only share his enduring appreciation for Verhoeven&#8217;s sharp-witted, wildly violent film, which he hopes this new series will emulate with a similar dollar-buying, squib-bursting elan. &#8220;Oh my god,&#8221; he said, &#8220;that original is one of my favorite films of all time. For me and so many of my friends, it was such an iconic movie; I think maybe one of [our] first exposures to extreme violence and also satire. I think that’s what I’m most looking forward to, hopefully preserving those elements.&#8221;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s worth noting that previous attempts to further the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboCop</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> brand have often resulted in toothless entries that sanitized both the satire of Verhoeven&#8217;s film and its zeal for wanton carnage. After a </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboCop</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> animated series premiered in 1988, a more kid-friendly approach was taken with </span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Se4jYZTZAhk"><span style="font-weight: 400;">the groin-decimating hero cop</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboCop</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">3</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, followed by the animated </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alpha</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Commando</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in 1998 and two anodyne live-action TV series in 1994 and 2001. In 2014, José Padilha revived the franchise with Joel Kinnaman in a soft-serve PG-13 affair that </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/it-s-a-good-and-bad-thing-that-the-new-robocop-is-not-t-1798179474"><span style="font-weight: 400;">waved its prime directives at the topical subject of drone warfare</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, but it remains memorable mostly for falling well short of the clever, vicious heights of the original.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That aside, it&#8217;s worthwhile to share Stevens&#8217; enthusiasm for the new series, since it&#8217;s being run by folks who don&#8217;t shy away from the gnarly, smart stuff: James Wan&#8217;s Atomic Monster gave us the magnificently messy </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Malignant</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, after all, and it previously dabbled in the violent robot subgenre with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">M3GAN</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Showrunner Peter Ocko&#8217;s previous series, the critically adored </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lodge</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">49</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, while more big-hearted than one might expect a </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboCop</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> series to be, has some Verhoeven embedded in it, with the show&#8217;s </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/lodge-49-reaches-zugwang-its-funniest-and-most-excit-1838637164"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omni Corp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> serving as a cute nod to the 1987 film. Maybe a thrilling, amusing, dare we say shocking synthesis of Stevens&#8217; engrossingly strange performance work, Wan&#8217;s enthusiasm for horror, and Ocko&#8217;s mindful sensibilities will emerge in this new </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboCop</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">? Until we have a trailer to look at, all we can do is ponder the permutations. As a wise robot cop once said, &#8220;We&#8217;re only human.&#8221;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
