<p>We may earn a commission from links on this page.</p><p>You can&rsquo;t turn off Strava&rsquo;s AI summaries anymore. Sometime in July or August, the feature, called &ldquo;Athlete Intelligence,&rdquo; became a built-in part of premium subscriptions. If you have a free account, you still won&rsquo;t see it, but if you subscribe to Strava Premium, you&rsquo;ll now have the summaries stuck onto all of your workouts.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="what-is-stravas-athlete-intelligence">What is Strava's "Athlete Intelligence"?</h2><p>Athlete Intelligence, conveniently abbreviated AI, is a Strava feature that gives text responses to your workouts. It&rsquo;s only available for premium subscribers. Strava refers to &ldquo;insights&rdquo; in these responses, but as far as I can tell, they just seem to regurgitate the information from the workout description, sometimes with added errors.&nbsp;</p><p>I used the feature briefly, around the time it launched (I was a premium subscriber then), but quickly turned it off when I realized it wasn&rsquo;t telling me anything interesting. I asked my colleague Meredith Dietz, who has a premium subscription, whether she had seen anything useful in her Athlete Intelligence. She said, &ldquo;They&rsquo;re usually positive, so that&rsquo;s nice. But ultimately: useless.&rdquo;&nbsp;</p><p>Meredith has written more about <a href="https://lifehacker.com/health/why-ai-fitness-summaries-are-mostly-useless" target="_blank"><u>why AI fitness summaries are mostly useless</u></a>, and Strava&rsquo;s AI checks every box: it doesn&rsquo;t use enough context to understand what&rsquo;s going on, it presents generic advice as if it were personal, and it&rsquo;s overconfident in its incomplete data. She showed me a recent Athlete Intelligence summary that said a run was both a &ldquo;recovery run&rdquo; and included her &ldquo;fastest pace in two weeks.&rdquo; Those are simply not compatible statements. Another message complimented her &ldquo;solid half-marathon effort&rdquo; on a run that Meredith says was &ldquo;not even close to a half-marathon effort.&rdquo;&nbsp;</p><h2 id="what-happened-behind-the-scenes">What happened behind the scenes?&nbsp;</h2><p>You <em>used</em> to be able to turn off the AI. When Strava <a href="https://press.strava.com/articles/stravas-athlete-intelligence-translates-workout-data-into-simple-and" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>launched</u></a> Athlete Intelligence in October 2024, it was a &ldquo;beta&rdquo; feature available to premium users only. Since then&mdash;as far as I can remember&mdash;Strava&rsquo;s <a href="https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401629-athlete-intelligence-on-strava" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>support page</u></a> had instructions for opting out of Athlete Intelligence.&nbsp;</p><p>But that changed recently. The last Wayback Machine snapshot that included those instructions is from June 30, 2026. A snapshot from Aug. 4 removed both the question and answer about opting out. On Aug. 26, I noticed a <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Strava/comments/1vyycog/there_isnt_an_option_to_opt_out_of_athlete/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>Reddit thread</u></a> where users were wondering where the opt-out feature went. The support page currently says, &ldquo;There isn&rsquo;t an option to opt out of Athlete Intelligence.&rdquo;&nbsp;</p><p>I asked what happened, and received an answer from a Strava spokesperson: &ldquo;As was standard practice for experimental features, Athlete Intelligence launched with a "turn off" mechanism scoped to its initial beta period. Given the feature&rsquo;s formal exit from beta, that mechanism is no longer available. We invite all subscribers to tap Give Feedback to help us improve the insights they see.&rdquo;</p><p>Athlete Intelligence officially <a href="https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-enhances-subscriber-experience-with-updates-to-key-features" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>left beta</u></a> in February of last year. If the support page was kept up to date over the subsequent year and a half, then it seems the opt-out was removed much later. (The instructions used to say to click &ldquo;leave beta&rdquo; and then changed to &ldquo;leave Athlete Intelligence&rdquo; sometime before June 21 this year.) I asked Strava for clarification on the timeline, and got the answer: &ldquo;Thanks for the follow up, we're not able to provide additional information.&rdquo;</p><h2 id="why-athlete-intelligence-has-been-such-a-disappointment">Why &ldquo;Athlete Intelligence&rdquo; has been such a disappointment</h2><p>Strava claimed in its February 2025 announcement that over 80% of users found Athlete Intelligence useful. That seems hard to believe, given the weaknesses that both Meredith and I noticed in our own AI summaries. For all my browsing of Strava user forums, I&rsquo;ve often seen people complaining about the AI&rsquo;s tone or pointing out its mistakes; I&rsquo;m really not seeing anyone praising it for being insightful or for helping to analyze or plan training.&nbsp;</p><p>Meredith finds the summaries easy to ignore. I found they annoyed me, so I hid them while it was still possible to do so. So far I&rsquo;m not seeing anybody who opted out complaining that the summaries are back; it&rsquo;s possible they will remain hidden. But the option to turn them off is no longer available. You can still &ldquo;leave feedback&rdquo; on a given response, but there used to be an option to leave Athlete Intelligence altogether. That option is no longer there, and given Strava&rsquo;s statement, it doesn&rsquo;t seem likely it will come back.</p>
