<p><a href="https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1cg67JW-8_9BnObb2v3yU5jJdpDDIZMC6/edit?usp=drivesdk&amp;ouid=118328836220657927964&amp;rtpof=true&amp;sd=true">New data compiled</a> by a Bay Area anti-surveillance advocacy group shows that not only are American localities <a href="https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/as-wisconsin-cities-flee-flock-its-shared-camera-network-loses-value/">dropping cameras from Flock Safety,</a> but they are doing it at an accelerating rate.</p>
<p>Communities from Lansing, Michigan, to Pflugerville, Texas, are ending their relationship with Flock, largely over concerns regarding out-of-control surveillance, unwanted data sharing, high expenses, and <a href="https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/flock-cant-tech-its-way-out-of-the-stalker-cop-problem-experts-say/">reports of police abusing the tool</a>.</p>
<p><a href="http://secure-justice.org/">Secure Justice</a>, an Oakland-based advocacy group, has <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/securejustice.bsky.social/post/3mu3zxl7qw624">recorded 214 cities and counties that have dropped Flock since 2021</a>. Of those, 90 ended their relationship with Flock in August 2026 alone, a fourfold increase compared to the previous month.</p><p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/cities-terminate-flock-contracts-at-record-pace-in-august/">Read full article</a></p>
