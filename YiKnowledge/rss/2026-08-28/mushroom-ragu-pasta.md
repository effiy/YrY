<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-mushroom-ragu-pasta">Mushroom Ragu Pasta</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mushroom Ragu is exactly what it sounds like &ndash; a rich savoury sauce that is built on mushrooms. It&rsquo;s hearty and comforting like a <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/slow-cooked-shredded-beef-ragu-pasta/" id="8097" type="post">slow cooked beef ragu</a>, but made with earthy mushrooms instead of meat. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The recipe I&rsquo;m sharing today is actually based on the Mushroom Lasagna recipe I created for Nagi&rsquo;s first cookbook,&nbsp;<em><a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/cookbooks/dinner-cookbook/" id="149692" type="page">Dinner</a></em>. It became one of the signature recipes in the book, and I&rsquo;ve always thought the mushroom ragu was too good to live only between layers of lasagna! So today, I&rsquo;m giving that ragu a recipe of its own, tossed through pasta.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">What I like about this one is that like all ragus, it tastes like it&rsquo;s been quietly simmering away on the stove for hours with an almost meaty flavour, but really, once your ingredients are prepped, the whole thing comes together in well under an hour.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu" class="wp-image-202330" height="1500" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Mushroom-ragu-sauce-0.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-ingredients-in-mushroom-ragu-pasta">Ingredients in Mushroom Ragu Pasta</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I use anchovies and chicken rather than vegetable stock to bring out the full potential of the mushrooms. But to make it vegetarian, simply use vegetable stock instead and an extra 1/4 cup of parmesan instead of the anchovies. If you don&rsquo;t eat parmesan and want to go fully vegan, stir through 1/4 cup of nutritional yeast instead for that same salty, cheesy depth.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-1-the-mushrooms">1. The Mushrooms</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - ingredients" class="wp-image-202337" height="857" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Mushroom-Ragu-Ingredients-1.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Cup mushroom &ndash;</strong> Also known as white button mushrooms. A whole kilo might sound like a lot, but they&rsquo;re the main part of the ragu, and they cook down significantly, so you need that volume to start with. You could also use Swiss Mushrooms (cremini). You could really make this with any mushrooms, even wild mushrooms, just chop and slice so you get a similar texture to mine.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Dried porcini mushroom &ndash;</strong> Small but important ingredient here. Porcinis are prized for their strong, earthy mushroom flavour and just a small amount (20 grams) brings a lot of savouriness to the sauce. You can find them at some large grocery stores (I got mine from Coles), and fresh produce stores like Harris Farms here in Sydney where I live. Nagi said she&rsquo;s also bought them from Aldi.</p><p><strong>If you cannot find them</strong>, substitute with dried shiitake or other dried mushrooms instead (like chanterelle and wild mushrooms) though I would use double to make up for the difference in  strength of mushroom flavour.</p></li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-2-everything-else-for-ragu">2. everything else for ragu</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-202420" height="857" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Mushroom-Ragu-Ingredients.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Red wine &ndash; </strong>Brings flavours into this sauce, both through soaking the porcini and later deglazing the pot. As usual, I like to use Pinot Noir, it&rsquo;s light and fruity without being too tannic. That said, don&rsquo;t feel like you need to reach for anything fancy, a cheap bottle works just as well since the wine cooks down and its flavour concentrates and changes anyway, so save the good stuff for drinking alongside dinner. &#128522;</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Anchovy &ndash;</strong> Also known as &ldquo;umami bomb&rdquo;, they melt away into the base. It doesn&rsquo;t make the dish fishy, just brings a savoury depth underneath everything. As I mentioned above, if you don&rsquo;t eat anchovies or you want to skip them, just replace with an extra 1/4 cup parmesan.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Tomato paste &ndash;</strong> Cooked until it lightly caramelises, this is another key flavour builder in this dish</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Chicken stock &ndash;</strong> Adds savouriness to the sauce without masking the flavour of the mushrooms. If you have <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/chicken-stock-recipe/" id="68752" type="post">homemade chicken stock</a>, even better, it&rsquo;ll take the sauce to another level and make your Mushroom Ragu restaurant-quality. You can also use vegetable stock if you prefer, though the sauce does lose a bit of the depth of flavour that only meat-based stocks can bring to dishes.</p><p>I don&rsquo;t recommend using powdered chicken stock, it wouldn&rsquo;t do justice to a dish like this. </p></li>



<li><p><strong>Parmesan cheese &ndash; </strong>Adds saltiness and richness into the sauce.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Onion and garlic &ndash;</strong> Aromatic base every good ragu starts with.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Flour &ndash;</strong> Plain / all-purpose flour, your thickener here.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Thyme and bay leaves &ndash;</strong> Classic herb aromatics combo.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Olive oil and butter &ndash;</strong> Used together for cooking, and unsalted butter comes back again at the end for that final glossy finish.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Salt and pepper &ndash;</strong> Cooking / kosher salt and black pepper.</p></li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-3-pasta">3. pasta</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I like using mafaldine pasta, that lovely long flat pasta with curls and ridges that the sauce grabs onto beautifully. But you can use any pasta you like though long thing pastas like spaghetti and linguine because the sauce won&rsquo;t hold onto it as well. Short pastas like orecchiette or fusilli (spiral pasta) would work better.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And don&rsquo;t forget to salt the pasta cooking water!</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - ingredients" class="wp-image-202339" height="857" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Mushroom-Ragu-Ingredients-3.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-make-mushroom-ragu-pasta">How To Make Mushroom Ragu Pasta</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Almost&nbsp;every&nbsp;step&nbsp;here&nbsp;is&nbsp;about&nbsp;concentrating&nbsp;flavour&nbsp;through&nbsp;reduction.&nbsp;You&rsquo;re&nbsp;reducing&nbsp;the&nbsp;wine,&nbsp;reducing&nbsp;the&nbsp;tomato&nbsp;paste&nbsp;down&nbsp;until&nbsp;it&nbsp;caramelizes,&nbsp;reducing&nbsp;the&nbsp;mushroom&nbsp;liquid&nbsp;until&nbsp;it&rsquo;s&nbsp;nearly&nbsp;gone.&nbsp;Nothing&nbsp;is&nbsp;rushed,&nbsp;but once you start cooking, it takes less than 30 minutes but gives&nbsp;you&nbsp;a&nbsp;sauce&nbsp;that&nbsp;tastes&nbsp;like&nbsp;it&rsquo;s&nbsp;been&nbsp;simmering&nbsp;for&nbsp;hours like a <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/slow-cooked-shredded-beef-ragu-pasta/" id="8097" type="post">Beef Ragu</a>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Use a food processor to slice and chop the mushrooms if you are feeling lazy (Nagi does it). (Nagi here: ONLY SOMETIMES!!! &#128517;)</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - steps" class="wp-image-202335" height="335" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Mushroom-Ragu-1.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Soak&nbsp;and&nbsp;prep&nbsp;the&nbsp;porcini &ndash;</strong>&nbsp;Soak&nbsp;dried&nbsp;porcini&nbsp;in&nbsp;red&nbsp;wine&nbsp;for&nbsp;thirty&nbsp;minutes.&nbsp;This&nbsp;softens&nbsp;them&nbsp;and&nbsp;lets&nbsp;them&nbsp;release&nbsp;some&nbsp;flavour&nbsp;straight&nbsp;into&nbsp;the&nbsp;wine&nbsp;you&rsquo;ll&nbsp;use&nbsp;later,&nbsp;so&nbsp;nothing&nbsp;is&nbsp;wasted.&nbsp;Once&nbsp;softened,&nbsp;strain&nbsp;them,&nbsp;gently squeeze&nbsp;out&nbsp;the&nbsp;excess&nbsp;wine,&nbsp;and&nbsp;finely&nbsp;chop&nbsp;the&nbsp;porcini.&nbsp;Set&nbsp;both&nbsp;mushrooms&nbsp;and&nbsp;reserved&nbsp;wine&nbsp;aside.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Build&nbsp;the&nbsp;base &ndash;</strong>&nbsp;Heat&nbsp;olive&nbsp;oil&nbsp;and&nbsp;melt the butter&nbsp;in&nbsp;a&nbsp;large,&nbsp;heavy-based&nbsp;pot&nbsp;over&nbsp;medium&nbsp;high&nbsp;heat. I use a 26cm/10.5&Prime; pot (it needs to be large for the volume of mushrooms). Then&nbsp;add&nbsp;the&nbsp;onion,&nbsp;garlic&nbsp;and&nbsp;anchovy.&nbsp;Cook&nbsp;for&nbsp;about&nbsp;two&nbsp;minutes,&nbsp;just&nbsp;until&nbsp;the&nbsp;onion&nbsp;starts&nbsp;to&nbsp;soften.&nbsp;The&nbsp;anchovy&nbsp;will&nbsp;kinda melt&nbsp;away&nbsp;here,&nbsp;you&nbsp;won&rsquo;t&nbsp;taste&nbsp;it&nbsp;as&nbsp;fish,&nbsp;just&nbsp;a&nbsp;savoury&nbsp;depth&nbsp;underneath&nbsp;everything&nbsp;else.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - steps" class="wp-image-202334" height="344" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Mushroom-Ragu-1-copy.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="3">
<li><p><strong>Cook&nbsp;tomato&nbsp;paste &ndash;</strong>&nbsp;Stir&nbsp;in&nbsp;tomato&nbsp;paste&nbsp;and&nbsp;cook&nbsp;it&nbsp;for&nbsp;about&nbsp;two&nbsp;minutes&nbsp;until&nbsp;it&nbsp;darkens&nbsp;slightly&nbsp;and&nbsp;lightly&nbsp;caramelises.&nbsp;This&nbsp;step&nbsp;matters,&nbsp;that&nbsp;light&nbsp;caramelisation&nbsp;is&nbsp;an important part of the flavour buildling.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Cook&nbsp;down&nbsp;mushrooms &ndash;</strong> Add&nbsp;the&nbsp;sliced&nbsp;mushrooms&nbsp;first.&nbsp;There&rsquo;s&nbsp;a&nbsp;lot&nbsp;of&nbsp;volume&nbsp;here,&nbsp;and&nbsp;adding&nbsp;everything&nbsp;at&nbsp;once&nbsp;would&nbsp;overcrowd&nbsp;the&nbsp;pot.&nbsp;After&nbsp;a&nbsp;couple&nbsp;of&nbsp;minutes when it cooks down a bit,&nbsp;add&nbsp;the&nbsp;chopped&nbsp;cup&nbsp;mushrooms,&nbsp;chopped&nbsp;porcini,&nbsp;salt,&nbsp;thyme&nbsp;and&nbsp;bay&nbsp;leaves.&nbsp;Cook&nbsp;for&nbsp;around&nbsp;ten&nbsp;minutes,&nbsp;stirring&nbsp;occasionally,&nbsp;until&nbsp;the&nbsp;liquid&nbsp;the&nbsp;mushrooms&nbsp;release&nbsp;has&nbsp;mostly&nbsp;cooked&nbsp;away.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - steps" class="wp-image-202333" height="346" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Mushroom-Ragu-1-copy-3.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="5">
<li><p><strong>Deglaze&nbsp;with&nbsp;wine &ndash;</strong> Pour&nbsp;in&nbsp;the&nbsp;reserved&nbsp;red&nbsp;wine&nbsp;and&nbsp;bring&nbsp;it&nbsp;to&nbsp;a&nbsp;simmer,&nbsp;cooking&nbsp;for&nbsp;about&nbsp;six&nbsp;minutes.&nbsp;Stir&nbsp;well&nbsp;at&nbsp;the&nbsp;start,&nbsp;scraping&nbsp;up&nbsp;all&nbsp;that&nbsp;fond&nbsp;stuck&nbsp;to&nbsp;the&nbsp;bottom&nbsp;of&nbsp;the&nbsp;pot,&nbsp;which&nbsp;is&nbsp;pure&nbsp;flavour.&nbsp;As&nbsp;the&nbsp;wine&nbsp;reduces,&nbsp;you&nbsp;can&nbsp;stir&nbsp;less&nbsp;often.&nbsp;You&rsquo;ll&nbsp;know&nbsp;it&rsquo;s&nbsp;ready&nbsp;when&nbsp;you&nbsp;can&nbsp;drag&nbsp;your&nbsp;wooden&nbsp;spoon&nbsp;along&nbsp;the&nbsp;base&nbsp;of&nbsp;the&nbsp;pot&nbsp;and&nbsp;the&nbsp;path&nbsp;briefly&nbsp;holds&nbsp;before&nbsp;the&nbsp;sauce&nbsp;flows&nbsp;back&nbsp;together.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Thicken&nbsp;with&nbsp;flour&nbsp;and&nbsp;stock &ndash;</strong> Add&nbsp;the&nbsp;flour&nbsp;and&nbsp;stir&nbsp;it&nbsp;through,&nbsp;cooking&nbsp;for&nbsp;about&nbsp;thirty&nbsp;seconds&nbsp;to&nbsp;cook&nbsp;out&nbsp;the&nbsp;raw&nbsp;flour&nbsp;taste.&nbsp;Add&nbsp;the&nbsp;chicken&nbsp;stock,&nbsp;bring&nbsp;everything&nbsp;to&nbsp;a&nbsp;simmer,&nbsp;and&nbsp;cook&nbsp;for&nbsp;a&nbsp;couple&nbsp;of&nbsp;minutes&nbsp;until&nbsp;it&nbsp;thickens&nbsp;into&nbsp;a&nbsp;gravy-like&nbsp;consistency.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - steps" class="wp-image-202332" height="335" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Mushroom-Ragu-1-copy-2.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="7">
<li><p><strong>Finish&nbsp;the&nbsp;sauce &ndash;</strong>&nbsp;Stir&nbsp;in&nbsp;pepper,&nbsp;parmesan&nbsp;and&nbsp;remaining&nbsp;butter.&nbsp;Keep&nbsp;stirring&nbsp;until&nbsp;the&nbsp;butter&nbsp;has&nbsp;fully&nbsp;melted&nbsp;through&nbsp;and&nbsp;the&nbsp;sauce&nbsp;turns&nbsp;glossy&nbsp;and&nbsp;silky.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Cook your pasta</strong> in salted water for one minute less than the packet instructions suggest. It&rsquo;ll finish cooking through as it mixes with the hot sauce, so this keeps it from turning mushy. When they are ready, scoop out 1 cup of cooking water, then drain.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Mushroom ragu - steps" class="wp-image-202336" height="324" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Mushroom-Ragu-2.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="9">
<li><p><strong>Toss it together</strong> <strong>&ndash;</strong> Add the pasta straight into the pot with the ragu. Add 1/4 cup of pasta cooking water and toss well until every strand is coated in that glossy sauce. If the pasta starts getting sticky, just add more of the pasta cooking water, that&rsquo;s why we take out extra.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Finish and serve &ndash;</strong> Plate it up and grate a little extra fresh parmesan over the top before serving. Enjoy!</p></li>
</ol>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-serve-mushroom-ragu-pasta">How To Serve Mushroom Ragu Pasta</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Serve it hot straight from the pot, while everything is still glossy, piled into bowls with an extra grating of parmesan on top. It doesn&rsquo;t need much else because it&rsquo;s rich enough to carry the meal on its own.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you want to round it out, a simple<a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/rocket-salad-with-balsamic-dressing/" id="39529" type="post"> rocket salad</a> with a sharp vinaigrette would be a great contrast to the richness of the sauce, and a piece of <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/easy-yeast-bread-recipe-no-knead/" id="43962" type="post">homemade crusty bread </a>on the side is never a bad idea, especially for mopping up whatever sauce is left behind. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Great one for a cosy dinner any day of the week, and easy to scale up if you have unexpected guests. A glass of good red would be good with it. It&rsquo;s been on our lunch table all week and we absolutely love it! So I really hope you do too!</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Bon app&eacute;tit!<strong> &ndash; JB</strong></p>

<div class="wprm-recipe-notes-container wprm-block-text-normal" id="recipe-202307-notes"><h3 class="wprm-recipe-header wprm-recipe-notes-header wprm-block-text-bold wprm-align-left wprm-header-decoration-none">Notes</h3><div class="wprm-recipe-notes"><span style="display: block;"><strong>1. Porcini &ndash;</strong>Porcini mushrooms are prized wild mushrooms with a rich and intensely earthy flavour. They&rsquo;re commonly sold dried, which concentrates their flavour even further. Even a small amount adds remarkable depth to the whole sauce.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;">They come chopped but usually sliced. 20g is of sliced dried porcini is 3/4 cup, chopped will be around 1/2 cup.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>2. Red wine &ndash; </strong>I like a Pinot Noir here, it&rsquo;s light and fruity without too much tannin, which softens beautifully as it reduces into the sauce. No need for anything fancy though, a cheap bottle works just as well since the flavor concentrates and changes as it cooks down, so save the good stuff for drinking alongside dinner.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>3. Cutting the mushrooms &ndash; </strong>Slicing most of the cup mushrooms and roughly chopping the rest gives you a nice mix of textures throughout the ragu, some smaller, softer bits, some bigger, heartier bites.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>4. Anchovy &ndash; </strong>Don&rsquo;t worry, they won&rsquo;t make the sauce taste fishy. They dissolve into the base and simply round out the savoriness underneath everything else.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;">If you prefer not to use anchovy, just leave it out and add an extra 1/4 cup of parmesan.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>5. Chicken stock &ndash; </strong>The best choice here, it adds savouriness without masking the flavour of the mushrooms. <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/chicken-stock-recipe/">Homemade stock </a>will take it to another level, but skip powdered stock, it won&rsquo;t do the dish justice. Vegetable stock also works if you&rsquo;d prefer, the ragu won&rsquo;t have quite the same richness you can only get from meat-based stocks but it is still very delicious (I have made this with vegetable stock).</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>6. No parmesan option &ndash; </strong>If you don&rsquo;t eat parmesan because it contains animal rennet, substitute it with 1/4 cup of nutritional yeast.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>7. Australians reading this</strong> &ndash; Heads up, avoid the San Remo curly fettuccini, it falls apart.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>Leftovers and storage &ndash; </strong>Keeps well in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of stock or water to loosen if needed. I don&rsquo;t recommend freezing, the mushrooms will lose texture.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
