<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2025/09/29201942/youtube-feature.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Though one might think that the FTC has its hands full with </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/paramount-david-ellison-california-move"><span style="font-weight: 400;">other antitrust issues at the moment</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the agency is now turning its attention to Alphabet Inc, aka Google, and more specifically, YouTube. According to </span><a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/us-ftc-probing-youtube-over-social-media-policies"><span style="font-weight: 400;">a new report in </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bloomberg</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the Federal Trade Commission is investigating whether YouTube broke consumer protection laws when it suspended undisclosed accounts. According to unnamed sources, the FTC has been investigating the company since last year. The agency is gearing up for a potential lawsuit focused on whether YouTube violated its own rules by leading users to believe they were allowed to post certain types of content but ultimately had their accounts suspended or videos removed.</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Again, no specific accounts have been named, and the company hasn&#8217;t been formally accused of wrongdoing. Still, it&#8217;s not difficult to read between the lines, given this administration&#8217;s grievance-oriented use of the legal system. As </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bloomberg </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">points out in its reporting, Donald Trump&#8217;s account was banned for about two years from 2021 until 2023 over the insurrection. A number of accounts were suspended around the same time for spreading misinformation about COVID-19.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Still, there&#8217;s a chance this investigation doesn&#8217;t go any further and no lawsuit is actually filed. If one does, YouTube could choose to settle the case—which would require a sign-off from the two Republican commissioners of the FTC—or it could choose to defend itself in court. YouTube declined to comment on the report, and the FTC&#8217;s Chair Andrew Ferguson said the leak of this news would be referred to the authorities but that it would &#8220;never stop or slow a single law-enforcement investigation or litigation at the FTC.&#8221;&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
