<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/07/30174850/MixCollage-30-Jul-2026-04-47-PM-1209.jpg" /><p>Undeterred and perhaps encouraged by the wave of conversation (and astronomical book sales) surrounding<a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-odyssey-review"> Christopher Nolan’s <i>The Odyssey</i></a>, Emily Wilson has announced plans to translate Homer’s epic yet again. Bucking the trend of announcing another AI-generated version of the movie, <a href="https://apnews.com/article/emily-wilson-odyssey-translation-homer-a05084e4753528dff13766703161d24d">Wilson tells <i>The Associated Press</i></a> she’s embarking on a &#8220;complete retranslation&#8221; of the text, &#8220;not a revision of the old version.&#8221; Wilson’s previous translation, released in 2017, was a massive best-seller and received a shoutout from the Oscar-winning filmmaker as the inspiration for his adaptation. Nevertheless, <a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-odyssey-translation-emily-wilson">Wilson panned the film in July</a>, offering such generous criticisms as &#8220;I’d be ashamed to have written any part of this script&#8221; and accusing the actors of color of playing &#8220;versions of the Black best friend, whose only role in the drama is to provide aid to the white protagonist.&#8221; She has since rescinded that last criticism and <a href="https://www.avclub.com/emily-wilson-atlantic-second-odyssey-essay">released a second takedown of the film</a>, deragotorially comparing <i>The Odyssey</i> to <i>Die Hard</i>.&nbsp;</p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p>For those not keeping up with the Classics, while translators correcting and altering their work is common practice, a complete, ground-up retranslation is practically unheard of. So much so that Wilson says her decision was &#8220;very controversial&#8221; with her publisher. However, considering her first translation sold more than a million copies, we’re unsurprised that her editor believes &#8220;her new approach is bound to be thrilling.&#8221;</p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p>Wilson’s film critiques weren’t without its critics. Smelling chum in the discourse, <a href="https://www.avclub.com/joyce-carol-oates-enters-odyssey-discourse">apex poster Joyce Carol Oates described</a> the criticisms of &#8220;this person, who has benefited enormously from Nolan’s film&#8221; as beyond the pale: “One would expect a translator, of all people, beholden to a text, in service to a text, to be just a wee bit more thoughtful &amp; respectful of others who are acting in good faith just as (she would claim) she is.&#8221;</p>
<p>Wilson doesn’t fear controversy, particularly not from adults obsessed with children’s literature. Elsewhere in her interview with <i>AP</i>, she dared to call <i>Percy Jackson</i> &#8220;not designed for an adult literary reader&#8221; and describes the joys of becoming a publicly discussed academic. She adds, &#8220;The last few weeks have been a clear illustration of how I’m not in control of the reception of my work [&#8230;] I actually think in some ways there’s a useful reminder about the powerlessness that comes with the awareness that &#8216;I’m going to be misunderstood. I’m going to be misread. People are going to hate me.&#8217; I’ve got to accept that.&#8221; Judging by this video of her forceful recitation of the opening of <i>The Odyssey</i> in ancient Greek, we can assume Emily Wilson can take the heat.</p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
