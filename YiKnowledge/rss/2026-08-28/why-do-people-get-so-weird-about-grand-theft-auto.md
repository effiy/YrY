<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/27203413/gta-6-art-feature.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over the past week, the biggest story in all of gaming was a series of videos that showed… not much, actually. The issue (which has now launched god knows how many YouTube reaction videos, </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/rockstar-games-addresses-leaks-online"><span style="font-weight: 400;">an odd apology post from the game&#8217;s developers</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and a whole host of </span><a href="https://variety.com/2026/gaming/news/gta-6-leaks-rockstar-subpoenas-microsoft-discord-1236840176/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">quickly deployed lawsuits</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) wasn&#8217;t the content of the videos—pretty innocuous reveals of such radical video game mechanics as &#8220;driving a car&#8221; or &#8220;dancing at a club&#8221; or &#8220;being near a beach&#8221;—but what game they&#8217;d been stolen from: This November&#8217;s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto VI</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Dropped online by an instantly notorious hacker, the videos landed a week before </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA VI </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">was set to get <a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAEShwEB6zswFZDOpy1LG372ULkINl7-0KyrK5OLGdMIQwtviAN3gDZCbr0wjxI10TUhZArHtmRfy0NgBP9WxTcaOwyj6ouZt6ZJ4PGs6tifxVUNvL-wyyaMPpml1qTZ9QJHEfnemyhGmZb_AeOMjOo7PiPGP04Wav1Gx3DjTOHtI_oBCPxnSwdldeQ">its big Netflix reveal announcement on August 27</a>, which would wind up showcasing a slightly shinier take on the same basic crime movie tropes the series has been mining for nearly 30 years. The weird thing about the leaks, then, wasn&#8217;t what they showed about </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, but about the brains receiving it. Because, hot damn, but is gaming as a whole weird about </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">these days.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For context: It&#8217;s now been 13 years since Rockstar Games released </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto V</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which became, in the first 24 hours of its release, the fastest-selling entertainment product in human history. (It&#8217;s currently the third-best-selling video game of all time, beaten only by </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Tetris </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Minecraft</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.) Something, clearly, had tipped over in consumers&#8217; brains: </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA V </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">sold nearly 10 times what </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto IV </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">had managed back in 2008, leapfrogging off that success to become a genuine cultural juggernaut. Arriving just as the PlayStation 3/Xbox 360 era was coming to a close, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA V</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> represented a certain high point for Rockstar&#8217;s take on the open-world gameplay it had pioneered and codified with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto III</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">—and then just sort of went on serving as said high point, as players obsessively dialed into the game&#8217;s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Online </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">offshoot, while Rockstar started work on, first, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Red Dead Redemption 2</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and then, inevitably, on </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA VI</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Sure, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA V </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">got official rereleases for the PlayStation 4/Xbox One </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">the PlayStation 5/Xbox X/S, but the fact that one of the planet&#8217;s biggest gaming franchises skipped nearly two entire console generations without apparently losing its grip on players&#8217; imagination remains one of the great oddities in modern gaming.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- admarker --> <!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Is it any wonder, then, that </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA VI </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">has taken on totemic status—the One True Game coming back to dispense salvation on a blighted hobby? It&#8217;s part of what makes these leaks such toxic brain candy for an engagement engine now spinning freely in response to their exposure. Long-delayed, and with the hope of an entire industry riding on its back, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA VI </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">has come to be all games to all people, to the point that internecine wars have been waged about </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/gta-6-leaks-fuel-realism-debate"><span style="font-weight: 400;">whether it should have working gas pumps</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, while </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">actual </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">wars might need to be </span><a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/grand-theft-auto-army-reenlistment-bonus/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">put on a brief pause</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> so recruiters can use time with the game as a bribe for re-upping soldiers. As someone who loved </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA III</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, thinks </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA V </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">is fine, and whose primary connection to the franchise these days comes from </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/staff-picks-gta-iv-the-fast-traffic-playthrough-100-foot-wave-2"><span style="font-weight: 400;">watching the cars in </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA IV </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">go too dang fast</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the whole thing can feel inexplicable. &#8220;Really?&#8221; I think from time to time. &#8220;You&#8217;re getting this wrapped around the axle on the video game with </span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JCiMVdYilC8"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trevor</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in it?&#8221;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">But the answer, I suspect, comes down to a potent blend of demographics and quality. The quality aspect is objective, if laden with unpleasant undertones: Rockstar&#8217;s games arrive with just as many plaudits for their scope and technical polish as they do </span><a href="https://kotaku.com/inside-rockstar-games-culture-of-crunch-1829936466"><span style="font-weight: 400;">stories about crunch culture</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, or </span><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/games/2025/nov/07/uk-union-accuses-gta-maker-rockstar-games-of-firing-employees-attempting-to-organise"><span style="font-weight: 400;">allegations of union busting</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> levied at the company. It&#8217;s genuinely easy to pick up </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto V </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">as your very first &#8220;serious&#8221; video game and understand its mechanics, its structure, and its simplistic crime story themes. Many games demand something from players, whether it&#8217;s mechanical or mental rigor, but that&#8217;s never been Rockstar&#8217;s worry. To enjoy <em>Grand Theft Auto</em>, you don&#8217;t need to futz around with complicated menus or obscure gameplay concepts or basically anything you haven&#8217;t seen in a dozen other video games before, only shinier. You start the game, you start running people over; it&#8217;s really as simple as that.</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The demographics issue, meanwhile, pokes at a whole host of festering boils that run all under the skin of modern gaming. The world of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">has always been a blend of college kids&#8217; favorite crime movies and a certain relentless cynicism that is hard not to code, to my own mind if nowhere else, as both aggressively young and aggressively masculine. (It&#8217;s not for nothing that the reveal that </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA VI</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8216;s Bonnie And Clyde-esque story would mean you spent part of your time playing as the Bonnie in question, Lucia, produced a frothing reaction in the online hordes.) The &#8220;jokes&#8221; that </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">fills itself with—including the fake commercials highlighted in that recent Netflix promo— are the jokes people might toss around with their college (or, if I&#8217;m being less charitable, high school) buddies: Corrupt sex fiend politicians, allegedly &#8220;shocking&#8221; transgressive humor, commercial parodies that wouldn&#8217;t make it through table read at </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">SNL</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and the kind of rarefied wit capable of creating a fast food chicken restaurant whose name kind of sounds like &#8220;Fucking Hell.&#8221;&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More than its frequently facile stabs at comedy and political satire, though, the world of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">serves the demographics that flock to it because that world, itself, is comfortably transgressive: It&#8217;s a reality where you can drive around, slam through fences, get into a shoot-out with the cops, and then successfully escape. (And if you don&#8217;t, no worries; it just means a quick respawn and a few bucks out of your typically overflowing wallet.) Even as these aspects have clashed at times with Rockster&#8217;s own growing interest in both gaming realism—hence the gas pump battles—and &#8220;heightened&#8221; narratives, their existence as pure escapism really can&#8217;t be discounted. While hundreds of imitators have cropped up in their wake, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GTA </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">games remain big enough to get lost in, and that allure isn&#8217;t getting any weaker as the years pass.</span></p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And so we collectively sit here, still nearly three months out from the release of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto VI</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and try to reckon with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">why </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">it&#8217;s mad cow&#8217;d its way into so many people&#8217;s brains. Even as an agnostic, I can feel its weight on the landscape: The kind of thing that forces other developers to shift release dates and flee its shadow as all the figurative oxygen gets sucked out of the room. Its bigness, if not its importance, is inescapable: It&#8217;s a game that feels like it&#8217;s poised to serve as a capstone to a whole generation of video games where the solution to growing lists of technical and existential problems has been to pour in more money, more time, more hours of human work to make something bigger and shinier and &#8220;better&#8221; than anything that&#8217;s come before. That cycle is now producing bouts of online madness from something as basic as leaked videos of gameplay that genuinely don&#8217;t look meaningfully different from what players were doing in </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto V </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">13 years ago; it&#8217;s a cycle that&#8217;s imbued a silly video game about taking a guy&#8217;s car and then running him over with it with the fate of an entire multi-billion-dollar industry. And, hey, maybe </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">that&#8217;s</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> a clue as to why we&#8217;re all so weird about </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grand Theft Auto </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">these days: Bubbles are pretty, after all. Big, shiny, and fascinating. And, of course, they make such interesting things happen when the inevitable happens, and they finally, blessedly pop.</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
