<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-mick"><em>The Mick</em></h3>



<p>For a time, the cast of <em><a href="https://www.joblo.com/tag/its-always-sunny-in-philadelphia/">It&#8217;s Always Sunny in Philadelphia</a></em> would attempt to venture off into their own paths while still staying committed to their show. Charlie Day showed more attention to his film career. Glenn Howerton started a new series called <em>A.P. Bio</em>. Rob Mac started a new series called <em>Mythic Quest</em>. <a href="https://www.joblo.com/tag/danny-devito/">Danny DeVito</a> already had his own film career established. Then, in 2017, Kaitlin Olson would start her own new series called <em>The Mick</em>, which aired on Fox. </p>



<p>Olson also ventured into her new show with two <em>It&#8217;s Always Sunny</em> writers &#8212; Dave Chernin and John Chernin. The Chernins had also created the show. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-s-the-mick-about"><em>What&#8217;s The Mick about?</em></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="the mick" class="wp-image-944668" height="630" src="https://www.joblo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Mick-2.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<div class="wp-block-spacer" style="height: 4px;"></div>



<p>At first glance, <em>The Mick</em> might seem like a show that carries over the same irreverent humor of <em>It&#8217;s Always Sunny</em> into a new plot where Kaitlin Olson plays a similar character to Dee Reynolds. The predominant similarity of both shows is that they put a more extreme twist on some famous TV tropes. <em>It&#8217;s Always Sunny</em> had been self-described as &#8220;<em>Seinfeld</em> on crack.&#8221; <em>The Mick</em> is what happens when you take the gimmicky, &#8220;forced family&#8221; classic sitcoms of the 70s, 80s and 90s and put <em>them</em> on crack. </p>



<p>Olson plays Mickey Molng, an unapologetically trashy woman who loves living hard. Mickey finds herself taking care of her rich sister&#8217;s spoiled children when their parents flee the FBI after an attempted arrest on some white-collar crimes. Mickey and her on-again/off-again boyfriend, Jimmy, take it upon themselves to stay at her sister&#8217;s mansion and become surrogate parents for the kids, who completely hate this situation.</p>



<p>The plot is a device to get the lower class and fish-out-of-water Mickey and Jimmy to butt heads with the entitled kids and the weird rich elite. Mickey constantly plays a game of power with the 17-year-old socialite Sabrina, who is played to &#8220;bitch-perfection&#8221; by Sofia Black-D&#8217;Elia. The middle child, Chip, played a little <em>too good</em> by Thomas Barbusca, is very reminiscent of Carlton Banks from <em>The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air</em>, where he very much basks in his superiority and upper-class status, but constantly finds himself being so detached from reality that he is ultimately the butt of the joke. And then there&#8217;s the youngest, Ben, who shows every sign of a troubled child, but does so in such an innocent way that everyone else is desperately trying to guide him in the right direction. Then, there&#8217;s Mickey&#8217;s sidekick, Alba, who is the former maid, but she hilariously decides to stop doing her job amid the parents&#8217; absence and just spends time with Mickey.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-makes-it-good"><em>What makes it good?</em></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="the mick" class="wp-image-944670" height="630" src="https://www.joblo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Mick-3.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<div class="wp-block-spacer" style="height: 4px;"></div>



<p>As previously stated, the Chernins and Olson bring a lot of similar humor from <em>It&#8217;s Always Sunny</em> to the show, but it somehow seems darker. The difference may be because <em>The Mick</em> still has a hint of classic sitcom sentimentality, but it constantly pulls the rug from under you and things always take a hilariously awful turn. When this show was appointment-viewing for me, I always loved how extreme the humor would get. On a recent rewatch, I realized that the show consistently utilized graphic violence like a throwback to <em>Looney Tunes</em> cartoons and <em>The Simpsons</em>. They were visceral punchlines, but having them done in live-action and played completely straight gave the jokes an extra oomph, which made <em>The Mick</em> feel bolder than most comedies.</p>



<p>Jimmy is played by Scott MacArthur, who also serves as a staff writer and story editor, and there is rarely an episode where he doesn&#8217;t get horribly physically abused in some way. This series made me an instant fan of him.</p>



<p>Unfortunately, Fox opted not to continue with the show after the second season and Olson returned to the still-running <em>It&#8217;s Always Sunny</em>, as well as a new show, <em>High Potential</em>. This was probably one of the most heartbreaking TV show cancellations for me (next to the discontinued <em>Kim&#8217;s Convenience</em>), but I am thankful we still got 36 glorious episodes out of it. And even more thankful that they are still available to view on <strong>Hulu</strong>. So, if you need something to binge this weekend, I recommend you check it out!</p>
