<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Kevin Dupzyk&#8217;s profile of Sam Rockwell, a &#8220;legendary scene-stealer&#8221; now in his fourth decade of work, is a crisp and entertaining one-act, with journalist and actor holed up in a spare Manhattan greenroom. Dupzyk studies Rockwell while Rockwell studies the room, testing the durability of his own idiosyncratic observations—about Red Bull and loss and rain—to see what they might yield in a conversation with a stranger. Dupzyk had wanted to &#8220;gin up some action for this story,&#8221; but it&#8217;s strangely compelling to watch Rockwell&#8217;s gaze prowl the room, to see what lodges in the filter of his creative process.</p>
<span class="__iawmlf-post-loop-links" hidden="hidden"></span>


<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Rockwell is a good tennis player. And like any good athlete, he trains. For as much as he appears to get by on natural charm and charisma, he is an actor’s actor, a true believer, in his words, an “acting nerd.” Research. Running lines. Exerci—</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Do you know what a gibbon is?” Rockwell asks.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Like, a monkey?” I say, confused.</p>
</blockquote>
