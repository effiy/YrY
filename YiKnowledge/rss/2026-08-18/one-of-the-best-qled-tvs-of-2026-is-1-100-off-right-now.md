<p>One of the first things you'll notice about the QM8L is the &ldquo;Zero Border&rdquo; bezel design, which gives it a modern, minimalistic look. When you turn it on, you'll likely note how bright this TV gets, which is a huge advantage <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/oled-vs-qled-differences" target="_blank">QLEDs have over OLEDs</a>. TCL claims it reaches 6,000 nits, and seeing as the <a href="https://www.flatpanelshd.com/news.php?subaction=showfull&amp;id=1784972009" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">brightest OLED TV on the market only reaches 700 nits</a>, no OLED can come close to this unit's brightness. Another important spec to note here is the local dimming zones: TCL claims this TV has up to 4,000, which means it gets top-tier backlight control, limited light blooming (thanks in part to TCL's "Halo Control"), and incredible contrast levels that some might not be able to distinguish from an OLED.</p><p>While many of us won't manually calibrate our TVs, the picture modes you can switch to in the settings will be most people's go-to. This TV's "Filmmaker mode" provides accurate color and picture, and if you're a gamer, you can use the native 144Hz high-refresh rate. If you don't already have a surround sound system at home and want to keep a minimalist setup, the QM8L comes with Dolby Atmos-ready Bang &amp; Olufsen speakers, which offer above-average audio output compared to most TVs. You can read more about these features in <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/home-entertainment/tcl-qm8l-tv-review/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">CNET's 8.8/10 review</a>. If 65 inches isn't quite right for your home theater setup, take a look at the QM8L's other sizes, which are also discounted right now.  </p><div class=" relative flex justify-center py-16 md:left-1/2 md:w-[780px] md:max-w-max md:-translate-x-1/2">
