<figure><img class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual" src="https://cdn1.matadornetwork.com/blogs/1/2026/08/BR705137.jpg" /></figure><p>The journey from the airport in Liberia, Costa Rica, to Nosara on the Nicoya Peninsula, a Blue Zone where people tend to live longer and healthier lives, takes you along the dirt washboard that turns a casual ride into a driving challenge. Nosara has no paved roads, big chain resorts, or easy access to the airport. Instead, it&#8217;s a place where you can hear howler monkeys as you venture away from the main drag. </p>
<p>The surf break at Playa Guiones is consistent, making it one of the top places in Costa Rica for riding the wave. At the heart of Guiones, a small town built around wellness and connecting with nature, sits <a href="https://www.thegildediguana.com/" target="_blank">The Gilded Iguana Surf Hotel</a>, which has been welcoming travelers since the 1980s. A few steps from the hotel is The Gilded Iguana Surf Club, where a team of certified instructors hosts classes for all levels. </p>
<p>I arrived at The Gilded Iguana after a 2.5-hour drive from Daniel Oduber Quirós International Airport. After a brief check-in at the hotel, I was greeted with a welcome drink and then went for a quick swim in the pool. The next day, I chose my surfboard, and we headed to Playa Guiones along the narrow trail that connects the surf school with the wide sandy beach. </p>
<p>After brushing up on surfing rules with my instructor, we headed straight for the water, where I joined a lineup of other surfers. The low afternoon tide created perfect surfing conditions, while Tajalin, an on-site bar, created the perfect post-surfing conditions with smoothies, beers, cocktails, or coffee. </p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">What it’s like to stay at The Gilded Iguana</h2>
<div class="wp-caption alignnone" id="attachment_896051" style="width: 1610px;"><img alt="the gilded iguana" class="size-full wp-image-896051" height="1066" src="https://cdn1.matadornetwork.com/blogs/1/2026/08/GildedIguanaSurfHotel1.jpg" width="1600" /><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-896051">Photos: The Gilded Iguana Surf Hotel</p></div>
<p>The Gilded Iguana’s surf club is the hotel’s signature feature, but the pool and hot tub are the property’s centerpiece amenity, with a small bar and hammocks by the side.</p>
<p>The on-site restaurant serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner made from locally sourced, organic ingredients. The menu leans into staples that define the region&#8217;s Blue Zone diet, notably the trifecta of squash, beans, and corn. But the options stretch well beyond that, too; smashburgers share the same menu page as both Caribbean and vegan ceviche, made with sea bass and apple-cucumber, respectively. </p>
<p>At lunch, I grabbed a spot outside on the patio and ordered creamy chickpea hummus topped with mushrooms, corn, avocado, and fresh mint, before my main course: a vegetarian grilled squash salad with house-made pesto and a mix of greens and herbs. All the food is prepared by the hotel&#8217;s chef from scratch. <em>Gallo pinto</em> (Costa Rica&#8217;s classic rice-and-beans dish), an assortment of meats and omelets, fruits, banana bread, and pastries are part of the daily buffet breakfast.</p>
<p>The Gilded Iguana also puts on a range of events and special programs aimed at connecting guests with one another and creating special memories for everyone. Its schedule often features music and themed events and performances.<br />
The hotel&#8217;s spa has massages and facials, built around products made by Costa Rican artisans, so even the lotion on your skin has roots in the region. After a morning of surfing or hiking around Nosara, book a deep tissue massage to work out the saltwater soreness.</p>
<p>Accommodations at The Gilded Iguana range from bigger suites that fit up to six people to surfer rooms for two, premium suites with one king bed ideal for couples, as well as treetop suites on the upper floor that feature a mezzanine layout. Details like comfy patio chairs, an outdoor shower surrounded by lush vegetation, hammocks, and friendly staff are part of why The Gilded Iguana feels more like home than a hotel at the base of one of Costa Rica&#8217;s best surfing spots. Take a peek outside your room, and, if you get lucky, spot iguanas crawling up the trees or an occasional opossum, especially late at night when wildlife is more active.</p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">Exploring Nosara</h2>
<div class="wp-caption alignnone" id="attachment_896050" style="width: 1610px;"><img alt="the gilded iguana" class="size-full wp-image-896050" height="1067" src="https://cdn1.matadornetwork.com/blogs/1/2026/08/GildedIguanaSurfActivities1.jpg" width="1600" /><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-896050">Photos: The Gilded Iguana Surf Hotel</p></div>
<p>Most of the area&#8217;s hotels, restaurants, and surf schools are clustered around Playa Guiones, but Nosara consists of three beaches, plus an inland part where many locals live. Playa Guiones is the surf and yoga hub; Playa Pelada sits north of Guiones, smaller and rockier with tide pools and a quiet atmosphere; and Playa Garza, a fishing village that skirted a tourist boom, is farther south.</p>
<p>I headed outside The Gilded Iguana and took a quick walk through town. Right next to the hotel is <a href="https://www.instagram.com/rosissodatica/" target="_blank">Rosi&#8217;s Soda Tica</a>, a dining establishment for traditional Costa Rican food where you can try the country&#8217;s signature dishes like <em>casado</em>, a combination plate built around rice and black beans, cooked separately rather than mixed. It comes with a side of protein, plus shredded cabbage or salad and fried sweet plantains.</p>
<p>Besides surfing, yoga is a favorite pastime in Nosara that brings scores of travelers year-round. Yoga studios and retreat centers are scattered around town, many open-air with views back toward Guiones. Every Tuesday, the Nosara Farmers Market takes over the road leading from the main Playa Guiones entrance from around 8 AM to early afternoon.</p>
<p>There, you can find arts and crafts, clothing, small treats, and fresh, locally grown produce. The market is great for mingling with locals, expats, and visitors all in one place. It can be reached by a quick trail that connects different parts of Nosara, including Guiones and Pelada. The trail is part of the broader network of walking paths maintained by the <a href="https://nosaracivicassociation.com/" target="_blank">Nosara Civic Association</a>, marked with plaques on native tree species and known for howler monkey sightings.</p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">Adventures beyond Playa Guiones</h2>
<div class="wp-caption alignnone" id="attachment_896054" style="width: 1610px;"><img alt="the gilded iguana" class="size-full wp-image-896054" height="1067" src="https://cdn1.matadornetwork.com/blogs/1/2026/08/shutterstock_1006831711.jpg" width="1600" /><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-896054">Photos: <a href="https://www.shutterstock.com/image-photo/olive-turtle-pacific-coast-guanacaste-on-1006831711">Xenia Photography</a>/Shutterstock</p></div>
<p>Río Nosara, just north of Pelada, is a prime spot for kayaking in town. Most tours launch near the river mouth and drift along the calm water rather than fight any current, which means it works for total beginners as much as experienced paddlers.</p>
<p>Kayaking tours are timed around the incoming tide, when the water pushes gently upstream, and the wildlife is most active; mornings bring out great blue herons, roseate spoonbills, and the occasional crocodile sunning itself on the bank. Trips usually run two to three hours and end back at the river mouth, where some outfitters send paddlers off with fresh coconut or a cold beer while the tide pulls back out to sea.</p>
<p>About 25 minutes north of Nosara, Ostional National Wildlife Refuge is a protected area and one of the largest nesting sites for olive ridley sea turtles in the world. Big <em>arribadas</em> (&#8220;arrivals&#8221; in Spanish), when thousands of turtles land on the beach to lay eggs, run roughly July through December, although smaller numbers of turtles nest on the beach all year. Access to the beach during an arribada requires a guided tour.</p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">The bigger picture</h2>
<div class="wp-caption alignnone" id="attachment_896053" style="width: 1610px;"><img alt="the gilded iguana" class="size-full wp-image-896053" height="1067" src="https://cdn1.matadornetwork.com/blogs/1/2026/08/BR902687-Edit.jpg" width="1600" /><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-896053">Photo: The Gilded Iguana Surf Hotel</p></div>
<p>The Nicoya Peninsula at large is a sliver of land cut off from the rest of the country by the Gulf of Nicoya and the Tempisque River, where dry tropical forest meets a coastline of coves and long empty beaches. Paved roads turn into rugged dirt paths, and cell service becomes spotty once you step outside your hotel. It’s one of only five original Blue Zones in the world, whose higher life expectancy is credited to a combination of lifestyle factors such as physical activity, diet, and a slower pace of life.</p>
<p>Unlike the black-sand volcanic beaches on Costa Rica’s mainland, the Nicoya Peninsula has a white-sand coastline due to a different wave pattern that breaks down marine materials like seashells and coral. Historically more remote, the Nicoya Peninsula has kept mega resorts and highways at bay, and although the roads here feel rough, there&#8217;s a strong sense of adventure as exploring the area requires effort, good navigation skills, and in some cases, four-wheel drive.</p>
<p>At the end of the day, all that effort gives way to something simpler: the same unhurried pace credited with adding years to lives here.</p>
<p>For one sunset during my stay, I headed to the half-moon-shaped Playa Pelada, separated from Guiones by a craggy outcrop. A quick walk along Guiones got me there, past tide pools and La Luna Restaurant, a tourist-favorite beachside dining spot, before I found a quieter stretch of sand farther down. I put my backpack on the coarse Pacific sand and sat down for the last few minutes of light, as the glowing red sun sank below the horizon from one of Nosara&#8217;s most photogenic beaches. <img class="post-ender" height="15" src="https://d36tnp772eyphs.cloudfront.net/assets/images/matador-logo-small-2017-bw@3.png" style="width: 15px; height: 15px;" width="15" /></p>
