<p>Kobo&rsquo;s built-in OverDrive integration is another reason the Clara Colour works well for frequent readers, since you can connect a library card and borrow supported ebooks directly on the device instead of paying for every title you want to read. Once those books are loaded, it is also easy to carry around for long stretches&mdash;at 6.1 ounces, it stays comfortable in one hand, while the IPX8 waterproof rating means you can read by the pool or in the bath without worrying too much about splashes. Its recessed screen also handles outdoor glare well, and its battery life can stretch for weeks depending on your brightness and wireless settings.</p><p>On the downside, loading your own books can be more inconvenient&mdash;unlike a Kindle, where you can email files or use Send to Kindle, the Clara Colour generally requires you to connect it to a computer, notes this <a href="https://www.pcmag.com/reviews/kobo-clara-colour" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>PCMag review</u></a>. You also miss out on physical page-turn buttons, which Kobo reserves for the more expensive Libra Colour. One thing Kobo does better than most <a href="https://www.pcmag.com/picks/the-best-ereaders" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>ereader</u></a> makers, though, is repairability. Its partnership with iFixit means you can buy <a href="https://www.ifixit.com/Parts/Kobo_Clara_Colour" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>official replacement parts</u></a> for components such as the battery or display instead of automatically replacing the entire device if something breaks. All said, the Clara Colour is easier to recommend, especially now that it gives you color, built-in library access, and waterproofing for $40 less than the Kindle Paperwhite.</p><hr /><div class=" relative flex justify-center py-16 md:left-1/2 md:w-[780px] md:max-w-max md:-translate-x-1/2">
