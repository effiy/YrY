<div class="rssBody"><div><p>I spent the first half of 2025 traveling through Europe and South America. Then, the month after I got back, I sold my house in Austin and moved to Spain, where I&#x27;ve been based for the past year. In that time, I&#x27;ve only made it back to the U.S. twice.</p><p>That has created a problem: between my <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">American Express Platinum Card®</a> and <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/221211836?name=Chase+Sapphire+Reserve%C2%AE&amp;issuer=Chase&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=221211836" target="_blank">Chase Sapphire Reserve®</a> (see <a href="/tc/221211836?pid=221211836&amp;name=Chase%20Sapphire%20Reserve%C2%AE&amp;data-testid=ratesFees" target="_blank">rates and fees</a>), I have up to $700 a year in restaurant credits that only trigger when I pay at a participating restaurant in the U.S.* I wasn&#x27;t willing to let that value go to waste just because I wasn&#x27;t going to be dining in the U.S. as often, so I researched a way to keep maximizing those credits from a distance.</p><p>That&#x27;s when I found <a href="https://useyourcredits.com/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">UseYourCredits.com</a>, a site that maps out participating restaurants for benefits on cards like these. It&#x27;s also how I learned that a good chunk of those Resy and OpenTable restaurants sell their own gift cards online. Buy one directly from the restaurant&#x27;s site, and it often triggers the statement credit just like paying your check would, even if you&#x27;re thousands of miles away and don&#x27;t have a reservation.</p><p>I&#x27;ve been doing this for about a year now, and it&#x27;s let me stay on top of these credits every single cycle without needing to be anywhere near the restaurant. Here&#x27;s how I make it work.</p><p><em>*Receive up to $400 per calendar year in Resy statement credits (distributed as up to $100 per quarter); simply pay with your Amex Platinum at an eligible U.S. Resy restaurant; no reservation required; enrollment required.</em></p><h2 class="rssH2">How I find restaurants that work</h2><p>I rely on UseYourCredits.com, which <a href="https://useyourcredits.com/maps/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">maps out participating restaurants for both programs</a>. The two credit pools look nothing alike.</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="useyourcredits.com screenshot" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2026/08/1.jpeg" /><figcaption>USEYOURCREDITS.COM</figcaption></figure><p><a href="https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/resy-credit-guide/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">Resy</a>&#x27;s list includes almost 11,000 restaurants nationwide, but only about a third of them sell e-gift cards directly. (This cohort will expand when <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/news/amex-resy-credit-tock-restaurants/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">select Tock restaurants join</a> starting Sept. 15.)</p><p>Resy statement credits for use in the U.S. are available on select cards, including the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">American Express Platinum</a> (up to $100 in statement credits quarterly; enrollment required) and the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/6344?name=American+Express%C2%AE+Gold+Card&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=6344" target="_blank">American Express® Gold Card</a> (up to $50 in statement credits semiannually; enrollment required).</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="useyourcredits.com screenshot" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2026/08/1-1.jpeg" /><figcaption>Participating Resy restaurants in Austin. USEYOURCREDITS.COM</figcaption></figure><p>Chase&#x27;s <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/chase-sapphire-reserve-exclusive-tables-guide/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables</a> list, run through OpenTable, is much smaller (around 400 restaurants), with about a third selling e-gift cards.</p><p>One detail that matters: buy the gift card through the restaurant&#x27;s own website or ordering page, not a generic third-party gift card marketplace. It&#x27;s usually fine if that page runs on a platform like Toast behind the scenes — what matters is that it&#x27;s the restaurant&#x27;s own official channel, not a marketplace that sells cards for dozens of different restaurants.</p><p>If a restaurant doesn&#x27;t sell gift cards online, calling the restaurant directly or buying one in person is also likely to trigger the credit, since the charge usually runs through the restaurant itself.</p><h2 class="rssH2">Why I always test with a small purchase first</h2><p>I never try to use a full credit at an untested restaurant. I buy the smallest gift card the restaurant offers (usually $5) and wait to see whether the credit posts before I put another dollar toward it.</p><p>That&#x27;s exactly what I did at Austin restaurant Suerte last December: a $5 test on the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/221211836?name=Chase+Sapphire+Reserve%C2%AE&amp;issuer=Chase&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=221211836" target="_blank">Sapphire Reserve</a>, followed by a $145 reload after the first credit posted.</p><p>At nearby Launderette, I tested $25 on my <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">Amex Platinum</a> before reloading another $75. Every time, including for the third gift card I&#x27;m currently building up at Fresas (also an Austin staple), the statement credit has posted within two days of purchase.</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2025/09/20250908_Hotel-Indigo-Auckland_ERosen-Bistro-Saine-dinner-5.jpg" /><figcaption>ERIC ROSEN/THE POINTS GUY</figcaption></figure><p>Testing small isn&#x27;t just caution for caution&#x27;s sake. Restaurants can change their gift card providers, and issuers can adjust how these transactions get coded. A $5 test protects you from finding out the hard way that $150 didn&#x27;t trigger anything.</p><h2 class="rssH2">How I stack multiple restaurant credits into one meal</h2><p>This is where the strategy really pays off.</p><p>Chase splits its <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/221211836?name=Chase+Sapphire+Reserve%C2%AE&amp;issuer=Chase&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=221211836" target="_blank">Sapphire Reserve</a> dining credit into two $150 chunks: one for January through June and another for July through December (unused amounts don&#x27;t roll over). Last December, I loaded that $150 test-and-reload combo onto a Suerte gift card to bank July-to-December 2025&#x27;s credit before it disappeared.</p><p>Then in February, I flew back to Austin and had dinner in person at Suerte. The bill came to $304 (tip included). I paid $150 of it with the gift card I&#x27;d already loaded, and charged the remaining $154 to my physical Sapphire Reserve. That $154 charge triggered a fresh $150 credit for the new January-to-June cycle.</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="Chase screenshot" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2026/08/1-2.jpeg" /><figcaption>CHASE</figcaption></figure><p>Between the banked credit and the newly triggered one, $300 of the $304 total was covered by the card, meaning I paid only $4 out of pocket.</p><p>I used a similar approach with my <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">Amex Platinum</a> at Launderette: a $100 gift card that used my Q4 2025 credit (up to $100 in statement credits quarterly for U.S. Resy purchases; enrollment required), then a $100 charge on my physical card during that same February trip, which triggered my Q1 2026 statement credit. Two credit cycles, one meal, $200 covered.</p><p>I&#x27;m doing it again at Fresas, where I&#x27;ve already used my Q2 statement credit to buy an e-gift card and reload with another $100 for Q3 before my next Austin trip.</p><p><strong>Related: <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/favorite-credit-card-dining-credits/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">The dining statement credits that save me over $1,000 every year</a></strong></p><h2 class="rssH2">Who should consider this strategy?</h2><p>You don&#x27;t need to be living abroad full-time to get value out of this. It&#x27;s worth considering if:</p><ul><li>You&#x27;re spending an extended stretch outside the U.S., whether that&#x27;s a year abroad, a six-month posting or a quarter-long trip, and you don&#x27;t want to watch your credits expire while you&#x27;re gone.</li><li>You want to bank credits toward one bigger night out, a group dinner or a meal you&#x27;re treating someone to, rather than trying to spend $50-$150 at a time.</li><li>You&#x27;re holding a card with a smaller, more frequent statement credit, like on the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/6573?name=Delta+SkyMiles%C2%AE+Reserve+American+Express+Card&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=6573" target="_blank">Delta SkyMiles® Reserve American Express Card</a> (up to $20 monthly credit for U.S. Resy purchases; enrollment required), and would rather consolidate several months into one gift card than remember to spend at a participating restaurant every single month.</li><li>You don&#x27;t dine out often and are looking for a way to make a high annual fee feel less daunting. The <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">Amex Platinum</a>&#x27;s $895 annual fee (see <a href="/tc/22504813?pid=22504813&amp;name=American%20Express%20Platinum%20Card%C2%AE&amp;data-testid=ratesFees" target="_blank">rates and fees</a>) almost halves once you fully capture its up to $400 annual restaurant credit as U.S. Resy locations; the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/221211836?name=Chase+Sapphire+Reserve%C2%AE&amp;issuer=Chase&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=221211836" target="_blank">Sapphire Reserve</a>&#x27;s annual fee drops to an effective $495 once you factor in its $300 annual Exclusive Tables credit (up to $150 in credits biannually).</li></ul><h2 class="rssH2">Where this strategy falls short</h2><p>Not every card is worth the effort. I canceled my <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/6344?name=American+Express%C2%AE+Gold+Card&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=6344" target="_blank">American Express Gold</a> last year because I couldn&#x27;t make the math work while living overseas:</p><ul><li><strong>Resy statement credit</strong>: Up to $50 in semiannual statement credits for U.S. Resy restaurants (enrollment required). You can use the same gift card strategy as with the Platinum and Sapphire Reserve cards, but with a much smaller payout relative to the effort.</li><li><strong>$10 monthly dining statement credit</strong>: Up to $10 in monthly statement credits at select merchants (enrollment required). It&#x27;s genuinely hard to use from abroad, since eligible merchants are mostly U.S.-based delivery and takeout brands.</li><li><strong>Uber Cash</strong>: Up to $10 in Uber Cash each month toward U.S. Uber rides and Uber Eats orders when you add your Amex Gold as a payment method and pay with any Amex card. It doesn&#x27;t work outside the U.S. at all.</li><li><strong>Dunkin&#x27; statement credit</strong>: Requires reloading your Dunkin&#x27; app balance for just $7 every single month to maximize at U.S. Dunkin&#x27; locations (enrollment required) — a lot of upkeep for a small return.</li></ul><p>That&#x27;s a lot of small, frequent maintenance for a card with a $325 annual fee (see <a href="/tc/6344?pid=6344&amp;name=American%20Express%C2%AE%20Gold%20Card&amp;data-testid=ratesFees" target="_blank">rates and fees</a>).</p><p><strong>Related: <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/maximize-amex-gold-credits-abroad/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">How to maximize Amex Gold credits when traveling abroad</a></strong></p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="woman at computer" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2024/06/Frustrated-person-using-computer-while-working-in-office-at-home_FreshSplash.jpg" /><figcaption>FRESHSPLASH/GETTY IMAGES</figcaption></figure><p>Compare that to the Platinum&#x27;s statement credit (up to $100 in statement credits quarterly for U.S. Resy purchases; enrollment required) or the Sapphire Reserve&#x27;s twice-yearly $150 credits, both of which only need one or two gift card top-ups a year, and it&#x27;s clear why the larger, less frequent statement credits are easier to justify chasing.</p><p>If you&#x27;d rather skip the gift card step entirely, the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/6396?name=Marriott+Bonvoy+Brilliant%C2%AE+American+Express%C2%AE+Card&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=6396" target="_blank">Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant® American Express® Card</a> — which carries a $650 annual fee (see <a href="/tc/6396?pid=6396&amp;name=Marriott%20Bonvoy%20Brilliant%C2%AE%20American%20Express%C2%AE%20Card&amp;data-testid=ratesFees" target="_blank">rates and fees</a>) — offers a simpler alternative: up to $25 per month in statement credits worldwide at any restaurant that accepts Amex.</p><p>It explicitly excludes gift card purchases, so there&#x27;s no work-around, just the plain requirement that you remember to use it when you&#x27;re actually eating out. Less to gain but also less friction.</p><p><strong>Related: <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/reviews/marriott-bonvoy-brilliant-amex-card-review?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant Amex review: Marriott&#x27;s most premium card</a></strong></p><h2 class="rssH2">Bottom line</h2><p>This strategy turned my restaurant credits into real, meaningful savings, including a $4 dinner in Austin that would have otherwise cost $304. It works best on cards with larger, less frequent statement credits, like the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/22504813?name=American+Express+Platinum+Card%C2%AE&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=22504813" target="_blank">Amex Platinum</a> and the <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/221211836?name=Chase+Sapphire+Reserve%C2%AE&amp;issuer=Chase&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=221211836" target="_blank">Sapphire Reserve</a>, and less well on smaller ones, which is why I stopped bothering with my <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/oc/6344?name=American+Express%C2%AE+Gold+Card&amp;issuer=American+Express&amp;utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=395489&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy&amp;utm_term=6344" target="_blank">Amex Gold</a>.</p><p>If you&#x27;ve got a restaurant credit sitting unused, it&#x27;s worth five minutes to check whether that restaurant sells gift cards.
