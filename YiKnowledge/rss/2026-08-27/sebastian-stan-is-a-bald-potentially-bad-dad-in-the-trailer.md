<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/05/23174851/fjord-feature.jpg" /><p>Shedding his <a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-apprentice-review-roy-cohn-and-donald-trumps-dark-1851496112">Donald Trump wig</a> and Bucky Barnes locks, Sebastian Stan returns to the screen as we&#8217;ve never seen him before: Bald. In <i>Fjord</i>, director Cristian Mungiu&#8217;s latest film, Stan plays Mihai, the Gheorghjiu family&#8217;s domineering patriarch. At the behest of Mihai&#8217;s wife, Lisbet (Stan&#8217;s <i>A Different Man </i>co-star Renate Reinsve), the family of seven moves to her hometown, a remote coastal village in Norway. But Mihai&#8217;s devout Romanian beliefs don&#8217;t jibe with neighbors. After growing close with the family nearby, Mihai becomes the subject of scrutiny for the community, leading to child endangerment allegations that put them on the stand.&nbsp;</p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p>Directed by Cristian Mungiu, <i>Fjord</i> won this year&#8217;s Palme d&#8217;Or, marking the filmmaker&#8217;s second prestigious golden leaf, becoming one of 10 filmmakers, including Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, and Ruben Östlund, to double up on commemorative plants. He didn&#8217;t have to go through all that trouble to make <i>Fjord </i>look compelling. Stan&#8217;s head is already doing the heavy lifting.&nbsp;</p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p><i>Fjord</i> opens in the U.S. on October 9.</p>
