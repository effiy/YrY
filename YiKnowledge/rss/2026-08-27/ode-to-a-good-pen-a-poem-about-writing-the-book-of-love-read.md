<p>I spent the summer using the <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2025/06/07/gianni-rodari-the-grammar-of-fantasy/">fantastic binomial</a> technique developed by Gianni Rodari &#8212; the beloved Italian writer whose stories lit up my Bulgarian childhood &#8212; as a creative prompt for poetry, part of the larger binomial two people co-create when their worlds touch each other in a meaningful way. Each week I&#8217;d be given two unrelated words and tasked with twining them into a poem. </p>
<p>Summers end. Worlds tilt away from each other, drift apart, resume their orbit, transformed. This is how the final binomial &#8212; &#8220;dust&#8221; and &#8220;life&#8221; &#8212; wrote itself in me, read here by the living poem that is <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/tag/nick-cave/">Nick Cave</a>. </p>
<p></p>
<blockquote><p><strong>ODE TO A GOOD PEN</strong><br />
<em>by Maria Popova</em></p>
<p>Over and over<br />
we borrow the book of love<br />
from the lending library of the possible<br />
and ask of it<br />
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;everything,<br />
only to find its pages<br />
blank and beckoning,<br />
impelling us<br />
to keep writing the story<br />
as it keeps changing,<br />
keeps reading us<br />
back to ourselves &#8212;<br />
an endless translation<br />
from some other tongue,<br />
unfinished and unfinishable,<br />
written in dust<br />
between endpapers<br />
marbled with life.</p></blockquote>
<p>Then, <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2025/01/17/forgiveness/">&#8220;Forgiveness.&#8221;</a></p>
