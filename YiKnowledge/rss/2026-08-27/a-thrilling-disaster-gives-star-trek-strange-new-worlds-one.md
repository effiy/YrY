<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/26223259/MixCollage-26-Aug-2026-09-32-PM-8560.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When it comes to embodying the defining ideals of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Star Trek, Strange New Worlds </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">has a very mixed track record.</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">On the positive side, it’s continued and expanded Gene Roddenberry’s commitment to diversity, particularly with how many women are main players on this version of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise. </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">On the negative side, it struggles to use its sci-fi metaphors to say</span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/star-trek-strange-new-worlds-recap-season-3-episode-7"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">anything meaningful</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> about the world we live in now, even as it aesthetically dabbles in the</span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/star-trek-strange-new-worlds-recap-season-4-episode-4-a-case-of-chiaroscuro"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">morally gray</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. And it’s been all over the place when it comes to the simple pleasures of watching a highly trained, science-minded crew tackle anything the universe throws at them. Sometimes Pike and company have a real sense of duty and competence to their characterizations. Other times the show deploys a goofy hangout vibe that makes them feel more like the teenagers of</span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/star-trek-starfleet-academy-review-tv-paramount-plus"> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Starfleet Academy</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> or the action junkies of the J.J. Abrams films.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The thrill of “Off Hour” is how much it addresses that latter concern in particular. It’s real-time disaster-movie riff&nbsp; puts our heroes through the kind of high-stakes scenario you could easily imagine causing chaos and yelling in those other two </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trek </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">series—only this one highlights how the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Strange New Worlds</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> crew are able to pull it together when it matters most. Emotions still run high and people still make imperfect, risky decisions as the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">gets sucked into a deadly slipstream wormhole that will rip the ship apart in less than an hour. But collectively, the crew are stronger together than they are apart—able to pull each other back from panic attacks and balance the cool, calm, collected attitude you need in a crisis with the kind of gut instinct and hope that has always been an unspoken part of the Starfleet ethos too. That leads to one of the most exhilarating, emotional hours the show has ever delivered.</span></p><!-- admarker --> <!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In fact, this </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Poseidon Adventure </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">riff made me realize I care about these characters and their relationships to one another in a way I’m not sure I would have identified beforehand. Without an alien world to establish or any one-off guest stars to introduce, this bottle episode has space for the whole ensemble to shine. The dynamics between Chapel/M’Benga and Scotty/Pelia are the clear standouts here, but just about everyone is well served, from Cadet A’Sha proving her mettle to Una, Ortegas, Uhura, Spock, La’An, and Pike calmly figuring out priorities and how to communicate them with the whole ship. Ortegas even gets to take the captain’s chair for a while!</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What “Off Hour” captures so well is how much the personal and professional mix in the kind of workplace family of a Starfleet ship. The pre-disaster fight between M’Benga and Chapel is kind of about work. She’s mad he’s brought on former </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">interim CMO Dr. Boyce (Brian Markinson) as a potential replacement in case he decides to leave one day. He’s mad she’s been serving on the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">with one foot out the door for some time now. Yet it’s clear their actual concerns are far more personal. Their time in the Klingon War gave them a complicated trauma bond that’s wrapped up in their deep understanding of one another. “Whenever things get real, you run,” M’Benga tells Chapel. Naturally, this ship-wide disaster puts her in a situation that tests that impulse.</span></p><!-- admarker --> <!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s a simple but incredibly well executed throughline, with Babs Olusanmokun and Jess Bush turning in their best work yet on the series. They wind up in what feels like a classic </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grey’s Anatomy </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">sweeps week episode as a heavy table all but slices M’Benga in half; with only the magnetized force of the table itself holding him together, which makes for an eerie visual. Medical protocols (and Dr. Boyce’s literal orders) tell Chapel to ease his pain and leave him to die. But their loving friendship inspires her to stay and pull off the impossible—or at least die alongside him if she can’t. It&#8217;s a story that gets its power from how tangible and human it all feels. These are two old friends thrust into an agonizing situation, trying their best to be brave and selfless for one another. As it turns out, sometimes letting the personal override the professional </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">is </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">the way to save the day.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">&nbsp;</span></p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Even more impressive, “Off Hour” manages to wring just as much pathos from a storyline that emphasizes the professional over the personal too. Scotty and Pelia don’t have the kind of intense friendship that Chapel and M’Benga do. Instead, they’re bonded by their mutual love of what they do for work, which, in this case, means they’re bonded by their mutual love of the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise. </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">As engineers, they have a different relationship to the physical ship than anyone else onboard—and a unique weight of responsibility on their shoulders too. There’s a great sense of continuity to “Off Hour,” which remembers not just M’Benga’s seldom mentioned daughter but also Scotty’s trauma over being the sole survivor of the Gorn attack on the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Stardiver</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> too.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s Pelia who helps him work through that as Carol Kane balances her always sharp comedic instincts with a welcome bit of dramatic heft. Pelia makes the case that every ship glitches sometimes, but every ship is capable of miracles too. A good engineer can listen to the bones of the vessel rather than the official computer readings in order to make those miracles happen, which we eventually see manifest in the absolutely gorgeous shot of sparks flying behind Scotty as he reaches out to touch the ship’s walls. As with Scotty’s friendship with Ortegas, it’s a best-case scenario for this kind of prequel series. It doesn’t alter canon, it expands it. Now every time we watch James Doohan’s Scotty secure his</span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t9SVhg6ZENw"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">“reputation as a miracle worker”</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Original Series, </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">we can imagine</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">it’s Pelia’s influence living on—which doubles as a nice way to weave more of a female presence into the male-centric 1960s show.&nbsp;&nbsp;</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">&nbsp;</span></p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Compared to all that, Una’s leg-shattering climb to a manual control panel and Spock and La’An’s dangerous space walks are a little more basic disaster-movie material, although they certainly fit with the personal/professional theme. All three characters prove willing to sacrifice themselves for the rest of the crew, even as it’s La’An’s personal history with Spock that lets her mind meld with him when she needs some crucial data. Still, they’re throughlines that mostly get by on style. Both the onscreen countdown clock and the shifting comic book panel aesthetic are clever ways to give this episode a distinctive visual look even though it unfolds on familiar sets. And though the idea that the “strong force neutrinos” in the wormhole can heal M’Benga </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">refuel the ship is a slightly silly resolution, at least the episode lampshades that in its technobabble.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Indeed, this close to the end of its run, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Strange New Worlds </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">probably isn’t going to suddenly become a tightly written show like </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Next Generation </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">or a morally complex one like </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Deep Space Nine. </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">But “Off Hour” proves it still has plenty of time to recalibrate its dials in a more </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trek-</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ian direction even as it maintains the bombast and movie-inspired premises the writers love so much. Chapel ultimately ends this hour ready to fully commit to the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and that’s how I felt too. After two lackluster episodes in a row, that’s just what the doctor (and nurse) ordered.</span></p>
