<p><strong>&#8220;American Dream&#8221; (Criterion)<br />&#8220;Harlan County, USA&#8221; (Criterion)</strong></p>



<p>A new film by Barbara Kopple is an event for documentary lovers, and her &#8220;Union Town&#8221; premieres at TIFF next month. Kopple is quite simply one of the best non-fiction filmmakers of all time, and Criterion honored her this with month with the inclusion of two of her best films into the collection. Her masterpiece will probably always be &#8220;Harlan County, USA,&#8221; Kopple&#8217;s film about Appalachian miners fighting for their rights via the Brookside Mine strike in the &#8217;70s. It won the Oscar for Best Documentary and really put Kopple on the map. The Criterion release includes not just a new 4K restoration but audio commentary, making-of documentary, and even an interview with John Sayles. Kopple&#8217;s follow-up, &#8220;American Dream&#8221; has also been given a Criterion 4K release with an interview and a conversation with Studs Terkel. These are great, essential releases.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/epic-elvis-presley-in-concert-baz-luhrmann-concert-film-review-2026">EPiC: Elvis Presley in Concert</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>When Baz Luhrmann was making &#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/elvis-movie-review-2022">Elvis</a>,&#8221; he came across hours of footage that had never been seen before, including an audio recording of Presley talking about his life and career. Much of the concert footage needed detailed audio recovery, which led the Oscar-nominated director to the production of this remarkable document, a movie that&#8217;s part bio-doc and part concert film. One of the most successful documentaries in years, this was a pretty sizable hit for Neon, who have now given it a stunning 4K home treatment. Of course, the most important thing: The audio mix here is incredible. I&#8217;m not sure why I put off seeing this for so long. I think I thought it was just another Elvis highlights reel. It&#8217;s not. There&#8217;s a passion to the assembly here by Luhrmann that illuminates his admiration and respect for the King in a manner that&#8217;s contagious. It&#8217;s fun, funny, and, of course, the music rocks.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/exit-8-video-game-movie-review-2026">Exit 8</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>Kazunari Ninomiya stars in this adaptation of the 2023 video game of the same name, a movie that feels of a piece with the trend in horror this year of ordinary situations becoming surreal and terrifying in films like &#8220;Backrooms,&#8221; &#8220;It Ends,&#8221; and even &#8220;Obsession.&#8221; This one is the story of a man who essentially gets stuck in a neverending loop in an underground subway tunnel. As he tries to figure out how to escape, the anomalies speak to his real-world anxieties and fears. It&#8217;s a smart movie that deserves more attention on the VOD and Blu-ray market, although the Neon physical release is a little slight. (What&#8217;s up with no digital codes, Neon?)</p>

Director Damian McCarthy does wonders with forced POV and deep shadows. It&#8217;s a smart, riveting piece of genre filmmaking, one of the best horror films of 2026.</p>

<p>Long before he was making Peak Dad Cinema like &#8220;Ford v Ferrari&#8221; and &#8220;A Complete Unknown,&#8221; James Mangold directed this mind-fuck of a horror film, a flick that rode a wave of &#8220;twist&#8221; movies in the wake of hits like &#8220;The Usual Suspects&#8221; and &#8220;The Sixth Sense,&#8221; and has earned quite a cult following over the years. So it makes sense that they would give this John Cusack flick about ten strangers stuck in an isolated motel with a serial killer the full Arrow treatment. The new release includes HDR versions of both the theatrical and extended cuts, along with new interviews with star Pruitt Taylor Vince and producter Cathy Konrad. It also includes previously available material like deleted scenes and a commentary with Mangold. For Arrow, it&#8217;s a little slight, but slight for Arrow is better-than-average for most companies.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;Ju-On: The Grudge&#8221;</strong></p>



<p>The influence of J- and K-Horror from the late &#8217;90s and early &#8217;00s still feels a tad underrated. Movies like &#8220;Ringu&#8221; and &#8220;The Eye&#8221; have a surreal visual language that clearly influenced the 2026 kings of horror like Curry Barker and Kane Parsons, with their chilling visions of reality gone askew. One of the most important films of this era was Takashi Shimizu&#8217;s &#8220;Ju-On: The Grudge,&#8221; which was technically the third film in a franchise that continued for years beyond this release, including 2004 and 2019 American remakes. The imagery most associated with J-Horror can be traced back to &#8220;Ringu&#8221; and &#8220;The Grudge,&#8221; and Arrow has given it a fantastic physical release with tons of special features, including deleted scenes, interviews, featurettes, and even a commentary with Sam Raimi, who produced the first American remake, along with excellent new writing on the film by some horror experts.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/kill-bill-the-whole-bloody-affair-quentin-tarantino-film-review">Kill Bill: The Whole Bloody Affair</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>In late 2025, the Movie Gods finally got around to gifting us with the long-awaited single film version of Quentin Tarantino&#8217;s &#8220;Kill Bill,&#8221; originally released in two volumes in 2003 and 2004. Conceived as one cinematic experience, Tarantino had cut the films back together into one back in 2011, but it took almost another 15 years for it to get a digital and physical media release. The changes are most notable around the connection between the two volumes (the survival of B.B. isn&#8217;t revealed at the end of the first volume anymore), along with the &#8220;House of Blue Leaves&#8221; sequence now being in color and O-Ren Ishii&#8217;s anime sequence running longer. The physical release also includes &#8220;The Lost Chapter: Yuki&#8217;s Revenge,&#8221; a Fortnite-related animated short that played with &#8220;Whole Bloody Affair&#8221; in theaters.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-people-vs-larry-flynt-1996">The People vs. Larry Flynt</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>I always loved how much Roger loved Milos Forman&#8217;s &#8220;The People vs. Larry Flynt.&#8221; He wrote, &#8220;Milos Forman’s “The People vs. Larry Flynt” argues that the freedom of speech must apply to unpopular speech, or it is meaningless. Beginning with this belief, Forman constructs a fascinating biopic about a man who went from rags to riches by never overestimating the taste of his readers.&#8221; A story of fighting against the systems that are trying to destroy free speech in this country couldn&#8217;t be timelier in 2026, which makes the drop of this gorgeous Arrow Home Video release even more essential than Forman or Flynt could have imagined. Arrow has not only remastered the film in 4K but commissioned new interviews with the brilliant writers Scott Alexander and Larry Karaszewski, star Woody Harrelson, and producer Janet Yang. They&#8217;ve also included a ton of great archival special features, including two commentaries, a 1997 interview with Forman, and a featurette about Flynt narrated by Dennis Hopper. This is one of the essential movies of the &#8217;90s, given its best physical media treatment to date.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/power-ballad-paul-rudd-nick-jonas-film-review-2026">Power Ballad</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>John Carney earnestly believes in the power of music to change your life. You can see that belief in every frame of &#8220;Once,&#8221; &#8220;Sing Street,&#8221; and even &#8220;Flora and Son.&#8221; His contagious love for creative expression infuses this fun comedy with a big heart. It&#8217;s the story of a wedding singer (Paul Rudd) who spends a drunken night with a pop star (Nick Jonas), only to discover that the heartthrob has taken his tune and turned it into a hit. Who owns creativity? And what does it mean to sing a song that you don&#8217;t feel in your heart? Rudd and Jonas are great in a movie that&#8217;s consistently engaging, and lands with one of 2026&#8217;s most satisfying twists.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/project-hail-mary-ryan-gosling-movie-review-2026">Project Hail Mary</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>The 4K release of this gigantic box office hit looks and sounds spectacular, which is the most important thing about this release, but it does feel kind of slight in the bonus material department for one of 2026&#8217;s biggest films. People may have forgotten a bit about Rocky and the story of &#8220;Project Hail Mary&#8221; in the wake of the Summer 2026 blockbusters, but I still suspect this film will have a resurgence on home video, not just for fans who want to see it again, but as it becomes an awards season major player. (It&#8217;s getting into Best Picture and Actor, almost certainly.) &#8220;PHM&#8221; was a phenomenon because it crossed so many demographics, appealing to both young and old viewers, and because it presented a relatable story of a reluctant hero, someone forced to be a savior who never thought he could become one. It&#8217;s a truly well-made piece of popcorn entertainment that plays nearly as well at home as it did in theaters. (Note: This is another release in the column without a digital code. A disconcerting trend emerging.) </p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-hugh-jackman-movie-review-2026">The Sheep Detectives</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>The best family film of the year has been given a lackluster physical media release, one with no special features at all, and not even a digital copy. Maybe Criterion or Arrow could one day save this one, but until then just enjoy the story of a group of mystery-loving sheep who get wrapped up in solving the murder of their shepherd. A sort of hybrid of Agatha Christie and &#8220;Babe,&#8221; this is a movie that works for all ages.</p>

<p><strong>&#8220;<a href="https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/star-wars-the-mandalorian-and-grogu-pedro-pascal-movie-review-2026">Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu</a>&#8220;</strong></p>



<p>Audiences couldn&#8217;t shake the feeling that they were being asked to pay theatrical prices for a mini-season of a Disney+ show, which led to Jon Favreau&#8217;s blockbuster becoming one of the few true bombs in the &#8220;Star Wars&#8221; universe. While $345 million worldwide sounds like a lot, it&#8217;s actually the lowest gross for a live-action &#8220;SW&#8221; film. The relatively awful previews that made Favreau&#8217;s flick look duller than it actually is didn&#8217;t help. I&#8217;ll admit to kind of dreading it, but actually found it goes down relatively smoothly at home, where the sense that you&#8217;re watching extended TV doesn&#8217;t hurt as much. It&#8217;s still a misfire (the Ratta CGI is criminal), but not the disaster you may have heard. And the physical release is pretty strong, including featurettes and a Favreau commentary.</p>
