<em>"Don't they have to prove what they claim?"</em> Neon has revealed the first official US trailer for the film titled <strong><em>Fjord</em></strong>, the latest from Romanian filmmaker <strong>Cristian Mungiu</strong> following <a href="https://www.firstshowing.net/2023/official-us-trailer-for-cristian-mungius-latest-r-m-n-from-romania/"><em>R.M.N.</em></a> a few years ago. This first premiered at the 2026 Cannes Film Festival earlier this year to a divisive set of reviews, with lots of love and hate across the board. <em>Fjord</em> went on to win the <a href="https://www.firstshowing.net/2026/cannes-2026-awards-mungiu-wins-his-second-palme-dor-for-fjord/" title="Cannes 2026 Awards: Mungiu Wins His Second Palme d'Or for 'Fjord'">Palme d'Or top prize</a> at Cannes, though in my opinion the entirely wrong pick. Set and filmed entirely in Norway. The Gheorghiu family, with a Romanian father and a Norwegian mother, have moved to the mother's birthplace, a tiny Norwegian village on a Fjord, befriending the neighboring Halberg family. When the Gheorghius are suspected of disturbing behavior regarding their children, their lives are thrown into chaos as they become the center of scrutiny. Starring <strong>Sebastian Stan</strong> as Mihai and <strong>Renate Reinsve</strong> as Lisbet, along with <strong>Lisa Carlehed</strong>, <strong>Henrikke Lund-Olsen</strong>, <strong>Vanessa Ceban</strong>, <strong>Jonathan Ciprian Breazu</strong>, <strong>Ellen Dorrit Petersen</strong>, <strong>Lisa Loven Kongsli</strong>, <strong>Markus Tønseth</strong>. While it is a well-made film with good performances, it's an entirely misguided and politically problematic depiction of modern society - as covered <a href="https://www.firstshowing.net/2026/cannes-2026-the-misaligned-politics-of-cristian-mungius-fjord-film/" title="Cannes 2026: The Misaligned Politics of Cristian Mungiu's 'Fjord' Film">in our Cannes review</a>.
