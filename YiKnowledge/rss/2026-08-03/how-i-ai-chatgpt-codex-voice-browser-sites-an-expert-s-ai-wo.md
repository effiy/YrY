Nick Baumann demos the new interface live: he triggers it with a hotkey, the orb reads what&#8217;s on his screen through Appshots, and Codex spins up separate threads to book a flight, file an expense report, and check his calendar at the same time. </p></li><li><p><strong>One of Codex&#8217;s most powerful features is also one of its least understood: thread-forking.</strong> When a task gets complicated, Codex can open a new thread, branch from an existing one, or pull context from previous conversations on its own. Users don&#8217;t have to manually organize everything first.</p></li><li><p><strong>Heartbeats in ChatGPT Work offer a practical glimpse of always-on agents.</strong> Nick gets notified when a suspicious charge appears, a package is nearby, or an email needs his attention&#8212;and he set it all up without writing code.</p></li><li><p><strong>ChatGPT Sites looks much closer to a real deployment platform than a lightweight demo.</strong> It includes a SQL database, file storage, environment variables, and email-based access controls. During the episode, Claire and Nick build and publish a live How I AI tips site, organized by tool and job function, in roughly the time it would normally take to set up a CMS template. For internal tools, small apps, and private resources, that starts to feel genuinely useful.</p></li><li><p><strong>Nick&#8217;s video-editing workflow is one of the clearest examples of AI cutting real production work.</strong> He records 50 or 60 raw clips, uploads them, talks through the rough story he wants, and lets a custom plugin transcribe everything, scan the footage, choose the strongest takes, and assemble finished vertical videos overnight. What would normally eat up most of a content team&#8217;s day is largely handled while he sleeps.</p></li><li><p><strong>Voice may be the best way to give AI context because it removes the pressure of figuring out exactly what to type.</strong> Claire calls this the &#8220;yapper&#8217;s API,&#8221; and the idea is simple: people often get more useful results when they talk freely than when they stare at an empty prompt box trying to be precise. Across two-person voice chat, mobile dictation, and Codex&#8217;s orb interface, the same pattern keeps showing up&#8212;people who talk to AI tend to give it more context, and more context usually leads to better work.</p></li><li><p><strong>As AI gets more capable, latency is becoming a much bigger product decision.</strong> Nick often uses the highest-intelligence mode in Voice, but once a model has the tools it needs, speed can matter more than another small jump in reasoning quality.
