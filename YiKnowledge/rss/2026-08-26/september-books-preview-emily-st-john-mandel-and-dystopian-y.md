<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25134544/MixCollage-24-Aug-2026-10-05-PM-1026.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The most wonderful time of the year in publishing isn’t Christmas; it’s fall. With big-name authors dropping new titles, buzzy sequels hitting shelves, and highly anticipated debuts arriving at a breakneck pace throughout September and October—this is the industry getting </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ready </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">for all those holiday shoppers—this time of year is a paradise for readers of all stripes. But, let’s not kid ourselves, the neverending stream of new releases can feel like a </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">lot</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.&nbsp; To help cut through the noise, we’re switching things up and offering a recommendation&nbsp;</span></p>
<hr class="brandeda" />
<h3><strong>Fiction:</strong> <strong><em>Exit Party</em> by Emily St. John Mandel (September 15)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Set in 1931 in an alternate version of the United States which has collapsed into regional factions, Mandel’s latest strings together parallel timelines, alternate realities, and disparate futures in a story that wrestles with timely dystopian themes of surveillance, chaos, resilience, totalitarianism, and loss. Protagonist Ari attends a party in Los Angeles, meant to celebrate the establishment of the new Republic Of California; the event serves as a nexus for all manner of thorny moral quandaries and what-ifs. Fans of Mandel’s work will also find intriguing connections to her previous novels.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-1859219099 aligncenter" height="453" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25135010/exit-party-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p>
<h3><strong>Thriller: <i>The French Illusion</i> by John Grisham (September 29)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The always reliable Grisham trades his traditional courtroom drama for international espionage in a fast-paced thriller about a honeymoon gone horribly wrong and a brutal kidnapping scheme. As the families race to come up with the ransom the kidnappers demand, various international agencies join forces to try and suss out the connections between this disappearance and a string of similar crimes. Full of twists and suspense.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- admarker --> <!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
<p><img alt="The French Illusion cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219100 aligncenter" height="456" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25135408/the-french-illusion-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p>
<h3><strong>Romance: <em>Grim Tidings</em> by B.K. Borison (September 15)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The second installment in Borison’s charming </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ghosted </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">series, this follow-up to last year’s (also excellent) </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Good Spirits</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> features an opposites-attract paranormal romance between a Grim Reaper and a Guardian Angel whose connection forces them both to wrestle with identity, connection, and grief. Equal parts whimsical and heartfelt, with a delightful grumpy-for-grumpy chemistry between its two leads, and featuring Borison’s trademark deft banter, this is a perfect love story for spooky season.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><img alt="Grim Tidings cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219102 aligncenter" height="452" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25140253/grim-tidings-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p>
<h3><strong>Historical Fiction: <i>Agrippa </i>by Robert Harris (September 1)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For those whose Roman Empire is, well… the Roman Empire, the latest novel from the author of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Conclave</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> offers readers an unconventional figure to obsess over. Harris’ book takes the form of a fictional memoir from Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa, the Roman general and statesman who served as the right-hand man to Caesar Augustus and is, for the most part, sidelined in our larger discussions of the period. Featuring familiar historical faces, frequently bloody political wrangling, and a thorny central friendship, the novel is an excellent mix of fact and imagination.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><img alt="Agrippa Cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219103 aligncenter" height="456" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25140354/agrippa-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p><!-- admarker --> <!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<h3><strong>Fantasy: <i>The Wretched Divine </i>by Adalyn Grace (September 22)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The adult fantasy debut from the author of the popular YA </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Belladonna</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> trilogy, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Wretched Divine</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> features knights, demons, angels, and a centuries-spanning war between them all. Human knight Aurelie lost most of her family to a demon attack when she was a child. Now grown, she’s preparing to soulbond with an angel in the hopes of helping her people finally end this ancient conflict. But when an unforeseen tragedy forces her to form a second bond with a secretive demon, everything she thought she once knew is thrown into chaos. The complicated dynamic between the three leads is intriguing, and the book ends on a breakneck cliffhanger that will leave readers eager for a sequel.</span></p>
<p><img alt="The Wretched Divine cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219106 aligncenter" height="444" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25141310/the-wretched-divine-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p><!-- admarker --> 
<h3><strong>Romantasy:</strong> <strong><i>The Knave And The Moon</i> by Rachel Gillig (September 1)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The highly anticipated follow-up to Gillig’s BookTok favorite </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Knight And The Moth</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, this sequel promises higher stakes and darker mysteries. The story picks up immediately following its predecessor’s devastating ending: Sybil Delling, a former nameless Diviner (read: prophetic seer) of the now-ruined Aisling Cathedral, is a prisoner, forced to wed the cruel king she once would have named a friend. But when a mysterious knight with no memory shows up at the tournament being held to show off King Benji’s power, Sybil may have found the answer to writing her kingdom an entirely new story. Expect plenty of lush imagery, palpable yearning, and fiction’s most adorable sentient gargoyle.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><img alt="The Knave and the Moon cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219107 aligncenter" height="450" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25141411/the-knave-and-the-moon-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p>
<h3><strong>Science Fiction: <i>Scion</i> by James Islington (September 1)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">James Islington’s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Hierarchy </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">series is some of the best, most inventive fantasy writing on shelves today. (Consider this a heads up to give these books a try before the third installment arrives late next year.) But the author is dipping his toe into a new genre this fall with </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Scion</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a fast-paced science fiction thriller. The high-concept cyberpunk story follows a bioengineered assassin often charged with killing “Lifers,” a.k.a the rich who can pay exorbitant amounts to medically extend their life expectancy. But when he accepts a job protecting an ultra-wealthy client, he finds himself swept up in a complicated conspiracy that may hold the key to answers about the death of a friend from several years prior. The story features considerably less world-building than the sprawling trilogies Islington is famous for, but his strong prose and dry wit remain.</span></p>
<p><img alt="Scion cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219108 aligncenter" height="453" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25141529/scion-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p><!-- admarker --> 
<h3><strong>Horror: <i>Trad Wife</i> by Sarah Langan (September 29)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Influencer fiction is all the rage right now, from thrillers that put social media fame front and center to dystopian tales about the dangers of internet notoriety and the surveillance state. Sarah Langan’s </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trad Wife</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is a psychological horror story that follows a thirtysomething journalist who loses her job after she’s targeted by online trolls. Desperate for work, Jenny agrees to profile the megapopular Mia Wright, a trad wife influencer who beams her perfect life at Black Swan Farms out to millions of eager fans. But something much darker seems to be lurking around the edges of her carefully curated world. A tense, creepy exploration of gender, oppression, and complicity that treats both its protagonists with care.</span></p>
<p><img alt="Trad Wife cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219109 aligncenter" height="455" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25141556/trad-wife.jpg" width="300" /></p>
<h3><strong>Young Adult:</strong> <strong><i>Winterveil</i> by Ava Reid (September 15)</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dystopian fiction is </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">so </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">back. You probably could have guessed this from the fact that there’s a new </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Hunger Games</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> movie in theaters in November, but the genre’s suddenly everywhere at bookstores too. And although author Ava Reid may be best known for her unique fairytale retellings (</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Wolf And The Woodsman</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">) and dark fantasy (</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Innamorata</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">), her latest novel, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Winterveil</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, proves that she’s just as skilled at crafting creepy dystopian worlds too.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Set in an alternate America where the Cold War never really stopped, </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Winterveil </span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">is a thoroughly addictive tale of four teenagers sent to a hidden military academy where they’re trained to use their unique skill sets—forgery, fraud, strategy, a photographic memory, a penchant for violence— in the service of a secretive CIA mission that’s more morally complicated than any of them anticipated.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><img alt="Winterveil cover" class="size-full wp-image-1859219111 aligncenter" height="453" src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/25141756/winterveil-cover.jpg" width="300" /></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
