<p>We may earn a commission from links on this page.</p><p><em>Sugar</em> on Apple TV is remarkable in a lot of ways. One part old-school throwback to classic noir (complete with literal flashes of classic films), one part unexpected sci-fi twister, and one part Colin Farrell&rsquo;s charisma, it&rsquo;s a show that defies easy categorization while being very easy to simply enjoy. </p><p>The big twist was divisive, but worked exceptionally well because it wasn&rsquo;t just a twist for shock value&mdash;it recontextualized the story and John Sugar&rsquo;s character in exciting ways that honored its noir roots. If you want more stories of deadpan detectives navigating a grimy world of secrets, mysteries, and violence (with a few surprises thrown in), there are plenty of <a href="https://lifehacker.com/entertainment/shows-like-sugar-to-watch-next" target="_blank">shows to check out</a>. If you&rsquo;ve been there and done that, there&rsquo;s one movie that all fans of <em>Sugar</em> should probably watch: 1955&rsquo;s <em>Kiss Me Deadly</em>.</p><h2 id="why-fans-of-sugar-will-enjoy-kiss-me-deadly-1955">Why fans of <em>Sugar </em>will enjoy <em>Kiss Me Deadly</em> (1955)</h2><div class="mx-auto mt-8 w-full max-w-4xl aspect-video">
    <div id="video-container-01M15N5G228T05YR7JJYBGMR8W"></div>
</div>


<p>You might recognize this film even if you&rsquo;ve never seen it: It&rsquo;s one of the old movies cut into <em>Sugar</em> to show John&rsquo;s inner monologue and the films he&rsquo;s watched to understand human nature. It shows up twice in season one, and the reasons why are clear: The main character, detective Mike Hammer (Ralph Meeker) drives around L.A. in a cool Corvette just like John Sugar; the story is a perfect tonal match for <em>Sugar</em>; and there&rsquo;s a big-swing, wild twist at the end that sets it apart from most other classic noirs from the middle of the 20<sup>th</sup> century.</p><p><em>Kiss Me Deadly</em> was adapted from Mickey Spillane's novel of the same title, and is one of the most influential noir films ever produced. Mike Hammer is the quintessential hard-nosed private detective who has a deep sense of ethics. Cynical and happy to solve his problems with violence, he can&rsquo;t resist pursuing true justice, so when a woman named Christina (Cloris Leachman) wanders in front of his car while wearing nothing but a coat, he offers her a ride. And when they&rsquo;re assaulted by goons and Christina is violently murdered, he wakes up in the hospital determined to find out what&rsquo;s behind her death.</p><p>Hammer is explicitly the precise type of detective that John Sugar wants to emulate, and <em>Kiss Me Deadly</em> is a story that straddles both the noir and sci-fi genres, just like the show (it&rsquo;s actually got an entry in <a href="https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/kiss_me_deadly" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction</a>). The visual style and bleak tone in the film have been referenced, repurposed, and outright stolen in hundreds of films since. If you love the noir atmosphere and shocking reveal in <em>Sugar</em>, <em>Kiss Me Deadly</em> is the perfect film to watch. It&rsquo;s actually kind of the perfect film to watch in general.
