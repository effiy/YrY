<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Northwest Arkansas has been changing. Walmart, which has been rooted in Bentonville for more than 75 years, has reshaped the small town, and now that growth is spreading into the surrounding countryside. With that change, writes Jordan P. Hickey in this poignant essay, come wealthy newcomers looking for a country lifestyle and gravel cyclists riding thousands of unpaved rural roads. And then there are the dogs—who have been here longer than any of these transplants—caught in the midst of it all. Hickey calls them &#8220;yarddogs,&#8221; finding in them a metaphor for a region in the process of transformation.</p>
<span class="__iawmlf-post-loop-links" hidden="hidden"></span>


<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Downtown Bentonville, so carefully planned, feels surreal today—&#8221;Disneyfied,&#8221; &#8220;Pleasantville,&#8221; &#8220;gentrification&#8221; are the apt words of the critics.And not that it needs to be said, but there are no yarddogs in Bentonville. Not anymore. Here, there are only good dogs. Dogs on leashes, dogs that have been to school, dogs that listen, obey, behave, and will not lunge at every passing jogger.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But, of course, this is not really about dogs at all.</p>
</blockquote>
