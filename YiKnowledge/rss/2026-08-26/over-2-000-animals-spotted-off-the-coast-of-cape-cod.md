<p class="article-paragraph skip">When most people gaze out of an <a href="https://www.popsci.com/technology/airplane-smart-glass/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">airplane window</a>, they admire the fluffy clouds slowly floating past them. But when New England Aquarium Aerial survey observers look out of a plane, they’re scouring the seas&nbsp;for marine <a href="https://www.popsci.com/category/animals/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">animals</a> swimming far below.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="article-paragraph skip">And on August 16, the team was&nbsp; rewarded—big time. While soaring over the <a href="https://www.popsci.com/exploring-obamas-seafloor-canyons-by-mini-sub/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Northeast Canyons and Seamounts Marine National Monument</a>, they spotted over 2,000 animals across 15 different marine species. The Atlantic Ocean marine sanctuary 130 miles southeast of Cape Cod was designated in 2016. It includes four underwater mountains and three deep-sea canyons, landmarks where many different animals feed.&nbsp;</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full is-resized"><img alt="an aerial view of a blue whale swimming " class="wp-image-781910" height="1365" src="https://www.popsci.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/blue-whale-survey.jpg?strip=all&#038;quality=85&#038;amp%3Bw=2048" style="width: 1004px; height: auto;" width="2048" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Blue whales are the largest animals to have ever lived on Earth.&nbsp; <em>Image: New England Aquarium.</em> </figcaption></figure>



<p class="article-paragraph skip">“The diversity of sightings was truly remarkable and a testament to why the Monument is such a special habitat,” <a href="https://www.neaq.org/person/katherine-mckenna/">Katherine McKenna</a>, a researcher at the aquarium’s Anderson Cabot Center for Ocean Life, said in a <a href="https://www.neaq.org/about-us/press-room/press-releases/aquarium-documents-2000-animals-including-11-whale-sharks-during-aerial-survey/">statement</a>. “From regular visitors like dolphins and sperm whales, to large baleen whales drawn by aggregations of zooplankton and whale sharks and manta rays following warm Gulf Stream waters, the Monument brings together an incredible variety of marine life.”</p>



<p class="article-paragraph skip">Scientists with the Anderson Cabot Center documented 1,800 dolphins, one endangered <a href="https://www.popsci.com/environment/sperm-whales-change-communication-ships/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">sperm whale</a> (<em>Physeter macrocephalus</em>), a humpback whale (<em>Megaptera novaeangliae</em>), one&nbsp;endangered fin whale (<em>Balaenoptera physalus</em>) mother with a&nbsp;calf, 11 whale sharks (<em>Rhincodon typus</em>), <a href="https://www.popsci.com/technology/manta-robot-swimming/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">manta rays</a> (genus <em>Mobula</em>), devil rays (genus <em>Mobula</em>), Chilean devil rays (<em>Mobula tarapacana</em>), and <a href="https://www.popsci.com/environment/smooth-hammerhead-shark-galapagos/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">hammerhead sharks</a> (genus <em>Sphyrna</em>). Just beyond the sanctuary, they also recorded an endangered <a href="https://www.popsci.com/environment/why-blue-whales-largest-animal/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">blue whale</a> (<em>Balaenoptera musculus</em>) and a small group of endangered fin whales.</p>

<p class="article-paragraph skip">“Seeing a variety of threatened and endangered species actively feeding in the Monument was particularly memorable and underscores the importance of protecting this productive habitat,” McKenna added. The Trump Administration has recently <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/02/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-unleashes-commercial-fishing-in-the-atlantic/">allowed commercial fishing</a> in the Monument.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="article-paragraph skip">During the aquarium’s previous aerial survey over the special habitat in July, scientists identified almost 650 animals, including over 600 dolphins, endangered sperm whales, one whale shark, and 23 pilot whales (genus <em>Globicephala</em>). A <a href="https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/csp2.12665">2022 study</a> authored mostly by Anderson Cabot Center researchers demonstrated that the habitat sustains a diverse and distinct group of marine mammals.</p>
