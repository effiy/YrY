<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/26173103/strong-zuckerberg-feature.jpg" /><p>While we haven&#8217;t been expecting Aaron Sorkin&#8217;s <em>The Social Reckoning&nbsp;</em>to be an&nbsp;<em>especially&nbsp;</em>flattering portrayal of Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg—given how Sorkin&#8217;s return to this <em>Social </em>universe 16 years later has swapped the barest traces of charisma Jesse Eisenberg brought to the role for Jeremy Strong&#8217;s meticulously observed dead eyes—it&#8217;s still brutal to see the moments that the movie&#8217;s marketing team is choosing to highlight in the lead-up to the movie&#8217;s release. In the case of the film&#8217;s first full trailer, for instance—which follows up on <a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-social-reckoning-teaser-jeremy-strong-mark-zuckerberg-aaron-sorkin">a pretty beefy teaser</a> that was rolled out back in June—it means opening with a moment where Zuck seems to only barely grasp the rules of being a human being, as he&#8217;s coached on how to respectfully nod to other human beings in a way that doesn&#8217;t make it look like he doesn&#8217;t actually know what either his neck or his smile parts are for.</p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p>Elsewhere in the trailer, Sorkin&#8217;s influences for his <em>Social Network </em>follow-up come into better focus, as it becomes clear we&#8217;re moving from the loose biopic structure of the original movie into something more akin to a political and journalistic thriller. That&#8217;s most obvious in the interactions between Jeremy Allen White&#8217;s reporter and his whistleblower source inside Facebook, played by Mikey Madison; the constant push for more information, and the ominous promises of protection he offers her, make it clear we&#8217;ve moved out of &#8220;seriocomic portrayal of nerds with too much money and power&#8221; territory into something a lot darker. (Including references to Madison&#8217;s character being motivated by watching a friend get politically radicalized by too much time on Facebook.) It&#8217;s not that there&#8217;s <em>no </em>humor on display here, just that it&#8217;s of the darkest sort—as when Bill Burr pops up to tell Zuckerberg to his face that he&#8217;s become akin to crossing guard blithely allowing traffic to plow into a bunch of schoolchildren.&nbsp;</p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p><em>The Social Reckoning&nbsp;</em>slams into theaters on October 9.</p>
