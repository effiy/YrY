<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/26104708/The_Uprising_marquee_image.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you were looking forward to Paul Greengrass&#8217; gritty, fictionalized account of the Great Uprising of 1381, in which Andrew Garfield&#8217;s lowly plowman ignites an open revolt against young King Richard II alongside Cosmo Jarvis&#8217;s historical rebel Wat Tyler, you may also be confused by recent developments in the film&#8217;s promotion.</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit -->
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With little more than two weeks before its September 10 release, Greengrass&#8217; </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Uprising</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> now invites moviegoers to &#8220;witness the rise of Robin Hood,&#8221; which we have to assume means that Garfield, whose scythe-toting role is still being referred to as &#8220;the Ploughman&#8221; in press materials and interviews, plays the legendary, and by most historical accounts, fictional English hero. In the eyes of the more severe historical buffs, such a showy reveal could sap some of the thematic immediacy from a film that delves into the perennially relevant Uprising, also known as the Peasant&#8217;s Revolt, especially when all marketing for the film had previously made </span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZVkrhHebz1Q"><span style="font-weight: 400;">zero mention of Robin Hood</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">—aside from one fleeting glimpse of a hooded archer, who could have been anybody, really.</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Focus Features, which is distributing the film in the United States, recently </span><a href="https://x.com/theuprisingmov/status/2090527590885040223"><span style="font-weight: 400;">posted a revised, Hood-focused teaser on X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, mystifying some with the creative choice to further crowd out Wat Tyler&#8217;s historical significance by featuring a universally known entity whose inclusion in the revolt isn&#8217;t necessarily a thing. Squint, however, and you might see where Greengrass is going with this. History tells us that the first documented reference to the Robin Hood myth appears in William Langland&#8217;s narrative poem </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Piers</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Plowman</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (Garfield? Maybe!), written between 1360 and 1387, and that it was appropriated by a priest-leader named John Ball during Wat&#8217;s Revolt. So, in a sense, the spirit of Robin Hood—taking back from the rich and redistributing to the poor—did indeed play some role in the Peasant&#8217;s Revolt. What&#8217;s the harm in cramming the legend himself in, too? Why so coy, Focus Features?</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Well, the dim box office returns for <a href="https://www.avclub.com/the-death-of-robin-hood-review">Hugh Jackman&#8217;s </a></span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Death</span></i> O<i><span style="font-weight: 400;">f</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Robin</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hood</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> earlier this summer might have contributed to Focus&#8217; decision to bury the arrowed lede of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Uprising</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in the first place. Perhaps Garfield&#8217;s Hood will have better luck when the newly minted </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Uprising:</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rise</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Of</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Robin</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hood</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> hits theaters on September 10. Still, that&#8217;s plenty of time to digitally squeeze in other notable and beloved titans of yore, such as Wallace and/or Gromit, Sherlock Holmes, or the immortal Mr. Humphries from </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Are</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">You</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Being</span></i> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Served?&nbsp;</span></i></p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
