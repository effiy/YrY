<img src="https://assetsio.gnwcdn.com/witcher-3-songs-of-the-past-06.jpg?width=690&amp;quality=85&amp;format=jpg&amp;auto=webp" /> <p>Since the <a href="https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/ooh-that-rumoured-witcher-3-dlc-could-be-something-really-funky-if-its-set-where-the-latest-report-suggests">persistent rumours</a> of a cheeky extra DLC for The Witcher 3 turned into <a href="https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/geralts-back-for-one-last-roach-ride-in-long-rumoured-the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-expansion-songs-of-the-past">confirmation from CD Projekt Red</a> that such a thing is on the way prior to <a href="https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/the-witcher-4">The Witcher 4</a>, there's been a nagging voice in the back of my mind. On one hand, Songs of the Past is more of an <a href="https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/best-rpgs">RPG</a> I love, the kind of unexpected gift it seems foolish to have reservations about getting a decade and change on from release. On the other, The Witcher 3 in its current form has long felt like as close to the ideal complete package as you can get.</p>
<p>Going into last night's opening salvo of Gamescom, I was expecting that the DLC's full reveal might dispel my concerns, getting me fully on board with the idea that, yes, Geralt does need one last ride before Ciri takes over going forwards. Instead, I'm still in two minds.</p>
