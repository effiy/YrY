<p>If you've invested in a Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, or Pixel 11 Pro Fold, then these are the best tricks to try out first. All of these hacks are exclusive to the 2026 flagships, though we might see some of them trickle down to older handsets over time (for now, Google isn't saying one way or the other).</p><h2 id="install-this-app-to-take-full-control-of-hilight">Install this app to take full control of HiLight</h2><p>HiLight is new to the Pixel 11, and uses a rear LED light to alert you to incoming calls from the most important people in your life&mdash;the idea being that it helps you cut out the rest of the noise associated with owning a smartphone in 2026.</p><p>You can configure HiLight from Settings by choosing <strong>System &gt; HiLight</strong>, but there's not actually much configuring to do. It only works for Gemini chats and calls from contacts marked as favorites, with a simple toggle switch in both instances. You can choose different colors for different contacts, but that's about it.</p><p>To do more with HiLight, though, you can <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/pixel-11-pro-hilight-app" target="_blank"><u>install HiLight Studio</u></a>. This third-party tool unlocks HiLight for use with more apps on your phone, and gives you full control over the colors displayed, making HiLight much more useful.</p><h2 id="use-camera-looks-filters-to-add-imperfections-to-your-photos">Use Camera Looks filters to add imperfections to your photos</h2><p>One of the Pixel 11 exclusives in the Camera app is what Google is calling Camera Looks. They work like subtle filters, similar to the Photographic Styles on iOS, with gentle adjustments to contrast and colors that give your images a different feel (and can remove the ultra-clean, over-processed vibe of Pixel photos in general).</p><p>Open the Camera and tap the controls button (lower right), then <strong>Looks</strong>. This gives you access to the <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/pixel-11-features/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>four different options</u></a> you can apply by default when snapping images: Original (the usual Pixel processing), Natural (for "relaxed details"), Shadows (for "deeper contrast"), and Vanilla (for "a warm, golden glow").</p><div class="eloquent-imagery-image">

<h2 id="limit-zooming-to-avoid-ai-generated-imagery">Limit zooming to avoid AI-generated imagery</h2><p>Here's a tip about <em>not</em> using a Pixel 11 feature. If you have the Pixel 11 and Pixel 11 Pro Fold you can go up to 30x with Super Zoom, while on the Pixel 11 Pro and the Pixel 11 Pro XL there's Pro Zoom up to an incredible 120x.</p><p>The Pro Zoom levels get you closer to the action, sure, but they also use generative AI to <a href="https://uk.pcmag.com/mobile-phones/166784/the-pixel-11-pros-camera-has-an-ai-hallucination-problem" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>enhance what's in the photo</u></a>. It's not just sharpening and enhancing; it's actually inventing new details based on the pixels that are already there (which is why you have to download a generative AI model the first time you use Pro Zoom).</p><p>The 30x Super Zoom uses AI too, but of a different type: Rather than generating details, it's combining photo frames and analyzing colors to sharpen up an image, like a traditional image editor filter. This applies up to a 30x zoom level, <a href="https://store.google.com/us/magazine/pixel-super-res-zoom" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>Google says</u></a>, so you know where to stop if you don't want any AI elements being added in.</p><p>With Pro Zoom shots, Google Photos will save both the AI-enhanced version and the raw digital zoom version&mdash;you can switch between them in the app and choose the one you like best.</p><h2 id="use-magic-capture-to-automatically-take-photos-while-recording-videos">Use Magic Capture to automatically take photos while recording videos</h2><p>It's the perennial problem of the modern age: How do you make sure you've got photo memories of an event while still staying present in the moment? Of course you'd like snaps of your kid's birthday party, but you don't want to spend the whole time glued to your phone.</p><p>This is where Magic Capture comes in: You can enable it from the Camera app by tapping the icon to the left of the photo icon, just under the main shutter button. You then tap the shutter button to start a video capture, and then let it record. You can focus on what's happening rather than looking at the Camera app and taking individual photos.</p><p>When you stop the recording, AI swings into action to pick out still images that look like highlights from the footage&mdash;so presumably someone blowing their candles out, raising a toast, or reaching the crest of a wave, depending on the scenario.</p><h2 id="use-pause-point-to-block-distractions-on-your-pixel">Use Pause Point to block distractions on your pixel</h2><p>Every phone maker has come up with multiple ways to try and keep us from getting too addicted to our devices, and the latest digital well-being idea from Google is the Pause Point feature for the Pixel 11 phones.</p><p>It's pretty simple, which means it just might work: For the apps you specify, you get a mindfulness prompt every time you open them up. Those few seconds of thought and self-reflection could help you realize that no, you don't actually need to open up Instagram or X <em>again</em> today.</p><p>To set this up, go to Settings, tap <strong>Digital Wellbeing</strong>, and then <strong>Pause Point</strong> to pick the apps you'd like it to apply to. This doesn't seem to be live yet, however, despite Google <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/pause-point/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>announcing it</u></a> and including it in its <a href="https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/9137850" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>official support documentation</u></a>. Keep an eye out for it appearing in updates over the next few weeks.</p><div class="eloquent-imagery-image">

<h2 id="set-custom-vibration-patterns-to-identify-specific-contacts">Set custom vibration patterns to identify specific contacts</h2><p>Another way to reduce screen time is to create unique alerts for each contact, so you know who's getting in touch with you just from the way your phone pings or buzzes. The Pixel 11 phones help with this by letting you customize vibrations in a couple of handy ways.</p><p>First, you can set specific vibrations for specific contacts, as well as specific audible ringtones (which has long been an option). From the Contacts app, tap on a name, then <strong>Contact ringtone</strong>, and you can choose a vibration pattern as well as a sound.</p><p>You can also change the default vibration patterns for calls and notifications, which you couldn't before&mdash;though not on a per-app or per-contact basis. From Settings, pick <strong>Sound and vibration</strong>, then <strong>Ringtone</strong> or <strong>Notification</strong>.</p><h2 id="use-the-built-in-teleprompter-to-create-content-directly-on-your-pixel">Use the built-in teleprompter to create content directly on your pixel</h2><p>Attention, content creators! If you're using your Pixel 11 to record video for public consumption, you can make use of a built-in teleprompter to read your scripts&mdash;no third-party app required.</p><p>From the Camera app, tap the video icon (to the right of the photo icon, under the shutter button), then tap the settings button up in the top left corner. If you switch to the Creator tab, you'll see there's a Teleprompter toggle switch you can turn on.</p><p>Once it's enabled, tap the Teleprompter icon on the capture window to enter your text (you can of course paste it in from somewhere else if you want). When the video recording starts, as you begin to talk through the script, it'll automatically advance for you.</p><h2 id="use-the-pixel-search-bar-to-find-your-stuff-faster">Use the Pixel search bar to find your stuff faster</h2><p>This is one of the more well-hidden new features on the Pixel 11 phones. Pixel Search is designed to make it easier to find stuff on your phone (like messages and files) from a single search bar, but it's not the search bar on the home screen.</p><p>Instead, it's the one above the app grid. Swipe up on the home screen to get to the app grid, then tap in the search box at the top. The first time you do this on your Pixel 11, you'll see an introduction to the feature, together with a <strong>Go to Settings</strong> link.</p><p>Follow that link, then <strong>Apps in search results</strong>, and you can choose the content that Pixel Search will dive into&mdash;across Google Calendar, Google Drive, Gmail, Google Photos, and Settings, for example.</p><div class="eloquent-imagery-image">

<h2 id="ask-rambler-to-edit-dictated-text">Ask Rambler to edit dictated text</h2><p>There's an exclusive Gboard add-on for Pixel 11 devices called Rambler, which Google has positioned as a super-smart upgrade on voice dictation. Phones have been able to turn speech into text for a long while now, but Rambler is better at dealing with natural speech rhythms, filler words, and pauses.</p><p>It's also better at editing. If you say "the meeting at 2pm... or rather 3pm" in your ramble, the "3pm" will make the finished transcript. If you're reviewing text Rambler has transcribed, you can launch Rambler again to make edits to it with natural prompts, too&mdash;something like "change the day from Friday to Saturday."</p><p>Load up Gboard on your Pixel 11 (any text input box will do), tap the microphone button, then read through the Rambler introduction and tap <strong>Get started</strong>. Once it's set up, it can be launched at any time through the microphone button.</p><p>You can go back to the standard voice dictation at any point, if you prefer it: From Gboard tap the menu button (the four squares on the left), then pick <strong>Settings &gt; Voice typing</strong>.</p><h2 id="use-this-screensaver-to-turn-your-pixel-11-into-a-bedside-clock">Use this screensaver to turn your Pixel 11 into a bedside clock</h2><p>You can use StandBy mode to turn an iPhone into a bedside clock, and the Pixel 11 handsets have something similar&mdash;sort of. From Settings, choose <strong>Display and touch &gt; Screen saver</strong>, and you'll see there's a new <strong>Charge</strong> option there.</p><p>This displays a progress bar showing charge status, and a clock. Enable it, and if you hook your Pixel up to a magnetic wireless charger overnight, you've got a decent, minimal bedside display that works in portrait or landscape orientations.</p>
