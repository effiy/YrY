<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/celebrity/adria_arjona"><strong>Adria Arjona</strong></a> is officially in her action star era. In <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/onslaught"><strong>Onslaught</strong></a></em>, the newest horror-action film from director <a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/celebrity/adam_wingard">Adam Wingard</a>, Arjona plays Celeste, a veteran suffering from PTSD, who lives on the fringes of society and must unleash her capacity for violence when a group of genetically enhanced soldiers escape custody and attack her and her daughter. In honor of her new film, Arjona sat down with <em><a href="https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/seen-on-the-screen-podcast/">Seen on the Screen</a></em> host Jacqueline Coley to talk about gearing up for her biggest action role yet.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“It’s so fun,” Arjona tells Coley of stepping into the horror-action genre. “I just got to do things that I would never necessarily do or was never really allowed to do, and it just felt so liberating.” Though Arjona has done some stunt work in the past — her credits in the action genre include <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/andor">Andor</a></em> and <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/6_underground">6 Underground</a></em> — she tells Coley that <em>Onslaught</em> was an entirely new experience. As the lead in the film, Arjona not only had much more to do, she also had to consider her young co-star, Blake Kennedy, who plays her daughter in the film. “There were a lot of stunts that weren’t supposed to be me, but they had to be me because she wouldn’t do it with my double,” Arjona recounts, adding that she was happy to do whatever it took to make Kennedy feel safe on set.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img alt="Adria Arjona in Onslaught (2026)" class="wp-image-296065" height="380" src="https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Seen-on-the-Screen-Onslaught-Adria_Arjona.jpg?w=700" width="700" /><figcaption><span class="media-credit">(Photo by Richard Foreman Jr./©A24)</span></figcaption></figure>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just because she’s reveling in her action star status doesn’t mean Arjona will stay in the genre. The actor, whose resumé includes <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hit_man_2023">Hit Man</a></em>, <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/blink_twice">Blink Twice</a>,</em> and <em><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/morbius">Morbius</a></em>, is not one to be pinned down. “Hopping in between genres has allowed me not to be stereotyped. It’s kind of saved my career, I think, as a Latin woman,” she tells Coley, adding that when she first arrived in Hollywood, she felt that the industry was trying to define her as “the next <a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/celebrity/michelle_rodriguez_2">Michelle Rodriguez</a>.”  So, she made the conscious decision to vary her roles as much as possible. “I think every Latin woman — and I think every actress — deserves their own spot and not to be compared, so I kind of decided to go a little bit more offbeat.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Watch Arjona’s full appearance on <em>Seen on the Screen</em> to hear her revisit some of those “offbeat” choices. Plus, she reveals why she’s “never been more at peace” than she was when working on <em>Hit Man</em> with <a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/celebrity/richard_linklater">Richard Linklater</a> and which of her former co-stars she calls “one of the best actresses of this generation.”</p>
