<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/06105009/MixCollage-06-Aug-2026-10-48-AM-5098.jpg" /><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For a while already, </span><a href="https://www.avclub.com/cinemark-joins-amc-regal-begging-for-paramount-warner-bros-merger"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Paramount&#8217;s potential acquisition</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of Warner Bros. has felt like one of the battles in a never-ending cultural and political war, pitting the Trump-friendly company against those who would rather not see one family and company control a massive segment of the media business. But if it only </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">felt</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> this way yesterday, today&#8217;s update makes this explicit. Two Republican attorneys general—Iowa&#8217;s Brenna Bird and Austin Knudsen—have filed a motion with the Supreme Court seeking to stop the antitrust lawsuit brought by 12 states and spearheaded by California AG Rob Banta, </span><a href="https://variety.com/2026/film/news/iowa-montana-lawsuit-supreme-court-block-politicized-paramount-warner-bros-california-1236843929/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">according to </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. The motion calls the antitrust lawsuit &#8220;a politicized enforcement action.&#8221;&nbsp;</span></p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">According to experts interviewed by </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Variety</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the filing is unlikely to go anywhere; one law professor referred to it as &#8220;political grandstanding.&#8221; It&#8217;s not hard to see how someone would come to that conclusion. &#8220;This case is about the legal limits on politicized antitrust enforcement by a small handful of states seeking to enjoin a $110 billion merger that the United States, most American states, and competition regulators worldwide have cleared,&#8221; reads part of the 56-page filing. This comes shortly after Bird wrote </span><a href="https://www.dailywire.com/news/to-save-american-movies-im-taking-california-to-the-supreme-court"><span style="font-weight: 400;">an op-ed in—get this—the</span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Daily Wire</span></i></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which made the culture war and red state/blue state resentment elements explicit. &#8220;Just like California should keep its hands off America’s bacon, California needs to understand that it doesn’t get to decide what Americans can choose to watch or stream,&#8221; she wrote. It was only a matter of time before someone started pushing for a corporate monopoly to own the libs.&nbsp;</span></p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
