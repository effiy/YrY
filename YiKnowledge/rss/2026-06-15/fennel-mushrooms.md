<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I thought it might be fun to highlight a few vintage cookbooks in the coming months. I pick them up here and there, without much rhyme or reason. There might be a design element I like, it might be the voice of the author, or sometimes a single recipe pops out at me. I typically flip through them in antique shops on road trips, at yard sales and flea markets, but I found this gem at <a href="http://omnivorebooks.com/">Omnivore Books</a> here in San Francisco &#8211; <i>The Seasonal Kitchen: A Return to Fresh Foods</i>. Published in 1973, it is by Perla Meyers, was published by <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holt_McDougal">Holt, Rinehart, &amp; Winston</a>, and designed by Al Corchia. I did an updated take on Perla&#8217;s Fennel Mushroom recipe for today&#8217;s post, it&#8217;s a simple, brilliant twist on everyday sauteed mushrooms with dill, chives, fresh fennel, and a kiss of creme fraiche.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><img alt="Fennel Mushrooms" border="0" src="https://images.101cookbooks.com/fennel_mushroom_recipe_2.jpg" width="620" /><img alt="Fennel Mushrooms" border="0" src="https://images.101cookbooks.com/fennel_mushroom_recipe_3.jpg" width="620" /><br />There are so many things that resonate with me in this book &#8211; subject matter, design, the emphasis on fresh, seasonal ingredients. More than anything, I love the approachability of Perla&#8217;s voice. She opens the book by saying, &#8220;Of all the reasons I have for writing The Seasonal Kitchen, the most important is to communicate and understanding of freshness and encourage a return to seasonal cooking. This is not a book for organic-food buffs, however, but for anyone willing to explore and rediscover the marvelous potential of fresh food.&#8221; Remember, this book was published nearly forty years ago. In many ways it reads like it was written yesterday.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You just don&#8217;t see cookbooks that look like this today. Even better, the ideas behind the recipes are straight-forward, produce-centric, and easy to update. To make these mushrooms, I replaced a substantial amount of sour cream with a dollop of creme fraiche, and never missed the former. And I give a few suggestions related to serving them in the head notes below. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This book was a real find, the sort of discovery that keeps me digging through stacks of books, but I&#8217;m certainly not the first to sing Perla&#8217;s praises. Perla Meyers elsewhere:<br /><br /> &#8211; <a href="http://theculinarycellar.blogspot.com/2010/04/perla-meyers-seasonal-cook.html">Perla Meyers: The Seasonal Cook</a><br /> &#8211; <a href="https://litchfieldmagazine.com/onourradar/legendary-perla-meyers/">The Legendary Perla Meyers</a></p>
