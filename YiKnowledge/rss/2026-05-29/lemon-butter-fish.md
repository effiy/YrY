<li><strong>Flexible recipe.</strong> This recipe works with so many different types of white fish, and can be tweaked and adapted to what you have on hand (throwing in any leftover herbs right into the butter sauce!), making it suitable and budget-friendly for the entire family.</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-key-ingredients-in-lemon-butter-fish">key ingredients in lemon butter fish</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-butter">Butter</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Use good quality salted butter (we love <a href="https://amzn.to/4bDImgK" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Kerrygold</a> in this recipe). If salted butter is unavailable, use unsalted butter, adding salt, to taste.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-garlic">Garlic</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">1 clove is used here but additional garlic cloves can be added for extra garlicky flavor goodness.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-lemon">Lemon</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You&#8217;ll need 1 lemon for zest, juice and lemon wedges for serving.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-parsley">Parsley</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This adds vibrant color and flavor, but can be omitted if needed.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-white-fish">White fish</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Orange roughy, snapper, sole, or rock fish fillets are all great options &#8211; they are mild, firm, and flaky white fish varieties and can be cooked in minutes.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-all-purpose-flour">All-purpose flour</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A thin, light coating of flour on the fish fillets will help create a textured, browned crust when pan-seared.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-vegetable-oil">Vegetable oil</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Vegetable oil is a neutral oil with a high smoke point, ideal for achieving that favorited golden brown crust on the fish.</p>
</div></div>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-46359" height="1140" src="https://damndelicious.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/251218_DD_Lemon-Butter-Fish_207-760x1140.jpg" width="760" /></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-make-lemon-butter-fish">How to make lemon butter fish</h2>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Make the butter sauce.</strong> Melt the butter over medium heat until the foam has subsided, watching carefully so it does not burn &#8211; it will be very golden brown in color. Stir in all the enhancements &#8211; garlic, lemon, and parsley (or any other herbs and seasonings you have on hand).</li>



<li><strong>Prep the fish.</strong> Pat the fish dry for any excess moisture before seasoning the fish with salt + pepper and coating lightly in all-purpose flour, shaking off any excess flour.</li>



<li><strong>Pan-fry the fish.</strong> Pan-sear the fish in a neutral oil (ex. vegetable oil) for that beautiful golden brown crust.</li>



<li><strong>Add finishing touches.</strong> Finish by spooning and drizzling the lemon butter sauce just before serving.</li>



<li><strong>Serve.</strong> Let the fish rest for 3-5 minutes before serving warm with <a href="https://damndelicious.net/2017/12/05/no-knead-rosemary-bread/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">crusty bread</a> and a <a href="https://damndelicious.net/2026/05/15/simple-side-salad/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">simple side salad</a>.</li>
</ol>



<div class="wp-block-group is-style-qna block-ingredients"><div class="wp-block-group__inner-container is-layout-constrained wp-block-group-is-layout-constrained">
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-pro-tips-for-the-lemon-butter-sauce">Pro tips for the lemon butter sauce</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-"></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Use cold butter.</strong> The slow melting process will help create that creamy buttery sauce, rather than a greasy puddle of melted butter.</li>



<li><strong>Lower the heat.</strong> Never let the butter come to a boil or the fat will separate and the sauce will break.</li>



<li><strong>Keep it moving.</strong> Stir frequently and constantly, keeping the butter sauce moving to force the melted butter and lemon juice to emulsify.</li>



<li><strong>A splash of wine will do wonders.</strong> Add a little bit of depth by adding a splash of white wine to the sauce.</li>



<li><strong>Add to taste.</strong> The sauce will be rich! Add a little at a time, to taste, without overpowering the fish.</li>
</ul>

<div class="wprm-recipe-notes-container wprm-block-text-normal" id="recipe-46804-notes"><h3 class="wprm-recipe-header wprm-recipe-notes-header wprm-block-text-bold wprm-align-left wprm-header-decoration-none">Notes</h3><div class="wprm-recipe-notes"><ul>
<li><strong>Salted butter goes a long way.</strong> <a href="https://amzn.to/4bDImgK" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Kerrygold salted butter</a> will work very well here (along with any other European style butter) due to its higher fat content (82% vs 80% for standard butter), providing a richer, creamier, smoother emulsification for your butter sauce.</li>
<li><strong>Pat dry.</strong> Pat the fish completely dry with paper towels to remove any surface moisture (moisture equals steam), allowing for a better sear and achieving a crispy, golden-brown crust.</li>
<li><strong>Use a neutral oil.</strong> Skip the olive oil and use a neutral-flavored oil with a high smoke point such as vegetable oil or peanut oil for pan-frying the fish, yielding a crispy exterior without burning the fish.</li>
<li><strong>Mix it up.</strong> Add additional garlic cloves or any other herbs on hand (basil, thyme, dill or rosemary), or even a teaspoon of Italian seasoning.</li>
<li><strong>Cook in batches.</strong> Work in batches to properly pan-fry the fish for that favorited golden brown crust. An overcrowded pan will steam the fish instead.</li>
<li><strong>Use fresh lemons.</strong> Fresh lemon juice is ideal here for its bright, vibrant, strong flavors, cutting through the richness of the butter, whereas bottled lemon juice may have a muted, flat taste.</li>
<li><strong>Add a splash of white wine.</strong> If you happen to have an open bottle of wine, add a splash of wine to the sauce. It will cut through the richness and further balance the flavors, transforming the lemon butter sauce into restaurant-quality goodness. Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, or unoaked Chardonnay are all solid options.</li>
<li><strong>Let it rest.</strong> Let the fish rest for 5 minutes before serving, allowing the juices to redistribute.</li>
</ul></div></div>
