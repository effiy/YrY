<h3><strong>Reviews &amp; Mini-Views</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>Ace Attorney Investigations Collection ($39.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328639" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/aceattorneyinvestigations.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>One thing I will say about the Nintendo Switch generation of gaming: it&#8217;s given us second chances at a lot of fish that got away from us in the past. <em>Trials of Mana</em>!<em> Live A Live</em>! The original<em> Fire Emblem</em>! And now, the one and only <em>Ace Attorney</em> game that remained unlocalized comes to us by way of the <em>Ace Attorney Investigations Collection</em>, a set including Miles Edgeworth&#8217;s two post-<em>Trials &amp; Tribulations</em> adventures. One thing this series has proven good at is building on plot threads established in previous titles, and the second<em> Ace Attorney Investigations</em> title does this quite well. It&#8217;s one of those sequels that makes the original better in hindsight, and it&#8217;s amazing to finally have it officially in English.</p>
<p>The <em>Ace Attorney Investigations</em> games, in focusing on Miles Edgeworth, show us how things look from the prosecution&#8217;s side of things. Outside of a couple of new gimmicks, things aren&#8217;t all that different mechanically. Search for clues, question witnesses, and try to crack each case. Still, the very different and rather cool presentation helps liven things up, and Edgeworth as a protagonist certainly lends the narration a particular feel. The pacing has less structure to it than the usual <em>Ace Attorney</em> games, and that can sometimes make some cases feel exhausting, but by and large I think anyone who enjoys the main games in the series will be happy with this sub-series. If you find the first game to be a bit of a drag, stick it through to the second. It&#8217;s a lot better, and as mentioned it makes some of the things you suffer through in the first game make more sense.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328640" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/aceattorneyinvestigations2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>In terms of bonuses, this feels more like the<em> Apollo Justice</em> set than it does the others. A gallery mode is on offer with art and music to enjoy, a story mode lets you chill out and let the game beat itself, and you have the option to switch between the original graphics/soundtrack and the fancy new stuff. There&#8217;s a dialog history you can access if you miss something that was said, which is something that should be a stock feature in games like this. I&#8217;m glad to see it.</p>
<p>The two games in <em>Ace Attorney Investigations Collection</em> offer an interesting contrast, and taken as one whole I think it&#8217;s a great experience overall. Getting the second game localized officially is amazing, and the suite of options and extras make for an excellent package. With this, every <em>Ace Attorney</em> game outside of the weird crossover with <em>Professor Layton</em> is now available on the Switch. If you like the series enough to have picked those up, you&#8217;ll definitely want to grab this as well.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Gimmick! 2 ($24.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328641" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/gimmick2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>It is enormously odd to see a sequel to <em>Gimmick!</em>. I just want to say that before moving on. One of Sunsoft&#8217;s later games for the NES, <em>Gimmick!</em> didn&#8217;t even manage to get a release in the West outside of Scandinavia. Here we are thirty years later, and not only is the original game more widely available than ever, we now have a follow-up. This game was developed by Bitwave Games with no involvement from the original creator, but it&#8217;s incredibly faithful to the original anyway. Maybe too faithful in ways for some tastes, but there&#8217;s nothing wrong with a first sequel hewing closer to the line than farther.</p>
<p>Six very lengthy levels of challenging physics-based platforming await you here, and as with the original you&#8217;re going to be up against the wall pretty quickly. There&#8217;s an easier mode this time, however, so those who want a slightly less bumpy ride can opt for it. Indeed, if you want to play the normal (Gimmick) difficulty, you&#8217;ll have to pass a little test right off the hop. The way protagonist Yumetaro&#8217;s star attack works is quite familiar also, with it serving as weapon, vehicle, and all-purpose puzzle solver all at once. One new element comes in the form of collectibles, often serving as rewards for completing more difficult sections that aren&#8217;t necessary for progression. These take the form of new customization options, but simply completing the more demanding tasks feels good in and of itself.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328642" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/gimmick2-2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>It&#8217;s not a terribly long game if you just try to bolt through the whole thing, but it&#8217;s going to be a tough one either way. In that respect, it&#8217;s also quite similar to the original. You&#8217;ll die a lot here, often in spectacularly stupid ways, but generous checkpointing keeps the aggravation from boiling over. Well, most of the time. The adorable look and lively music also try to keep the fire from burning too hot, but don&#8217;t for one second underestimate <em>Gimmick! 2</em>. For all of its small concessions, this game understands that part of what made the original game so memorable came from its challenge. Platforming mastery is a must, and you&#8217;ll need to make clever usage of Yumetaro&#8217;s star and the enemies to make it much farther than the first level.</p>
<p><em>Gimmick! 2</em> defies the odds by being a very good follow-up to a decades-old game made by another team entirely. It builds on the original game in some smart ways, but it never feels like it&#8217;s paying too much homage to be its own thing. If you enjoyed the first game, you&#8217;ll be thrilled with this. Platformer fans who aren&#8217;t put off by a high level of challenge will also want to check it out. That said, if you&#8217;re looking for something easy-going, don&#8217;t be fooled by the look of <em>Gimmick! 2</em>. It&#8217;s just as tough of a nut to crack as its predecessor, even with an easier difficulty setting.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Valfaris: Mecha Therion ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328363" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/valfarismechantherion.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p><em>Valfaris: Mecha Therion</em> takes a real risk with its game design, dropping the action-platformer set-up of the original game in favor of shoot &#8217;em up action along the lines of Lords of Thunder. The most surprising thing is that it kind of works. Indeed, the biggest problem is that it&#8217;s sometimes a bit too much for the Switch&#8217;s aging hardware to do proper justice to. I don&#8217;t think anyone can really be blamed here. This is just where we&#8217;re at in the console&#8217;s lifespan now, I think. It&#8217;s not terrible, mind you. If this is the only option you have, you&#8217;ll still get a kick out of <em>Mecha Therion</em>&#8216;s intense action, rocking soundtrack, and creepy visuals.</p>
<p>There&#8217;s a fun juggling act going on in <em>Mecha Therion</em> thanks to how the weapons are set up. You&#8217;ve got your usual gun, but when it runs out of energy it weakens significantly. You&#8217;ve got a melee weapon that you can use to slice bullets and enemies, which recharges your gun energy. Finally, there&#8217;s a rotating third weapon or item. You start off with a lock-on missile attack, but you&#8217;ll have access to others. You can also dash, an offensive and defensive maneuver depending on the situation, and it has its own cooldown. With how many enemies and obstacles the game throws at you, mastering the rhythm of keeping your weapons ready and fending off shots is essential and satisfying.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328643" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/valfarismecha-2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Don&#8217;t come into <em>Valfaris: Mecha Therion</em> expecting a similar kind of game as the first, but you <em>can</em> look forward to a similar <em>vibe</em>. It&#8217;s a heavy metal shoot &#8217;em up with a style all its own, and it thankfully avoids many of the traps that so many games that try something different in this genre end up falling into. You&#8217;ll get better performance on other platforms, and in this genre that&#8217;s important, but this Switch conversion will absolutely do the job should it be your console of choice.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Umamusume: Pretty Derby &#8211; Party Dash ($44.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328435" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/umamusumeprettyderbypartydash.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Saying a licensed game is for the fans feels like a truism. Of course it&#8217;s for the fans. Some do a better job of pleasing that bunch than others, but typically speaking fans of the property used in a licensed game will get more out of it than non-fans. Which boils all of this down to two main questions. First, just how much fan service is baked in? In <em>Umamusume: Pretty Derby &#8211; Party Dash</em>, I can say that there is enough to please those who enjoy the exploits of these horse-girls. Indeed, I think that is the one thing the game does best. The writing is good and fits the property, and there are lots of meta-systems built in that reward you with things for the fans.</p>
<p>The second question pertains to whether or not there is anything here for non-fans. Regrettably, I can&#8217;t be as positive here. You get a small number of half-baked mini-games to play, and while the presentation is solid there just isn&#8217;t much to do here if you aren&#8217;t really into <em>Umamusume</em>. You just play the same four mini-games again and again, watching a story unfold that only fans are going to properly enjoy. One of those mini-games offers so little interaction that I hesitate to even count it. The other three are better, but they just don&#8217;t have the staying power that a party game needs. The best part of all of this is an unlockable mini-game, and even that is a meager meal.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328644" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/umamusumepartydash2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Even for fans of the property, <em>Umamusume: Pretty Derby &#8211; Party Dash</em> feels like it emphasizes the wrong things at great expense. I think a great job has been done here in terms of serving the look, sound, and world of <em>Umamusume</em>, and the wide array of unlockables might be enough to keep those dedicated to the horse-girls interested for a while. But it fades far too fast, and if you&#8217;re lacking that attachment to start with, you&#8217;re probably going get tired of this well before the final furlong.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 3/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Sunsoft is Back! Retro Game Selection ($9.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328645" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/sunsoftisback.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I think when Western game enthusiasts think of Sunsoft, if they think of Sunsoft at all, they probably think of games like<em> Blaster Master, Batman</em>, or <em>Fester&#8217;s Quest</em>. Recently some of the publisher&#8217;s rarer treasures have gotten a new shot at fame, bringing <em>Ufouria, Gimmick!</em>, and<em> Trip World</em> into the light. Even short-term mascot <em>Aero the Acro-Bat</em> and his buddies are getting play again. But there is a whole other side to Sunsoft that is less well-known in the West, and what makes it amusing is that it is what the company is mainly known for in its home country of Japan: charmingly rough-around-the-edges 8-bit games. <em>Sunsoft is Back! Retro Game Selection</em> aims to change that, offering up three such games in one very reasonably priced package.</p>
<p>In this collection, you get the straightforward side-scrolling platforming of <i>Firework Thrower Kantaro&#8217;s 53 Stations of the Tokaido </i>(just rolls off the tongue, don&#8217;t it?), the adventure game <em>Ripple Island</em>, and the slightly hard to categorize action game <em>The Wing of Madoola</em>. All three games are delivered in a wrapper with most of the basic features you would want. That includes save states, rewind, some display options, manual scans, and a little art gallery for each game. Perhaps the most amazing thing is that all three games have been fully localized, all the way down to their title screens. For <em>Ripple Island</em>, that was no small task! This is the first time we&#8217;ve seen these games get an official release in English, and that&#8217;s worth celebrating on its own.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328646" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/sunsoftisback2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>So how are the games? A real mix, friends.<em> 53 Stations</em> is a frustrating game due to how your weapon is awkward to use against the swarms of enemies that appear, but there is a real charm to it thanks to how hard it leans into its theme. <em>Ripple Island</em> is a good adventure game, and I would lightly recommend it to anyone who enjoys that genre. <em>The Wing of Madoola</em> is the most ambitious game of the three, and it sometimes feels like it misses as often as it hits, but it&#8217;s worth putting some time into. None of these will make the list of top NES games by any means, but I wouldn&#8217;t call any of them bad either. Vexing, perhaps.</p>
<p>Sunsoft fans and those who like digging into the margins of console libraries will get a real kick out of<em> Sunsoft is Back! Retro Game Selection</em>&#8216;s offerings. Each game has been handled with care, and it&#8217;s great to see some games that missed their shot at localization back in the day finally get a chance. Hopefully this is just the first of a series of such collections, but even if this is all we get, it&#8217;s nice to see another side of this once-mighty publisher&#8217;s library of classics.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Select New Releases</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>Cyborg Force ($9.95)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328647" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/cyborgforce.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>If you like run, but also gun while running, you might want to give <em>Cyborg Force</em> a look. It&#8217;s a very tough action game in the vein of things like<em> Metal Slug</em> and <em>Contra</em>, and you can play alone or bring a buddy along for some local multiplayer fun. This has been out for at least a few months on a number of other platforms, including the NEOGEO, so you can probably find some feedback out there if you&#8217;re in need of it. I think fans of this genre will be into it, at the very least.</p>
<h3><strong>Billy&#8217;s Game Show ($7.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328648" height="591" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/billysgameshow.jpg" width="1050" /></p>
<p>I know, this looks like a <em>Five Nights</em> rip-off at a glance. Thematically, it kind of is. But this is actually more one of those games where you&#8217;re moving around trying to find stuff while a weird creep stalks you. You have to hide or run away when he shows up, because you have no way to stop him. You&#8217;ll also need to watch out for traps and keep the three generators running, because you do not want the power to go out. Not my kind of thing, but it might be yours.</p>
<h3><strong>Mining Mechs ($4.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328649" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/miningmechs.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>A very descriptive title that doesn&#8217;t leave me with much to add. Use mechs to do some mining. Collect ores and treasures, sell them, buy upgrades or better mechs that allow you to make even further progress underground. The deeper you go, the more dangerous it gets. As you reach certain profit levels, the story will progress. Not the fanciest of dining, but for a fiver I imagine you&#8217;ll get a fair amount of enjoyment from it.</p>
