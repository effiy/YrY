<h2 id="add-cards-to-express-mode-to-use-them-even-when-your-phone-is-dead">Add cards to Express Mode to use them even when your phone is dead </h2><p>Express Mode in Apple Wallet allows you to use transit cards (and payment cards specifically for transit), keys, student IDs, and other compatible passes without waking your device or authenticating with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode. These passes are also available on a battery reserve, so you can still use them even if your iPhone is dead. This means you can still get home on public transit or unlock your door without needing to charge your device first. Select the card, pass, or key in Apple Wallet, tap the <strong>three dots &gt; Card Details &gt; Express Transit Settings</strong> or <strong>Express Mode</strong>. </p><p>It's worth noting that Express Mode would allow anyone with physical access to your device to use your passes since authentication isn't required. You may want to keep keys secured with biometrics and reserve Express Mode for transit cards, which you're more likely to need to tap and go. </p><h2 id="generate-a-virtual-card-number-to-use-apple-cash-where-apple-pay-isnt-available">Generate a virtual card number to use Apple Cash where Apple Pay isn't available</h2><p>Apple Pay is increasingly accepted as a payment option online, in apps, and at brick-and-mortar merchants, but there are still plenty of places that won't accept a direct Apple Pay transaction. If you're shopping online or making a payment over the phone, you can generate a virtual card number for Apple Cash, which allows you to spend those funds directly and also keeps your real card information hidden. When you select your Apple Cash card in Wallet, tap the card icon at the top of the screen and authenticate to get the number. The three-digit security code will change each time you use AutoFill or access your card in Wallet. </p><h2 id="use-shortcuts-to-automatically-select-a-card-based-on-location">Use Shortcuts to automatically select a card based on location </h2><p>If you use different payment cards for different transaction types&mdash;one card offers better rewards on groceries while another is good for gas, for example&mdash;Apple Wallet can automatically suggest the correct one based on your location so you don't have to remember which is which or manually select a card each time you check out. (This is also handy for pulling up digital loyalty cards or membership passes as soon as you walk in the door.) This relies on a custom automation through Shortcuts. In the <strong>Library</strong> tab, tap the "+" button, type "Card" into the search bar, and select <strong>Open Card</strong>. Then go to <strong>Automation</strong>, tap "+," select <strong>Arrive</strong> and add the <strong>Location</strong>. You can select when to run the automation, then hit <strong>Next</strong>. For the Action, select <strong>Open Card in Wallet</strong>, select the pass or card, and confirm. </p><h2 id="use-tap-to-cash-to-split-bills-instantly-without-needing-contact-information">Use Tap to Cash to split bills instantly without needing contact information</h2><p>Many methods of peer-to-peer payment require you to obtain <em>some</em> type of contact information&mdash;such as a username, email, or phone number&mdash;from the person you're sending money to or receiving money from. If they're your friend or you pay them often using a service like Venmo or Cash App, this probably isn't a hassle. But you could use Apple Wallet's built-in Tap to Cash, which transfers money instantly between two iPhones or Apple Watches without sharing private contact information. You simply need to have Apple Cash set up in advance. When you're ready to send or receive funds, open Wallet, tap your Apple Cash card, and select <strong>Send or Request &gt; Tap to Cash</strong>. Enter the amount, tap <strong>Next</strong>, double-click your iPhone side button to confirm, and hold your device up to the other person's. </p><h2 id="use-wallet-to-share-digital-keys-with-custom-permissions">Use Wallet to share digital keys with custom permissions </h2><p>Apple Wallet can securely store a few different types of digital keys: home, apartment/condo, hotel room, car, and scooter. Your device will automatically pull up the correct key when you're in range of the lock, and you can also enable Express Mode so you don't need to authenticate with Face ID or your passcode. Car and scooter keys can then be shared, so other drivers can lock, unlock, and start your vehicle without needing a physical key. You can also set custom permissions, such as restricting the recipient's ability to reshare and, depending on the car manufacturer, setting limits on things like speed and radio volume. </p><p>When you select your car key in Wallet and tap <strong>Share</strong>, set the available permissions, add an optional activation code, and choose your recipient. You can share via Messages, Mail, WhatsApp, and AirDrop, to name a few. </p><h2 id="enable-apple-pay-and-mail-order-tracking-to-see-all-of-your-shipments-in-wallet">Enable Apple Pay and Mail order tracking to see all of your shipments in Wallet</h2><p>In addition to showing you recent transactions on each of your cards, Wallet serves as a hub for tracking orders on purchases made with Apple Pay. You just have to enable this in <strong>Settings &gt; Wallet &amp; Apple Pay &gt; Order Tracking</strong>. (Also in beta is an Apple Intelligence and Siri feature that can update orders in Wallet with information pulled from Mail&mdash;this is a separate toggle in <strong>Settings</strong>.) Back in Wallet, tap the <strong>three dots &gt; Orders</strong>. </p><h2 id="add-your-drivers-license-to-wallet-to-verify-your-identity-with-your-iphone">Add your driver&rsquo;s license to Wallet to verify your identity with your iPhone </h2><p>Apple has continued to expand its digital ID options within Wallet so you can add your driver's license and/or state ID <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/you-can-now-add-your-passport-to-your-iphone" target="_blank">as well as your passport</a> to the app and use your iPhone&mdash;instead of the physical documents&mdash;to verify your identity. Practically speaking, this is most useful when flying, as digital IDs are accepted at TSA checkpoints. (It's worth noting that <a href="https://lifehacker.com/you-can-use-your-iphone-as-an-id-in-these-states" target="_blank">only a small subset of states</a> have compatibility with Apple Wallet.) You may also be able to use your ID from Wallet at some entertainment venues, as well for in-app verifications. Tap the "+" sign in Wallet, select <strong>Driver's License and ID Cards</strong>, choose the ID you want to add, and follow the prompts. </p><h2 id="enable-payments-on-mac-to-authenticate-apple-pay-from-your-iphone">Enable payments on Mac to authenticate Apple Pay from your iPhone</h2><p>While Apple Pay is relatively seamless for online purchases if you're using a Mac and Safari, there are other cases where you can't authenticate payment simply by using Touch ID&mdash;for example, you have your laptop docked and closed or you're using an older device without built-in biometrics. On a non-Safari browser, you also have to scan a generated code with your iPhone to complete an Apple Pay purchase. While you can't get around the latter, you can enable Allow Payments on Mac for the former, which then lets you authenticate from your iPhone or Apple Watch if it's not possible to do on desktop. Go to <strong>Settings &gt; Wallet &amp; Apple Pay &gt; Allow Payments on Mac</strong>. </p><h2 id="convert-physical-cards-into-apple-wallet-passes">Convert physical cards into Apple Wallet passes</h2><p>While many types of passes&mdash;library cards, event tickets, membership passes, etc.&mdash;already integrate seamlessly with Apple Wallet, some businesses may still hand out physical cards. If you want to digitize, you can use a third-party app like <a href="https://apps.apple.com/us/app/makepass-ai-wallet-editor/id1450989464" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">MakePass</a> or <a href="https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pass2u-wallet-add-store-card/id1142473931" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Pass2U Wallet</a> to scan and add unsupported cards to Apple Wallet. iOS 27 is coming out with a <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/20-hidden-features-apple-didnt-announce-at-wwdc-2026" target="_blank">Create a Pass feature</a> that will do this natively via Apple Intelligence. </p><h2 id="audit-your-connected-merchants-to-remove-unused-subscriptions">Audit your connected merchants to remove unused subscriptions </h2><p>If you use Apple Pay for recurring charges, such as subscriptions and monthly bills, you can view a list of merchants right in Wallet. Tap the three dots in the upper right corner and select <strong>Preauthorized Payments</strong>. You can also revoke payment tokens, though Apple notes that this won't cancel your subscription and associated payment&mdash;rather, it'll simply communicate to the merchant not to charge the Apple Pay method again. To actually cancel or change your payment method, you'll have to go directly to the merchant. </p>
