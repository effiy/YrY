<p>Gucci Morty isn't the only inexplicable viral star this week. There's also Chanty Beluga. The videos are generally about five seconds long and get millions of views. <p>There is no more eagerly awaited video game than Rockstar's <em>Grand Theft Auto VI</em>. Scheduled for release on November 19, 2026, <em>Grand Theft Auto VI is </em>is a game just about every gamer wants to play&mdash;but a few people are actually doing it. Mysterious hacker group Cyberleek has somehow gotten hold of a playable build of the game and has uploaded at least 10 gameplay videos to a Telegram Channel and a website. From there, they quickly spread everywhere, giving everyone an illicit peak of what to expect when this hits the market. But this is no viral marketing gimmick. Rockstar Games and its parent company Take-Two Interactive are furiously issuing subpoenas to Discord, Microsoft, and hosting providers to pull IP addresses and accounts linked to Cyberleek, nuking the videos when they appear. Right now, Cyberleek's site and Telegram are down, but I wouldn't be surprised if that does not stop them from posting more videos in the coming day. Cyberleek's drops are often couched in a message decrying digital-only game releases, but the real motivation isn't altruism, it's a crypto-currency scheme. <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/GTA6unmoderated/comments/1vwpsti/some_guy_dropped_10k_on_strip_club_2_leak/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Cyberleek has published a poll on its website</a> where people can vote on what to show next  through purchasing "CyberLeek" memecoin. As yet, there's no information on how, exactly, this group got its hands on <em>GTA VI</em> source code, leading to theories that a disgruntled employee made off with the code, or that some kind of phishing or other attack was wildly successful. The leaks are not expected to delay or alter "<a href="https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/9k2kaa1o3297k9/grand-theft-auto-vi-an-extended-look" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Grand Theft Auto 6: An Extended Look</a>," a special streaming on Netflix beginning on August 27, nor should it affect the release date. </p><h2 id="what-is-a-misery-girl">What is a Misery Girl?</h2><p>"<a href="https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/misery-gir" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Misery girl</a>" is slang for a young women with piercings, a penchant for the darkly dramatic, and highly stylized makeup that makes them appear to have a skinny, pointy nose and huge eyes and lips. <p>Back in 2017, unknown YouTuber Jimmy "MrBeast" Donaldson went viral for the first time by sitting in his room <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xWcldHxHFpo" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">counting 100,000.</a> That no-budget webcam stunt was enough to get millions of views on YouTube back then. This week's viral video from MrBeast has nearly 45 million views after only three days online, and it proves that you <em>really</em> have to up the production if you expect to go viral with a stunt video in 2026. <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qtl8lJwbd4g" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Escape 100 Cops, Win $500,000</a> is among the most elaborate video ever shot for YouTube.  MrBeast hired 100 off-duty Indiana State Police troopers to try to catch him and four of his friends as they hid out in a simulated town rigged with tunnels, explosives, secret rooms and more. Anyone who avoids capture for 12 hours gets a $100,000. The police department gets $500,000 if everyone is apprehended. The video required a huge production crew, legal department for the permits, explosive experts, post production department, and more. Judging from what MrBeast has said in the past about his more elaborate videos, the budget was comfortably in the multimillion dollar range. </p>
