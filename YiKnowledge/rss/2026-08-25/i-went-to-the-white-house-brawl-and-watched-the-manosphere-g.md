<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I found it easy to resist the early dispatches from &#8220;UFC Freedom 250,&#8221; the Ultimate Fighting Championship event held in June on the White House South Lawn. But Jon Ronson is the sort of writer I can&#8217;t help following into battle. Fresh off a new book about masculinity, Ronson brings a delightfully skewed perspective to the fights and the days that surrounded them. The result is a portrait of a planned media spectacle that can&#8217;t quite make sense of itself. The best battle here may be Ronson&#8217;s tussle with his own photographer, 79-year-old Bruce Gilden, who is a bit of a hugger.</p>
<span class="__iawmlf-post-loop-links" hidden="hidden"></span>


<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“I’m pretty certain you hugged Stephen Miller first,” I say. “I don’t see Stephen Miller as initiating a hug.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“You may be right,” he says. “I don’t remember. Look, I do things maybe other people aren’t comfortable doing, but I felt comfortable and I trust my instincts.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">He acknowledges that “nobody is immune to misreading a situation” and that because of him we&nbsp;<em>were</em>&nbsp;all forced back indoors, but he blames Miller’s entourage for not checking with their boss on whether he minded being hugged.</p>
</blockquote>
