<h2 id="what-we-know-about-the-new-garmin-fenix-9-and-fenix-9-pro">What we know about the new Garmin Fenix 9 and Fenix 9 Pro</h2><p>Marketed as Garmin's "most advanced training, mapping, and connectivity experience yet," here are the most notable features we learned from today's livestream about the Fenix 9 and 9 Pro. Starting with design and style:</p><ul><li><p>A brighter AMOLED display reaching up to 3,000 nits (for context, the Fenix 8 AMOLED is 1,000 nits).</p></li><li><p>A new 1.5-inch display option on the 51 mm Pro model (larger than Fenix 8 Pro's 1.4 inches). </p></li><li><p>The Fenix 9 Pro is the first Fenix smartwatch to feature a titanium watch case.</p></li><li><p>New ComfortFit accessory bands with a stretchy fabric design. </p></li></ul><p>Here are the software updates that caught my eye:</p><ul><li><p>Garmin Epic (not to be confused with its <a href="https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/894067/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Epix</a> line) is a <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Garmin/comments/1vxwyim/new_garmin_feature_epics_will_anyone_be_using_this/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">new feature</a> in Garmin Connect that lets you group multiple activities and training blocks into a single, shareable story. </p></li><li><p>New stamina tracking features&mdash;including stamina zones and stamina curve&mdash;show athletes how their endurance changes over time. </p></li><li><p>Additional indoor rowing insights and expanded rucking features (a nod to the growing rucking and hybrid-training crowd).</p></li><li><p>With 64 GB of memory, you can download twice as many maps as the Fenix 8, and store up to 7,000 songs. </p></li><li><p>Built-in routable maps that Garmin claims are "smoother and more intuitive than ever before." With 50% more RAM than the Fenix 8, map panning will allegedly get up to 30% faster.</p></li><li><p>For the first time, connectivity with <a href="https://www.garmin.com/en-US/c/outdoor-recreation/satellite-communicators/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">inReach</a> (Garmin's satellite communicators) is available on all Fenix 9 Pro size options.</p></li><li><p>The Fenix 9 Pro also offers the option for solar-charging displays.</p></li></ul><p>Notably, the optical heart rate sensor hasn't changed&mdash;it's still the Elevate Gen 5 sensor from the last generation, so anyone hoping for a sensor bump will have to wait for a future release.</p><h2 id="what-this-means-for-garmin-fans">What this means for Garmin fans</h2><p>As is no surprise these days, the steep pricing here suggests Garmin is positioning this as an evolutionary release for its most dedicated users, rather than a reasonable way for casual Fenix 8 owners to immediately upgrade.</p><p>The 16% thinner titanium case could be a big draw for existing Fenix fans. That said, as with any product launch, the marketing demos and spec sheets only tell part of the story. Things like real-world battery life, fit and comfort, and software stability tend to reveal themselves over weeks of actual use, so stay tuned for my full review after I get the chance to put this watch through its paces.</p><p>There's also a silver lining here: The Fenix 9's arrival has already <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/garmin-fenix-8-smartwatch-sale-august-2026" target="_blank">pushed Fenix 8 prices down</a> from $1,099.99 to <a href="https://zdcs.link/z7GRAV?pageview_type=RSS&amp;template=content&amp;module=content_body&amp;element=offer&amp;item=text-link&amp;element_label=%24849.99&amp;object_type=content&amp;short_url=z7GRAV&amp;u=https%3A%2F%2Flifehacker.com%2Ffeed%2Frss" rel="sponsored" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">$849.99</a>. If you don't need titanium or the latest mapping engine, last year's model could be today's savvy value buy.</p>
