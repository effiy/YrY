<p>There is a version of Halloween tourism that involves a man in a rubber mask jumping out from behind a plywood wall. It has its pleasures. It is not this.</p>
<p>The places below are real. The things that happened in them are documented — in court records, in coroners' reports, in parish registers, in the diaries of people who were there. Some have ghost stories attached. The ghost stories are almost always less frightening than what actually took place, which is the point.</p>
<p>This is not an itinerary. These places are scattered across three continents and you are not meant to visit them all. Pick the kind of dread you are in the mood for, and go.</p>
<p>One exception, and it is worth leading with, because this year there is somewhere you can only go now.</p>
<h2>Salem and Monroe: the one with a deadline</h2>
<p>For most of fifty years, the artifacts Ed and Lorraine Warren collected sat in the basement of their house in Monroe, Connecticut. Objects from the Amityville case. The mirror. The dolls. Things they believed were actively dangerous and locked away accordingly. <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-warrens-occult-museum-monroe-connecticut"><strong>The Warrens' Occult Museum</strong></a> closed to the public in 2019, the year Lorraine died, over a zoning dispute — and for six years the most famous collection of haunted objects in America was simply unavailable.</p>
<p>That changed twice, quickly.</p>
<p>In 2025 the comedian Matt Rife and the YouTuber Elton Castee bought the house and took a five-year guardianship of the collection. The Monroe house now takes private overnight bookings — the entire property, eight guests maximum, flat rate regardless of headcount, with a paranormal investigation kit and supervised access to the museum itself. Guests sign two waivers on arrival: one for physical liability, one for spiritual. The second lists, among the outcomes the operators decline responsibility for, possession, levitation, and astral projection. No tours, no drop-ins, and a security system that treats the driveway as seriously as the museum.</p>
<figure class=" contains-caption "><img alt="article-image" class="article-image with-structured-caption " src="https://assets.atlasobscura.com/article_images/108852/image.jpg" width="auto" /></figure>
<p>In late August, Annabelle, the Shadow Doll, the Satanic Idol, the Conjuring Mirror and the <em>Devil Made Me Do It</em> dinosaur leave Monroe for Salem.</p>
<p>The <strong>Haunted Warren Museum</strong> opens August 27 at 259 Essex Street — more than a thousand artifacts across thirteen exhibits, a self-guided flashlight tour of roughly an hour, in a four-thousand-square-foot space in the middle of the most concentrated Halloween tourism market in the United States. The Warrens lectured in Salem for years and made artwork about it.</p>
<p>And it is temporary. The operators have said there is no guarantee it stays in Salem past December 23 while they develop a permanent site elsewhere. Salem in October, with Annabelle in it, is a thing that has never happened before and may not happen again.</p>
<p>If you want the Monroe house instead, the overnight bookings are gone through the end of the year; the next availability is deep winter, which is arguably the better season for it anyway. Ed and Lorraine are buried less than a mile away.</p>
<h2 class="article-subheading-pre-rd">Documented horror: Taunton State Hospital and Outagamie County</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/taunton-state-hospital"><strong>Taunton State Hospital</strong></a> in Massachusetts is a psychiatric institution whose reputation for hauntings is entirely downstream of a documented record of how patients were treated there. The building does not need a ghost.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/outagamie-county-asylum-cemetery"><strong>Outagamie County Asylum Cemetery</strong></a> in Appleton, Wisconsin holds the bodies of at least 133 former patients. Most were buried without names.</p>
<h2 class="article-subheading-pre-rd">Folklore taken seriously: the Greenbrier Ghost, the Three Brujas, the Satanic Mill</h2>
<p>Some places are frightening because of what people once believed, and what those beliefs made them do.</p>
<p>In West Virginia, the <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/greenbrier-ghost-of-sam-black-church"><strong>Greenbrier Ghost</strong></a> marker commemorates Zona Heaster Shue, whose mother testified in open court that her murdered daughter's ghost had appeared to her and named the killer. The testimony was admitted. The husband was convicted.</p>
<p>In Taos, New Mexico, <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/las-tres-brujas-kit-carson-cemetery-taos-new-mexico"><strong>The Three Brujas</strong></a> are three unmarked concrete graves said to hold witches — deliberately anonymous, deliberately sealed.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/selee-satanic-mill-marker"><strong>Selee Satanic Mill Marker</strong></a> in Easton, Massachusetts is a roadside sign identifying the site of an eighteenth-century sawmill whose owner was locally believed to employ Satanic imps. Someone thought this worth commemorating in metal.</p>
<p>And <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/springdale-cemetery-illinois"><strong>Springdale Cemetery</strong></a> in Peoria contains the so-called Witch's Circle, a formation that has generated more than a century of local explanation.</p>
<h2>Graveyards: Highgate, Georgiana, and the Spinning Headstone</h2>
<p>The cemetery is Halloween's most reliable destination and also its most misunderstood. The good ones are not scary. They are strange.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/highgate-cemetery"><strong>Highgate Cemetery</strong></a> in London opened in 1839 as one of the city's Magnificent Seven, fell into overgrown disrepair after the war, and then in the 1970s became the site of something genuinely extraordinary: two rival occultists publicly feuding over whether a vampire was living in it, drawing crowds of amateur hunters into the grounds at night. Tombs were broken open. Corpses were staked. One of the men was jailed in 1974 for interfering with human remains. Karl Marx and Douglas Adams are buried there, which tells you something about the range.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/georgiana-cemetery"><strong>Georgiana Cemetery</strong></a> on Merritt Island, Florida holds Ethel Allen, murdered at nineteen in the early 1900s in a case never solved. Her headstone is handmade, unlike every other marker there. Someone still decorates her grave, and nobody knows who.</p>
<figure class=" contains-caption "><img alt="article-image" class="article-image with-structured-caption " src="https://assets.atlasobscura.com/article_images/108856/image.jpg" width="auto" /></figure>
<p>The <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/jackson-grave-houses"><strong>Jackson Grave Houses</strong></a> in North Carolina are small structures built over graves — a tradition intended to shelter the spirit rather than contain it.</p>
<p>In Philadelphia, the <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-grave-of-americas-unknown-child-joseph-augustus-zarelli"><strong>grave of Joseph Augustus Zarelli</strong></a> marks a boy who went unidentified for sixty-five years. He has a name now.</p>
<p>And in Hillside, Illinois, <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-spinning-headstone"><strong>the Spinning Headstone</strong></a> belongs to an ordinary husband and wife in a cemetery otherwise full of holy men and mobsters.</p>
<p>If you want more of this: the Atlas holds <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/united-states/graveyards"><strong>189 unusual graveyards in the United States</strong></a>, <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/massachusetts/catacombs-crypts-cemeteries"><strong>seventeen catacombs, crypts and cemeteries in Massachusetts alone</strong></a>, and a list of <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/lists/abandoned-cemeteries"><strong>21 lost and lonely cemeteries</strong></a> — including a Serbian village grave belonging to a man whose 1725 death produced one of the first written accounts of vampirism in Europe. The full <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/categories/graveyards"><strong>graveyards</strong></a> and <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/categories/cemeteries"><strong>cemeteries</strong></a> categories go deeper still.</p>
<h2>Sleep in it: the Lizzie Borden House, Fall River</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/lizzie-borden-bed-and-breakfast-and-museum"><strong>Lizzie Borden Bed and Breakfast</strong></a> in Fall River, Massachusetts is the actual house where the actual murders took place in 1892. You can stay in it. People do — including one Atlas Obscura community member who booked it as a romantic weekend away and later married the man she brought.</p>

<h2 class="article-subheading-pre-rd">The object: Robert the Doll, Key West</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/robert-doll"><strong>Robert the Doll</strong></a> has been causing trouble in Key West for over a century and is the origin point of an entire subgenre. Visitors who photograph him without permission write letters of apology afterward. The museum keeps them.</p>

<h2 class="article-subheading-pre-rd">The debunking: Helltown, Ohio and the Bolton Strid</h2>
<p>The most Atlas Obscura thing you can do on Halloween is discover that the story is wrong and the truth is better.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/helltown-ohio"><strong>Helltown, Ohio</strong></a> is the case study. The internet will tell you about a satanic church, a crybaby bridge, an abandoned school bus full of murdered children, and a mutant python. What actually happened: the village of Boston, Ohio was bought out by the federal government for a national park, the residents were moved, and the houses were marked and eventually torn down in 2016. A town emptied by paperwork is stranger than a town emptied by demons. It's permanently closed to visitors now; the entry survives as a record.</p>
<p>And the <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/bolton-strid"><strong>Bolton Strid</strong></a> in Yorkshire is the opposite case — a place whose reputation is, if anything, understated. It looks like a stream you could step across. It is the River Wharfe turned on its side, running vertically down a shaft it has carved through rock, with voids beneath the banks. The local claim is that nobody who has entered has come out. There are no hard numbers, which is its own kind of answer. There's <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/podcast-bolton-strid"><strong>a podcast episode</strong></a> if you want to be properly unsettled before you go.</p>
<figure class=" contains-caption "><img alt="article-image" class="article-image with-structured-caption " src="https://assets.atlasobscura.com/article_images/108860/image.jpg" width="auto" /></figure>
<p>For a structured route rather than a menu, the <a href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/itineraries/ghosts-from-mississippi"><strong>Ghosts From Mississippi itinerary</strong></a> runs from the grave of a witch who hexed a town, through a candlelit tour of the McRaven House in Vicksburg, to a cemetery statue in Natchez that some say moves at night.</p>
<h2 class="article-subheading-pre-rd">How to do dark tourism well</h2>
<p>Every place here is somewhere people lived, and in most cases somewhere people died. The line between dark tourism and rubbernecking is almost entirely a matter of what you bring to it.</p>
<p>Read the history before you go. Don't trespass — several of these sites are closed, private, or both, and the ones that are open have rules for reasons. And take the folklore seriously <em>as folklore</em>. The belief that put a ghost's testimony into a West Virginia murder trial was a real belief, held by real people, in response to real grief. That is more interesting than a haunting. It is also considerably more frightening.</p>
