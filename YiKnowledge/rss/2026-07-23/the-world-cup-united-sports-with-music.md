	<p>No matter how you look at it, the 2026 World Cup was a resounding success. On the field, the world saw stars like <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DaqsjFYz7t3/?hl=en">Erling Haaland</a>, Jude Bellingham, and Lamine Yamal thrive in the spotlight. Off the field, the game was the cultural event of the year. Dua Lipa was one of many artists who made a splash by <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DatNxS5vZCr/?hl=en">attending the games</a>, while <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DbBFcoSDh5f/?hl=en&#038;img_index=1">Rosalía celebrated her country&#8217;s victory</a> in style. And then there was the first-ever halftime show for the final, where <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/Da_bGDyDX8r/?hl=en&#038;img_index=1">Madonna, Coldplay, Burna Boy, and Justin Bieber performed in a massive spectacle</a> that featured everyone from Ted Lasso to The Muppets.</p>
<p>At UPROXX, we surrounded the games with our <a href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL4B018AE6C611F0C6">Sounds Of The Cup playlist</a>, featuring many of the artists tied to the halftime show — all of whom saw a real bump in their music around the games. The results were massive: 28M total video views, 77.2M total minutes watched, and 125.8K total engagements.</p>
<p>Every tentpole moment has a soundtrack — and the numbers prove just how tightly sport and culture are woven together with the music that surrounds them.</p>
