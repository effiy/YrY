<p>The <a href="https://zdcs.link/QL2MYZ?pageview_type=RSS&amp;template=content&amp;module=content_body&amp;element=offer&amp;item=text-link&amp;element_label=Nothing%20Phone%20%284a%29%20Pro%E2%80%99s&amp;object_type=content&amp;short_url=QL2MYZ&amp;u=https%3A%2F%2Flifehacker.com%2Ffeed%2Frss" rel="sponsored" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">Nothing Phone (4a) Pro&rsquo;s</a> 6.83-inch AMOLED display is one of the highlights, with a 1,260-by-2,800 resolution and a 144Hz refresh rate that makes scrolling and animations feel especially smooth. Nothing also claims up to 5,000 nits of peak brightness, so the screen should hold up well outdoors. Around back, the updated Glyph Matrix uses micro-LEDs for visual alerts and other information, while the aluminum unibody gives the phone a more premium feel than the plastic-heavy builds you typically still see in this price range. The tradeoff is that the unibody design hurts repairability, and there is no wireless charging, notes this <a href="https://www.pcmag.com/reviews/nothing-phone-4a-pro" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window"><u>PCMag review</u></a>.</p><p>For everyday performance, the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 and 8GB of RAM of the <a href="https://zdcs.link/QL2MYZ?pageview_type=RSS&amp;template=content&amp;module=content_body&amp;element=offer&amp;item=text-link&amp;element_label=%284a%29%20Pro&amp;object_type=content&amp;short_url=QL2MYZ&amp;u=https%3A%2F%2Flifehacker.com%2Ffeed%2Frss" rel="sponsored" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">(4a) Pro</a> should comfortably handle messaging, streaming, navigation, social apps, and regular multitasking. I would not choose it specifically for demanding mobile games, but that is unlikely to be a problem for most people shopping in this price range. As for its camera setup, it gives you a 50MP main camera, another 50MP camera, an 8MP ultrawide, and a 32MP front camera, so there is enough flexibility for travel, family photos, and everyday shooting without making this a camera-first phone. Battery life is also one of its stronger points, with PCMag&rsquo;s testing showing more than 15 hours of use, which should make getting through a normal day fairly easy. You also get dual-SIM support with eSIM, an in-display fingerprint sensor, and IP65 resistance against dust and water.</p><hr /><div class=" relative flex justify-center py-16 md:left-1/2 md:w-[780px] md:max-w-max md:-translate-x-1/2">
