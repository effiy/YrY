<p>In our tour through retro games available to buy on Switch, we&#8217;ve worked our way through the NES, Game Boy, and SEGA Genesis selections among other more recent console libraries. This time we&#8217;re taking a look at the games that originated with Nintendo&#8217;s 16-bit console, the Super NES. While you can find plenty of great games from the console in the Nintendo Switch Online app, we&#8217;re instead looking at those games that dare to exist in the wilds of the Switch eShop. We&#8217;ve got ten of our favorites here, plus five more games that we&#8217;d like to see. No particular order, of course. Oh, and to cut off potential comments at the pass: <em>Mega Man X</em> isn&#8217;t here because I feel the input lag in the <em>Legacy Collection</em> hurts it too badly. Stone-cold classic, just not a great play experience on Switch. On with the show!</p>
<h3><strong>Sparkster &#8211; Rocket Knight Adventures: Re-Sparked ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-324915" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/rocketknight2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>A very rare case of a Konami franchise born on a SEGA platform,<em> Rocket Knight Adventures</em> was a game Super NES owners could easily envy. While that game never came to Nintendo&#8217;s console, a version of the sequel did. <em>Sparkster</em> is the name of two distinct games, and the Super NES offering bearing that title is probably the better of the two. Not quite up to the original game, but plenty of fun.</p>
<h3><strong>Rock &#8216;n Roll Racing &#8211; Blizzard Arcade Collection ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-277932" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/blizzardarcadecollection3.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Before Blizzard became a household name for joking about phone ownership, it was making some very good console games. Also a pretty iffy <em>Justice League</em> fighting game, but we&#8217;re not here to talk about that. Instead, we&#8217;re talking about the highly enjoyable isometric racer that fused exciting gameplay with a fantastic licensed soundtrack. This game alone is worth the price of admission for this collection, but you get a handful of other cool Super NES games with it.</p>
<h3><strong>Top Racer &#8211; Top Racer Collection ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-320061" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/topracercollection.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>More popularly known under the title <em>Top Gear</em>, <em>Top Racer</em> is one of the better choices in the racing genre on the Super NES. It rides the line between arcade and sim very well, and the soundtrack is absolutely outstanding. You get all three of the games in this set, and I&#8217;d argue the second game goes toe-to-toe with the first. The third game&#8230; well, how about those first two games, eh?</p>
<h3><strong>Secret of Mana &#8211; Collection of Mana ($39.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328461" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/secretofmana.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Square Enix seems to favor remakes with its Switch takes on its Super NES classics, and while those aren&#8217;t technically against my rules for these lists I think I&#8217;ll go with a purer choice. <em>Secret of Mana</em> can be found in the excellent <em>Collection of Mana</em> alongside the Game Boy original and the Super NES third game in the series. The emulation was handled by M2, so it&#8217;s working just as it did back in the day, bugs and all. <em>Secret of Mana</em> is a thoroughly enjoyable action-RPG, despite its many rough edges. You get to save Christmas at the behest of Rudolph! What more do you need? A fluffy dragon you can ride in a Mode 7 overworld map? Well, you get that too!</p>
<h3><strong>TMNT IV: Turtles in Time &#8211; TMNT: The Cowabunga Collection ($39.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-296681" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/tmntcowabungacollection.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>The 16-bit generation was the era of beat &#8217;em ups, and<em> Turtles in Time</em> jostles with <em>Streets of Rage 2</em> for the title of king among the many challengers. It looks almost as good as the arcade game, plays even better, and leans into its time-traveling gimmick in a lot of fun ways. One of the best games on the Super NES full-stop, and I&#8217;m glad we&#8217;re able to buy it on modern consoles for the time being.</p>
<h3><strong>Contra III: The Alien Wars &#8211; Contra Anniversary Collection ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-254249" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/contraanniversarycollection6.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Lots of Konami here again. It occupies a lot of spots in these lists, but it has earned it. Here&#8217;s the third <em>Contra</em> game, and the first to have no roots in the arcades whatsoever. It aims for a more futuristic setting and feel, and in true Konami style it pushes the hardware in a variety of fun ways. Sure, those Mode 7 overhead levels don&#8217;t play quite as well as you might like them to, but there&#8217;s no denying the style that runs through this entire game.</p>
<h3><strong>Super Castlevania IV &#8211; Castlevania Anniversary Collection ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-251835" height="600" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/castlevaniaanniversarysc4.jpg" width="782" /></p>
<p>For some people, this is the best action-based installment in the <em>Castlevania</em> series. I&#8217;m not sure I&#8217;m fully on-board with that, but this is one heck of a great game. The visuals, sound effects, and music create an incredible vibe, and Simon&#8217;s overpowered whip skills make you feel like a real pro. The Super NES would see a lot of great action games, but few of them are as confidently showy as this one. A highly memorable game, and if you&#8217;re looking for a somewhat fairer entry point into the series this is one of the better choices.</p>
<h3><strong>Zombies Ate My Neighbors &#8211; Zombies Ate My Neighbors &amp; Ghoul Patrol ($14.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-282012" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/zombiesatemyneighborsandghoulpatrol.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Okay, Konami published this back in the day, but it&#8217;s really a Lucasarts title. A really unique one, at that. A stage-based top-down action game with tons of wacky enemies, locations, and tools you can find and use, <em>Zombies Ate My Neighbors</em> offered an experience you couldn&#8217;t find anywhere else on the console. Play alone or pull in a friend, especially if you want to make an actual go at beating this tough game.</p>
<h3><strong>Aladdin &#8211; Disney Classic Games Collection ($29.49)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328460" height="591" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/disneyclassicaladdin.jpg" width="1050" /></p>
<p>The debate rages on about which 16-bit console version of <em>Aladdin</em> is better, but the nice thing about the <em>Disney Classic Games Collection</em> is that it doesn&#8217;t force you to choose. Both are here, have fun. Since this is a Super NES list, we&#8217;re talking about that version of the game. Directed by Shinji Mikami, who later got up to some zombie nonsense, this version of <em>Aladdin</em> can&#8217;t match the authentic presentation of the Genesis game, but makes up for it with some excellent Capcom platforming action. A bit of a breezy affair, but that&#8217;s not so bad sometimes.</p>
<h3><strong>Puzzle Bobble/Bust-A-Move (16-Bit Console Version) ($7.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-306624" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/puzzlebobble16bit2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Let&#8217;s squeeze a puzzle game in here to finish things out. Call it Bust-A-Move, call it Puzzle Bobble, either way you&#8217;re in for a good time. Shoot the bubbles, make the matches, and try to clear the field before you get squashed. A charming presentation and some devilish stage designs make this a hard one to put down. Sure, the sequels have a lot more to offer, but you can have plenty of good times with this version all on its own.</p>
<h3><strong>And&#8230; 5 Super NES Games We&#8217;d Like to See on Switch</strong></h3>
<p><strong>Chrono Trigger</strong></p>
<p>I mean, of course. One of the best games on the console, and cited by many as the finest game in the genre. It&#8217;s a bit silly that it isn&#8217;t on Switch, even if there are probably good reasons for it. Well, this is a wishlist. I don&#8217;t care about reasons! I want results! Bring me pictures of Chrono! Er, bring me <em>Chrono Trigger</em> on Switch!</p>
<p><strong>SimCity</strong></p>
<p>The theme of these picks is going to be licensing issues from here on out, though I have to believe <em>SimCity</em> is somewhat possible. All we need is Electronic Arts and Nintendo to come together to make it happen, and it&#8217;s not like it hasn&#8217;t happened before. Why don&#8217;t we have any <em>SimCity</em> on Switch? Mysterious stuff. Give me this one and I&#8217;ll forget about how you parked the IP in the river, EA. For a while, anyway.</p>
<p><strong>Terranigma</strong></p>
<p>The one that got away for North American Super NES owners. Quintet&#8217;s run on the Super NES was outstanding, and I&#8217;d love to see all of them available to buy again. If I had to pick just one, it&#8217;s this one. A very enjoyable action-RPG with a story that will definitely stick with you, wrapped up in a gorgeous package that rivals what Square was doing on the console in a lot of ways. I&#8217;m hoping this can one day slip out of the vault it&#8217;s currently trapped in, even if that place isn&#8217;t on the Switch.</p>
<p><strong>U.N. Squadron</strong></p>
<p>There&#8217;s something about<em> U.N. Squadron</em> that tends to click with people who aren&#8217;t usually into shoot &#8217;em ups. Maybe it&#8217;s the manga/anime-born style. Perhaps it&#8217;s the lenience afforded by the life bar. The cool jets? Maybe it&#8217;s the cool jets. Whatever the case, it would be amazing to see this game get a reissue. It&#8217;s tied up with a license which probably makes things difficult, but I&#8217;ll keep my fingers crossed that Capcom makes it happen someday.</p>
<p><strong>Spider-Man &amp; Venom: Maximum Carnage</strong></p>
<p>The author&#8217;s bias on full display here, wow. <em>Maximum Carnage</em>? That iffy beat &#8217;em up based on the comic event where Spider-Man defeats Carnage with the literal power of friendship? Yes. I love this game. It is stupid and kind of bad and I love it. I want it, even if I have to put up with it being packaged with <em>Separation Anxiety</em> or something. Get Green Jelly on the phone, patch Marvel into the conference call, find whoever bought the husk of LJN/Acclaim, and get it done.</p>
