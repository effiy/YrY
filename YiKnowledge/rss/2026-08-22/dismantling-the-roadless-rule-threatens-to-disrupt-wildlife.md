<p>Pause for a moment and listen. What do you hear? Chances are, somewhere in the background, is the ever-present hum of a road.</p>
<p>More than <a href="https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2023/vmt421c.cfm">4.2 million miles of public roads</a> crisscross the lower 48 states—enough to <a href="https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/moon-distance/en/">reach the Moon and back</a> almost nine times. This vast network of roads spiderwebs its way across the contiguous US, <a href="https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/csp2.288">leaving only about 5 percent</a> as an inventoried <a href="https://gagecarto.github.io/roadless/">roadless area</a> or <a href="https://wilderness.net/learn-about-wilderness/fast-facts/">wilderness</a>.</p>
<p>Now, some of those last remaining lands free of roads are under threat from the Trump administration’s <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-16965/special-areas-roadless-area-conservation">proposed rollback</a> of the <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R46504#_Ref48593706">2001 Roadless Rule</a>. That includes southeast Alaska’s <a href="https://seacc.org/our-work/tongass/">Tongass National Forest</a>, where eagles, bears, salmon, and many other species thrive in old-growth coastal forest along the Inside Passage.</p><p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/08/dismantling-the-roadless-rule-threatens-to-disrupt-wildlife-and-water-in-us/">Read full article</a></p>
