<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="The Loveliness of Letting Go: An Illustrated Ode to Life&#8217;s Lasts" class="cover-landscape alignright size-medium" height="349" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends_cover.jpg?fit=320%2C349&amp;ssl=1" width="320" /></a></p><p>The great cruelty of life, and also its great mercy, is <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2024/02/16/endling/">that only hindsight ever knows each last</a> &#8212; the last time your lover put their hand on the small of your back, the last sip of the last cup at your favorite coffee shop before it vanished overnight, the last living note of your mother&#8217;s voice. Bittersweet or downright devastating, lasts are the currency of loss with which we pay the price for loving. They are the <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/11/12/necessary-losses/">necessary losses</a> that make us who we are, our training ground for <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2026/07/03/one-art/">mastering the art of losing</a> by which we apprentice ourselves to love. </p>
<p>Great children&#8217;s books are works of philosophy in disguise &#8212; philosophy being, in Montaigne&#8217;s perfect definition, the art of learning to die, which is of course <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2026/08/18/camus-sea/">the key to learning to live</a>, just as learning to lose is the key to learning to love. <a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><strong><em>Odds and Ends</em></strong></a> (<a href="https://search.worldcat.org/title/1534513870" target="_blank"><em>public library</em></a>) by Finnish author and artist Maija Hurme is one such book &#8212; a tender illustrated catalogue of losses, from the everyday to the existential, that is at bottom a love letter to what we have, to the bittersweet knowledge that we have none of it for the keeping, calling to mind poet Lisel Mueller&#8217;s poignant line: <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2020/02/24/immortality-in-passing-lisel-mueller/">“What exists, exists so that it can be lost and become precious.”</a></p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87759" height="765" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends1.jpg?resize=680%2C765&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>Taxonomized by type, there are the &#8220;late lasts&#8221; (&#8220;the last rays of the sun, spreading color across the sky&#8221;), the &#8220;hopeful lasts&#8221; (&#8220;the last migrating bird to arrive might still find a mate&#8221;), the &#8220;last chances&#8221; (&#8220;a lost toy is always found in the very last place you look&#8221;), the &#8220;lasts that are firsts&#8221; (a baby&#8217;s &#8220;last wobbly moment before she starts walking&#8221;), the &#8220;lingering lasts&#8221; (&#8220;watching the last snowflake of winter drift slowly and silently down&#8221;), the &#8220;lonely lasts&#8221; (&#8220;the last tree standing&#8221;). </p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87753" height="745" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends6.jpg?resize=680%2C745&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87754" height="471" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends7.jpg?resize=680%2C471&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87755" height="755" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends8.jpg?resize=680%2C755&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>Page after page we come to feel that we are rehearsing for the final act, the &#8220;very last last&#8221; that awaits every living thing.</p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87756" height="773" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends9.jpg?resize=680%2C773&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>Everything ends with a last:<br />
the very last thought,<br />
the very last breath,<br />
the very last heartbeat.<br />
And the very last goodbyes.</p></blockquote>
<p>Pulsating beneath it all is an invitation to practice <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/11/12/necessary-losses/">the art of letting go</a>, which, paradoxically, is what allows us to go on living. </p>
<p><a href="https://enchantedlion.com/all-books/odds-and-ends" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-87757" height="754" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/oddsandends10.jpg?resize=680%2C754&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>When we look up at the night sky, we see a star suddenly twinkle and then go out.</p>
<p>Maria tells me that this star died hundreds or millions of years ago, long before we were even born. The last rays of its light are reflected in our eyes.</p></blockquote>
