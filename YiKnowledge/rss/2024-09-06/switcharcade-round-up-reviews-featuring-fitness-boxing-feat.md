<h3><strong>Reviews &amp; Mini-Views</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU ($49.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328668" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Fitness-Boxing-feat-HATSUNE-MIKU-switch-review-main.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>After Imagineer&#8217;s success with the <em>Fitness Boxing</em> games that led to the hilarious but good <em>Fitness Boxing Fist of the North Star,</em> I didn&#8217;t know what IP the team would work with for a new fitness game. When <em>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em> was announced, I thought it was a smart collaboration, but was curious to see how the game felt after <em>Fist of the North Star</em>. I&#8217;ve been playing it over the last few weeks alongside <em>Ring Fit Adventure</em> and I&#8217;m impressed with <em>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em> in many ways.</p>
<p>If you&#8217;re new to the<em> Fitness Boxing</em> series, these games, as the name suggests, use boxing and rhythm game movements to get you into shape through daily exercises, mini-games that also get you sweating, and more. With <em>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em>, everyone&#8217;s favorite vocaloid gets in on the action and there&#8217;s even a separate dedicated mode for her songs in addition to the normal songs included in this release. Keep in mind that this is a Joy-Con only game so you can&#8217;t play it with a Pro Controller or any third party accessories as far as I&#8217;m aware.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328669" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Fitness-Boxing-feat-HATSUNE-MIKU-switch-review-3.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>As with other games in the series, there are difficulty options, a free training mode to do as you please, warmups, and tracking with reminders and the systemwide alarm that can remind you even if your Switch is in sleep mode. There are cosmetics you can unlock through the points you earn by playing as well. I can&#8217;t comment on the DLC yet, but as for the base release, I liked<em> Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em> more than <em>Fist of the North Star</em> aside from one issue.</p>
<p>The audio in <em>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em> is great, but the main instructor&#8217;s voice isn&#8217;t great. I ended up turning this voice down because it felt out of place with the rest of the game, and just sounded a bit weirdly directed.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328670" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Fitness-Boxing-feat-HATSUNE-MIKU-switch-review-2.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>Just like the <em>Fitness Boxing Fist of the North Star</em> release from Imagineer, <em>Fitness Boxing feat. HATSUNE MIKU</em> is a solid fitness-focused game that does a great job of bringing Miku to the world of <em>Fitness Boxing</em> to try and appeal to her fans. While it is a competent fitness game with its daily workout, customization, and tracking, I feel like it would be better as a complement to something like<em> Ring Fit Adventure</em> or your own other exercise routine rather than being the sole exercise you do during the week. <em>-Mikhail Madnani</em></p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Magical Delicacy ($24.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328687" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Magical-Delicacy-switch-review-main.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>Magical Delicacy</em> from sKaule and Whitethorn Games is one I hadn&#8217;t paid much attention to until a friend of mine linked me to an Xbox Game Pass announcement for it. Since then, I&#8217;ve played it on Switch as well, but feel like it needs a bit more time in the oven. I adore Metroidvania-style platformers and cooking games, but it feels like <em>Magical Delicacy</em> takes the good bits of both genres while not putting them together in the best way possible. This results in a game I adore in parts, but one where the flaws hurt a bit more.</p>
<p>You play as Flora who is a young witch in a story that&#8217;s equal parts mysterious, magical, and wholesome. Your aim is to cook meals and craft for various folks, and I was surprised at how well the exploration was implemented aside from the backtracking that annoyed me sometimes. I expected it to be lacking compared to the cooking and crafting, but the team nailed the Metroidvania elements. There are some issues with ingredients in the crafting and in how inventory is managed. This is made a bit worse by the UI that I took a while to get used to with interaction.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328688" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Magical-Delicacy-switch-review-4.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>Magical Delicacy</em> makes a great first impression with its gorgeous pixel art visuals, lovely music, and tons of settings. The latter includes UI scale and text options that are both very useful when playing on Switch in handheld mode. I feel like <em>Magical Delicacy</em> would have benefited a lot from an early access release or will from potential updates.</p>
<p>When it comes to the Switch version, it plays very well aside from some seemingly frame pacing-related hiccups. It has good rumble as well. Having played it on Xbox Series X, this is definitely a game I&#8217;d play more on a portable so a Switch or Steam Deck version would be best. I just play the genre more on handhelds.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328690" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Magical-Delicacy-switch-review-2.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>Magical Delicacy</em> is a game I should&#8217;ve loved given it is basically a blend of a Metroidvania and a crooking/crafting game, but it feels a little undercooked (I couldn&#8217;t resist) in parts thanks to some inventory and backtracking issues. While it is a very good game right now that feels at home on Switch, a few quality of life updates and polishing will make it an essential. <em>-Mikhail Madnani</em></p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Aero The Acro-Bat 2 ($5.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328862" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/aerotheacrobat2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>A lot of companies tried their hand at the whole mascot platformer thing in the 16-bit era in the wake of <em>Sonic the Hedgehog</em>&#8216;s explosive success. Like, the sheer number of them is just baffling. Even more than I remembered. Not many of them managed to see sequels, but <em>Aero The Acro-Bat</em> is part of that elite few. How each member of that club got there is its own story, but in Aero&#8217;s case it seems to have been a combination of the first game going over well enough, and sheer force of will to try and make fetch happen. Sadly, fetch did not happen. <em>Aero</em> spin-off <em>Zero the Kamikaze Squirrel</em> launched several months after <em>Aero 2</em>, but outside of that Aero was never seen in another original title again.</p>
<p>Does that make <em>Aero The Acro-Bat 2</em> a bad game? I don&#8217;t think so. It doesn&#8217;t seem like it was a big enough success to merit another follow-up, at the very least. But then again, Sunsoft&#8217;s parent company had recently lost a ton of money on a bad investment involving golf courses, and that certainly put it in a fiscally conservative mood for a while. Either way, I don&#8217;t think the fault can be placed on the quality of the game. It&#8217;s as good as the first game was, with all that implies. A more polished experience that in shedding some of those rough edges loses some of its character, but a decent enough platforming romp.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328863" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/aero2-2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I was fully expecting this release to be in Ratalaika&#8217;s usual emulation wrapper, but much to my surprise there&#8217;s been an upgrade in the presentation. This feels a lot more tailor-made for the game, and in terms of both options and extras I think this is a far better experience overall. Box and manual scans, achievements, a gallery of sprite sheets, a jukebox, a bunch of cheats, and more. I frankly have no complaints at all about how the game plays and the suite of features here. The only nit I will pick is that you only get the Super NES version of the game here. I feel like the SEGA Genesis/Mega Drive version should have been included too, just to keep everyone happy.</p>
<p>Those who enjoyed<em> Aero The Acro-Bat</em> should naturally give <em>Aero The Acro-Bat 2</em> a shot. I even think those who were slightly put off by the first game&#8217;s quirks might have a better time here. I really have to commend Ratalaika for stepping up its game with the emulation wrapper for this release. Perhaps I&#8217;m being greedy, but I&#8217;d love to see the first game updated with a similar interface just for consistency. A fine release for <em>Aero</em> fans and those on the look out for 16-bit platformers to play, and one that makes me optimistic for the remaining reissues of this series.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 3.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Metro Quester | Osaka ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328864" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/metroquesterosaka.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I got quite a bit of enjoyment out of the original <em>Metro Quester</em> when it released a while back. It&#8217;s a bit of a bear to get into, but once you understand its rules and what you have to do, it&#8217;s an immensely satisfying dungeon-crawling turn-based RPG. Experimenting with party builds to try to find the optimal team was a task I truly enjoyed taking on. With that in mind, I was very happy to see that there was another game in the series coming. <em>Metro Quester | Osaka</em> feels a little more like an expansion pack than a proper sequel, but I&#8217;m more than happy with that given how much I got out of the original.</p>
<p>The story here is a prequel to the original game, and we&#8217;ve moved to Osaka as a setting. You get a whole new dungeon to explore, and some new character types to do that exploring with. Osaka is a much wetter location, so you&#8217;ll need to use your canoe to travel over the water in places. With the new dungeon comes new weapons, skills, and enemies to deal with. Definitely more to chew on here for those who have wrung out the original game, and if you had a good time there then you will enjoy this one, too.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328865" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/metroquesterosaka2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>That said, this game shares much of its broader mechanics with the original <em>Metro Quester</em>. If you need an explanation, please check out <a href="https://toucharcade.com/2024/01/08/metro-quester-switch-review-terra-nil-nintendo-eshop/">my review</a> of that game for more details. Short version: turn-based combat, top-down dungeon exploration, and a whole lot of inching your way outward a little further each time before having to head back to camp. You know, the good stuff. Those who are careless will find themselves in hot water very quickly, as planning and cautious play is practically a necessity in this game.</p>
<p>Those who found themselves fully absorbed in <em>Metro Quester</em> will find plenty to enjoy in <em>Metro Quester | Osaka</em>, and new players should probably just skip right to this game. It&#8217;s more of an expansion pack than a sequel, but that in no way should be taken as a bad thing. Rather, it just seems like the designer wanted to send players on another adventure that pushes out the existing systems in some fascinating ways. It&#8217;s certainly a game you&#8217;ll have to be patient with to get the most out of, but those willing to invest the time will enjoy the dividends.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
