<h3><strong>Steam Deck Game Reviews &#038; Impressions</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>NBA 2K25 Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328842" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nba-2k25-steam-deck-review-gameplay-hdr.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>While many scoff at yearly sports releases, I&#8217;ve always been a fan of 2K&#8217;s NBA games despite the issues. NBA 2K25 is notable for a few reasons. The first is that this is the first time since the PS5 generation began that the PC version of NBA 2K25 is actually the &#8220;Next Gen" version rather than being based on the older consoles. The second reason is that the <a href="https://nba.2k.com/2k25/pc-faq/">official PC FAQ</a> confirmed that NBA 2K25 is optimized for Steam Deck. While it doesn&#8217;t have an official rating from Valve yet, this was great to see. Now that I&#8217;ve played NBA 2K25 on Steam Deck and also both consoles thanks to review codes and buying a disc, I&#8217;m more than pleased with the game, but it has some of the usual issues you&#8217;d expect.</p>
<p>If you&#8217;ve been playing NBA 2K on PC for a few years, the standout features of this year&#8217;s entry are the ProPLAY technology for improved gameplay which has only been on PS5 and Xbox Series X prior to NBA 2K25. WNBA also makes its PC debut with NBA 2K25 alongside the MyNBA mode. If you ended up skipping the last few years of NBA 2K games on PC in the hope of getting the full experience, NBA 2K25 is it for sure, and I hope it does well enough se we keep seeing not only the Next Gen version on PC, but also 2K doing good work on Steam Deck.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328830" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nba-2k25-steam-deck-review-gameplay-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On PC and Steam Deck, NBA 2K25 has 16:10 and 800p support which is already a good sign. Beyond that, it has AMD FSR 2, DLSS, and XeSS support, but I disabled this. I&#8217;ll get into why a bit later. You can also adjust v-sync, dynamic v-sync that targets 90fps during gameplay and 45fps outside that (including The City), toggle HDR (yes it works on Steam Deck), texture detail, overall quality, and two shader options. I recommend letting the game cache all the shaders on initial boot for the best possible experience during gameplay. For some reason, NBA 2K25 on Steam Deck does a quick shader cache on each boot. It isn&#8217;t too long but is notable. </p>
<p>The NBA 2K25 PC graphics options have an advanced menu with many options including shader detail, shadow detail, player detail, crowd detail, NPC density, volumetric effects, reflections, era filters, global illumination, ambient occlusion, TAA, motion blur, depth of field, bloom, and max anisotropy. This is quite a feature packed PC port when it comes to settings. I left most on low or medium with turning off the upscaling since it made gameplay look a bit too blurry. The main settings I left at medium were player level and shader detail. I also capped the game to 60fps at 60hz using the Steam Deck quick access menu. This provided the best and most stable experience with better clarity than the defaults. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328831" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nba-2k25-steam-deck-review-gameplay-settings-display.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Speaking of the default, if you don&#8217;t care about adjusting any settings, the game has a Steam Deck visual preset which works fine out of the box, but it looked a bit too blurry for my liking. This is what made me play around with the settings over the last few days. </p>
<p>I was curious if NBA 2K25 is playable offline. I know many of the modes require an internet connection but what happens if you try booting up the game when not online? When trying this on my Steam Deck OLED in airplane mode with wifi turned off, the game booted up but didn&#8217;t let me load MyCAREER or MyTEAM. There&#8217;s a Connect option in the menu to retry connection, but I could get into quick play and eras fine while offline.  I noticed some load times were quicker when offline as well. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328829" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nba-2k25-steam-deck-review-gameplay-vsync-dynamic-90hz.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>When looking at the console versions, NBA 2K25 is a much better experience than Steam Deck technically, but I find myself playing it more on Valve&#8217;s handheld after years of enjoying these games on Switch and more-recently on Steam Deck. The biggest differentiator is the load times. Even on my Steam Deck OLED&#8217;s internal SSD, NBA 2K25 doesn&#8217;t load as fast as PS5 or Xbox Series X. It isn&#8217;t as bad as older systems, but this was a notable difference. Beyond that, keep in mind that there is no crossplay for the PC version with both consoles. </p>
<p>As with every recent NBA 2K, the microtransactions are still a problem. This is important if you want to play some of the modes that have a focus on them. If you just want to get this to have a great looking and playing basketball game, they might as well not exist, but keep that in mind when you make your purchase since NBA 2K25 is a $69.99 game on PC as opposed to being priced lower for the last few years. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328833" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nba-2k25-steam-deck-review-gameplay-settings.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>With NBA 2K25, the Steam Deck now delivers a fantastic portable basketball experience at feature parity with PS5 and Xbox Series X. With a bit of tweaking, it looks and runs great, and I&#8217;m glad to see 2K finally bring all the features to PC after many years. If you own a Steam Deck and were hoping for a good experience with NBA 2K25, 2K and Visual Concepts have delivered. Just be aware of the microtransactions in some of the game modes though.</p>
<p><strong>NBA 2K25 Steam Deck review score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Gimmick! 2 Steam Deck Impressions</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328835" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/gimmick-2-steam-deck-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>If you&#8217;re not familiar with Gimmick! 2 yet, read Shaun&#8217;s review of the Switch version <a href="https://toucharcade.com/2024/09/04/gimmick-2-switch-review-ace-attorney-investigations-collection-eshop-nintendo/">here.</a> I was curious to see how it felt on Steam Deck. As of this writing, Gimmick! 2 hasn&#8217;t been tested by Valve, but it runs fine out of the box. In fact, it even has a mention of Steam Deck and Linux fixes in its newest patch.</p>
<p>Gimmick! 2 is currently capped at 60fps on Steam Deck, and I even recommend forcing your Steam Deck screen to 60hz if you play on an OLED to avoid any jitter issues. There are no graphics options at all, but it does seem to support 16:10 display by default for the menus at least. When I forced the game to run at 1080p on the Steam Deck&#8217;s own screen, I wanted to make sure the game wasn&#8217;t stretching the visuals like some others do. I can confirm it indeed supports 16:10 properly after making sure of the aspect ratio of the UI and other elements. This only applies to menus though as the actual gameplay runs at 16:9.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328834" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/gimmick-2-steam-deck-review-graphics.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>While I was hoping to see Gimmick! 2 support above 60fps, it isn&#8217;t a deal breaker or even close. I wouldn&#8217;t be surprised if this gets Steam Deck Verified soon because it runs perfectly out of the box. As for the game itself, I definitely agree with Shaun&#8217;s review linked above. I&#8217;m just glad to see Gimmick! 2 run so well out of the box on Steam Deck.</p>
<h3><strong>Arco Steam Deck Mini Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328845" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/arco-steam-deck-review.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>Arco is a game I adored from the get go, but it felt like it was a few updates away from greatness. The dynamic turn-based RPG with pixel art visuals and a lovely story hit PC and Switch a few weeks ago, and it has gotten a major update on Steam already fixing most of my issues. As of this writing, the update isn&#8217;t live on Switch and I decided to review the Steam Deck version since that is the most up to date right now.</p>
<p>When looking at Arco trailers, you might think it is just a nice looking tactical game. Well, it is a lot more than that, and the combat system is very interesting. In fact, the audio and story surprised me the most. Arco has you experiencing different stories with characters that have unique gameplay styles (in a way). While it is a turn-based affair, there are real-time elements. I saw someone call it Superhot meets a pixel art tactical RPG and I feel like that isn&#8217;t giving you the full picture, but it is a glimpse into the kind of blend of gameplay you can expect here.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328822" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/arco-steam-deck-review-2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On Steam Deck, Arco ships Verified from the get go, and I had no issues playing it on both my Steam Decks. It is capped to 60fps as far as I can tell and has 16:9 support only. The current build on Steam Deck includes an assist mode (beta) letting you skip combat, get infinite dynamite, and more. These are good to have if you want to skip the combat, but I&#8217;m happiest about the option to skip the first act when replaying the game. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328848" height="1080" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/arco-combat-gameplay-steam-deck.jpg" width="1920" /></p>
<p>On paper, Arco seemed like it would be very good, but I didn&#8217;t expect to love it as much as I do now. It feels dynamic across the board with superb visuals, music, and a lovely story. If you want an interesting tactical RPG with a memorable story, get Arco right now. There&#8217;s even a free demo you can try on Steam right now.</p>
<p><strong>Arco Steam Deck review score: 5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Skull and Bones Steam Deck Steam Deck Mini Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328837" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/skull-and-bones-steam-deck-review-5.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Skull and Bones is an interesting release to cover. It launched on PS5, Xbox Series X, and PC earlier this year, but wasn&#8217;t on Steam until just over a week ago. Having never played it before, I wasn&#8217;t sure what to expect aside from a friend of mine telling me he enjoyed it despite its many flaws on Xbox. I was also curious to see how it felt on Steam Deck as a current generation console title, but Ubisoft posting about it being playable gave me some hope. For this review, I&#8217;m focusing on the Steam Deck port more than the game.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328839" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/skull-and-bones-steam-deck-review-3.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On Steam Deck, Skull and Bones has an official Valve rating of being Playable. On booting it up for the first time, you have to login to your Ubisoft Connect account and this process isn&#8217;t smooth because the interface feels sluggish. I was hoping it would just pick up my details since I logged into Ubisoft Connect for Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown, but I had to do it again for Skull and Bones. Beyond that, the tutorial runs fine, but I wanted to get more stable performance. I set it to a 30fps frame rate limit and it runs at 16:10 and 800p. I opted for FSR 2 quality upscaling as well, though performance is more stable with the performance upscaling preset. I left everything else on low aside from textures which I set to high. I found this delivered a good experience overall. </p>
<p>As for Skull and Bones as a game itself, I&#8217;m still early on since I&#8217;ve only played it for just over a week now, but I see the potential here. If Ubisoft keeps supporting it, it will be an easier recommendation. It is already a better experience than before looking at what was added in recent updates.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328841" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/skull-and-bones-steam-deck-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Skull and Bones is a hard game to recommend at full price, but I do recommend checking out the free trial. I enjoy naval combat and open world Ubisoft games, and Skull and Bones with all its updates so far is good, but it has potential to be better. If you do plan on playing Skull and Bones on Steam Deck, keep in mind that it is an online only experience. I will likely grab it on console to also play there with cross progression.</p>

<h3><strong>ODDADA Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328847" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/oddada-steam-deck-review-2.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>I love interactive toys like Townscaper because they are excellent at letting me spend a few minutes trying to create something cool. When ODDADA was announced, it felt like the next great interactive toy for me, and that&#8217;s exactly what it ended up being with a small caveat in its controls.</p>
<p>ODDADA is not technically a game, but a music maker hybrid that feels like a game. The aesthetic reminds me of <a href="https://toucharcade.com/2012/01/13/windosill-ipad-review/">Windosill</a> in a way, but it really is a gorgeous and elegant little toolbox with a plethora of ways for you to create something special. You interact with it using a mouse or touch controls on Steam Deck, and slowly progress through the different levels and instruments working your way to the end. The randomness element makes it so every creation sounds and feels different. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328820" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/oddada-steam-deck-gameplay-review-graphics-settings.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On Steam Deck, I mentioned ODDADA has no controller support yet, but it runs perfectly at 90fps out of the box. There are a few graphics options like resolution, v-sync, and anti-aliasing toggles. You don&#8217;t need to worry much about settings in this one, but the text in the menu is a bit small for Steam Deck.</p>
<p>Aside from the lack of controller support, I have nothing bad to say about ODDADA. I would argue that even with full controller support, it will play best with touch or mouse controls, but I know some folks prefer a controller across the board. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328821" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/oddada-steam-deck-gameplay-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>If you enjoy music, art, or just want to make your own music with a lovely little game, ODDADA is for you. In its current state, ODDADA doesn&#8217;t have controller support, but it plays perfectly with touch controls on Steam Deck. The team is working on getting it Steam Deck Verified so keep that in mind if you plan on purchasing it now or later.</p>
<p><strong>ODDADA Steam Deck review score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Star Trucker Steam Deck Mini Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328850" height="1080" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/star-trucker-2-steam.jpg" width="1920" /></p>
<p>Sometimes, a game is released blending two genres together in a way that might not satiate either fanbase. These situations can result in a game that doesn&#8217;t hit as hard for anyone, but there&#8217;s also a chance that it manages to capture a new audience. I feel like Star Trucker is one of those with how it tries to do automobile simulation and space exploration at once, but without going too deep into either of those genres. This one hasn&#8217;t been rated by Valve, but I didn&#8217;t have any issues playing it on Proton Experimental right now. </p>
<p>Your aim in Star Trucker is to explore space, take on jobs, earn money, and unlock more to do on the massive galactic map. It includes many difficulty options and the ability to tweak things as you like before starting a new game. While I love the gameplay loop, the real highlights are the visuals, writing, and voiced banter on the radio. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328826" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/star-trucker-steam-deck-graphics.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On PC and Steam Deck, Star Trucker lets you adjust video mode, resolution (16:10 support included), refresh rate, v-sync, graphics quality, render scale, shadow quality, temporal anti-aliasing, ambient occlusion, mesh detail, and light shafts. I opted to use a custom preset with low shadows but left everything else at normal. I also turned off temporal anti-aliasing. This was good enough for a 40fps or so target. I&#8217;m sure someone will find a better compromise in visuals for improved performance, but I was happy with this while playing.</p>
<p>The major issues holding Star Trucker back are the controls that took me a good while to get used to. I hope these see some improvements in updates. </p>
<p>Star Trucker surprised me with its blend of truck simulation and its setting in a way I didn&#8217;t think was possible. This feels like a game catering to what I enjoy a lot in automobile simulation and exploration games. While it might not be for everyone, I&#8217;ve enjoyed playing Star Trucker quite a bit ever since I got review code on Steam Deck. I hope the developers optimize it a bit more for Valve&#8217;s handheld though.</p>
<p><strong>Star Trucker Steam Deck review score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia Steam Deck Mini Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328844" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/date-a-live-ren-dystopia-steam-deck-review.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>Originally released in Japan on PS4 only back in 2020, Sting and Idea Factory&#8217;s DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia has made its way to the West only on Steam, and it is a great follow up to DATE A LIVE: Rio Reincarnation. If you&#8217;re not familiar with it, Date A Live is a light novels series from Koshi Tachibana with illustrations by Tsunako. DATE A LIVE: Rio Reincarnation was a great introduction to that world, and every fan of that release needs to play DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328824" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/date-a-live-ren-dystopia-steam-deck-review-2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia has you playing as Shido who has a dream about a girl named Ren, and this is where the narrative begins with multiple paths and returning characters. There are many choices to make as you&#8217;d expect, and it is all accompanied by very good art from Tsunako. DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia feels more lighthearted across the board, and it serves as a perfect compliment and follow-up to DATE A LIVE: Rio Reincarnation. I&#8217;m just surprised it took this long to see it released in English.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328825" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/date-a-live-ren-dystopia-steam-deck-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia runs perfectly on Stema Deck out of the box. I didn&#8217;t need to change anything. It has 16:9 support at 720p and plays all cut-scenes without issues out of the box. I recommend checking the system settings to ensure the confirm button is set to A and not B. Also make sure it isn&#8217;t stretching the 16:9 image to 16:10 in fullscreen mode.</p>
<p>DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia is an easy recommendation for fans of DATE A LIVE: Rio Reincarnation with its lovely art, music, and memorable characters. I don&#8217;t recommend playing it before Rio Reincarnation though.</p>
<h3><strong>DATE A LIVE: Ren Dystopia Steam Deck Review Score: 4/5</h3>
