<h3><strong>Steam Deck Game Reviews &#038; Impressions</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>Bakeru Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p>When Bakeru was released in Japan, a friend of mine imported it and praised it quite a bit. I tried the demo and was turned off by the technical issues. It was charming, but not enough to put up with no localization and performance issues on Switch. When it leaked on the eShop, I was hoping we&#8217;d see performance improvements or a port to PC. We ended up getting both more or less. Bakeru&#8217;s Switch release in the West is improved over the Japanese launch version, and it is also now on PC with an excellent port. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328096" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/bakeru-steam-deck-review-pc-port-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Bakeru is an action platformer from Good-Feel who are most known for the Yoshi games and also more-recently Princess Peach Showtime from Nintendo. The studio has done some games outside Nintendo brands, and the newest in this line of non Nintendo games from Good-Feel is Bakeru. Shaun will be doing a full review for this one, but so far Bakeru feels and plays great on Steam Deck.</p>
<p>On Steam Deck and PC, Bakeru lets you adjust display mode (windowed and borderless only), resolution (with full 16:10 support), toggle v-sync, adjust frame rate cap (Note that v-sync off means the game runs at 60fps. Turn it on if you would like to play at your monitor&#8217;s refresh rate or at 90fps on Steam Deck OLED), anti-aliasing (off, FXAA, SMAA), adjust filtering quality (trilinear, 2x, 4x, 8x, and 16x), and draw distance (low, medium, high). </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328095" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/bakeru-steam-deck-review-pc-port-2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>I played Bakeru on both my Steam Decks. My LCD is running the newest SteamOS beta. On that, I had zero issues with the game and it ran at a locked 60fps at maximum settings. On Steam Deck OLED running the public build of the OS, I only had some issues at 90fps. This seemingly wasn&#8217;t related to the settings, but something in the game because I had some shader-related or traversal stuttering in parts, but they only happened initially. I imagine Bakeru will be Steam Deck Verified soon as it runs perfectly if you play at 60fps and near-perfectly at 90fps. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328096" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/bakeru-steam-deck-review-pc-port-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>The one issue I ran into pre-release that Spike Chunsoft and the developers already fixed is button prompts being incorrectly displayed. This had the game showing Switch positions instead of Xbox ones when using my 8BitDo Xbox controller or the Steam Deck itself. I&#8217;m glad this was resolved pre-release as the game has no port issues, and it is dramatically better than the Switch version. When using a DualSense, it correctly showed PlayStation prompts with the proper labels and positions as well.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328094" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/bakeru-steam-deck-review-pc-port-3.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Bakeru is a good platformer that I recommend playing on PC over Switch right now. The Switch version, despite performance improvements over the Japanese demo, still has too much variance in its performance to be an easy recommendation. The HD Rumble there is nice, but everything else holds it back. On PC, you miss out on the HD Rumble, but Bakeru runs a lot better across the board, and I&#8217;ve been enjoying digging into it over the last week. Stay tuned for Shaun&#8217;s full review of the Switch version in the near future.</p>
<h3><strong>Sam &#038; Max: The Devil&#8217;s Playhouse Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p>There have been many game franchises that I completely missed out on over the years. The Sam &#038; Max series of games is one of those. When I was offered a code to play Sam &#038; Max: The Devil&#8217;s Playhouse, I figured now was the time to finally play these games with the new remasters. If you&#8217;re just here for the game itself, I urge you to read Shaun&#8217;s Switch review <a href="https://toucharcade.com/2024/08/20/elrentaros-wanderings-switch-review-sam-max-the-devils-playhouse-nintendo-eshop-release/">here</a>. I&#8217;m going to focus on how it plays on Steam Deck.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328091" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sam-and-max-the-devils-playhouse-remaster-steam-deck-review.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>Sam &#038; Max: The Devil&#8217;s Playhouse is the final game in the episodic Sam &#038; Max main trilogy. This new remaster is amazing in basically every way. This includes how it controls, looks, sounds, and feels. It even has many accessibility options to make things go smoothly if you&#8217;re new to this kind of adventure game. You can toggle different hint levels and disable flashing in the accessibility settings. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328102" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sam-and-max-the-devils-playhouse-steam-deck-pc-review-best-settings-90fps-oled.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>On the graphics and display side, you can play Sam &#038; Max: The Devil&#8217;s Playhouse at higher resolutions when docked on Steam Deck, but performance doesn&#8217;t hold close to 60fps if you play at 4K. I recommend sticking to it in handheld mode for a 60 or higher experience. You can adjust the graphics quality, texture quality, anti-aliasing, toggle v-sync, and adjust display mode and resolution in the graphics settings. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328101" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sam-and-max-the-devils-playhouse-steam-deck-pc-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>With Sam &#038; Max: The Devil&#8217;s Playhouse, I&#8217;m glad I finally got around to playing these games. Not only are they all absolutely worth your time and money, but they play superbly on Steam Deck. I&#8217;d love for the remasters to hit iPad, but until then the Steam Deck is my favorite way to play Skunkape Games&#8217; remasters.</p>
<h3><strong>SCHiM Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p>Given the sheer amount of releases lately, I didn&#8217;t get around to playing SCHiM much at launch. I&#8217;m glad I finally rectified that because it is equal parts charming and gorgeous, and it has been the perfect game to relax with as a break from the longer upcoming games I&#8217;m playing for review right now. Developers Ewoud van der Werf and Nils Slijkerman have released a brilliant take on a 3D platformer using light, shadow, and more for puzzles, and it is all elevated by excellent audio.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328103" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/schim-steam-deck-review.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>Aside from the striking aesthetic, what really carries SCHiM through its four or so hours long runtime is the animation work and sound design. As you work towards solving the various puzzles in the game, every interaction has a lovely little animation, and it all feels beyond polished when played at a rock solid 90fps on Steam Deck OLED. The blend of the shadows and lights for puzzles also are enhanced by the screen in handheld mode. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328099" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/schim-steam-deck-review-.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>So SCHiM runs perfectly out of the box on Steam Deck at 90fps, but how about docked? When played at 720p docked, it nearly does a locked 144fps. Playing at 1080p results in well over 100fps at all times. SCHiM supports 16:10 as well which is great to see on Steam Deck. I have no complaints with it aside from the fact that it might seem short for its price to some, and that there really is no evolution of the mechanics over the course of the game. While this isn&#8217;t a dealbreaker, it does hold SCHiM down from perfection.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328098" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/schim-steam-deck-review-2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>SCHiM is a lovely abstract take on games like Frogger for the modern era. Come into this expecting a work of art with excellent audio design more than a complex puzzle game. I enjoyed just about every level despite the mechanics not really evolving. I enjoyed it enough on Steam Deck to order a physical copy of the Switch version from Japan already. This is definitely worth your time if you enjoy delightful, gorgeous, and relaxing puzzle games.</p>
<p><strong>SCHiM Steam Deck review score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Gestalt: Steam and Cinder Steam Deck Review</h3>
<p></strong></p>
<p>Gestalt: Steam and Cinder is another game I wish I got around to playing sooner like SCHiM above. It has been on the review backlog for a bit now, but having played it, this action platformer is fantastic. Not only does it nail the aesthetic with its blend of 16 and 32-bit visuals, but the sound design, story, and gameplay are sublime.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328087" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/gestalt-steam-and-cinder-deck-pc.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>What initially drew me to Gestalt: Steam and Cinder, is the steampunk visual style in pixel art. I adore the character designs, but wasn&#8217;t sure another action platformer would be able to hit hard enough for me in recent years. Having played it, the story is very good, but without getting into spoilers, I feel like there&#8217;s more to this tale. Hopefully we see some story DLC in the future. </p>
<p>On the gameplay side, Gestalt: Steam and Cinder felt a bit easier than I expected outside of two bosses I had trouble with. Nothing major though, and the responsive controls help across the board.</p>
<p>Gestalt: Steam and Cinder ships Steam Deck Verified, and it is perfect on Valve&#8217;s handheld. The only thing I&#8217;d like to see, if feasible, is support for 90fps. Right now, the game is capped at 60fps and 16:9. I tested this on both my Steam Decks docked and in handheld mode. </p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-328097" height="800" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/gestalt-steam-and-cinder-steam-deck-review.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Given how crowded the genre is, I know most people roll their eyes when they see another action platformer, but Gestalt: Steam and Cinder is one you cannot afford to miss. It is one of the best in the genre with its excellent gameplay, narrative, music, and slick visuals. Gestalt: Steam and Cinder is also perfect on Steam Deck from start to finish. </p>
<p><strong>Gestalt: Steam and Cinder Steam Deck review score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun &#8211; Forges of Corruption Expansion Steam Deck Review</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-327837" height="337" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/warhammer40kboltgunforges.jpg" width="600" /></p>
<p>The original <em>Boltgun</em> was a refreshing surprise for me when it was released last year. While I&#8217;m not as into first-person shooters as deeply as some, I can certainly enjoy a good boomer shooter. Give me fast, furious action with some interest levels to explore, a satisfying selection of weapons, and challenging enemy encounters and I&#8217;m there. <em>Boltgun</em> had all of that, all dressed up in a <em>Warhammer</em> theme that if nothing else helps the world feel like it extends outside of the borders of the game.  A thoroughly enjoying game to play through, and one that left me properly sated&#8230; or <em>did</em> it?</p>
<p>While I thought I had had my fill of <em>Boltgun</em>, I was actually quite happy when <em>Forges of Corruption</em> was released. I guess I was still hungry. This expansion gives you five more levels of carnage to engage in, and they run on the larger side. You get two new guns to play with, one being an enhanced flamethrower and the other a rocket launcher. Sure, can&#8217;t argue with that. You also get some new enemies to try those toys out on, and you&#8217;ll need every edge you can get to deal with them.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-327838" height="337" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/warhammer40kboltgunforges2.jpg" width="600" /></p>
<p>When compared to the original game&#8217;s levels, <em>Forges of Corruption</em>&#8216;s stages favor more wide open spaces. This puts your mobility skills to the test, as you&#8217;ll have to keep on moving to avoid getting surrounded or cornered. If you haven&#8217;t mastered the dash already from playing the main campaign, you&#8217;ll need to get up to speed fast here. It&#8217;s an interesting change-up from the more compact design that the base game relied on so much. The enhanced flamethrower&#8217;s spread can really help you out here, and rockets always prefer open spaces.</p>
<p>Difficulty-wise, these levels are mostly going to be a relative breeze for those who played through the first game. The last one is a real beast though, and you&#8217;re going to have to use everything you&#8217;ve learned to overcome it. Which is to say that while you <em>can</em> play this expansion without finishing the main game, you really should leave it for after. All-up, it takes a few hours to get through it all.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-327839" height="337" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/warhammer40kboltgunforges3.jpg" width="600" /></p>
<p><em>Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun &#8211; Forges of Corruption</em> keep the party rolling with a set of new stages that show off a slightly different design intent than those of the original. The new weapons and enemies are appreciated, and the level design makes the most of both. I do wish there was something more interesting going on visually here, and the difficulty curve isn&#8217;t as smooth as it was in the base game. Still, those looking for more <em>Boltgun</em> challenges to tackle will find them here.<em>-Shaun Musgrave</em></p>
<p><strong>Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun &#8211; Forges of Corruption Expansion Steam Deck review score: 4/5</strong></p>
