<p>We may earn a commission from links on this page.</p><p>Fans of bad vibes and cynicism found their perfect series in <em>Black Mirror</em>, Charlie Brooker&rsquo;s modern take on the <em>Twilight Zone</em>. The show&rsquo;s focus on technology and all the ways it can ruin and terrify (and, <em>very</em> occasionally, save and sustain) us has shown us the darkest sides of social media, memory retention, virtual worlds, and dating algorithms, usually to chilling effect. If the show&rsquo;s long breaks between its short seasons have you craving more dystopia and tech-fueled psychological horror (and you've watched all the <a href="https://lifehacker.com/best-shows-like-black-mirror" target="_blank">similar series out there</a>) maybe what you need is more long-form desolation. If that&rsquo;s you, there&rsquo;s one movie that fits the <em>Black Mirror</em> vibe perfectly: <em>Moon</em>, starring Sam Rockwell.</p><h2 id="why-moon-is-the-perfect-companion-to-black-mirror">Why <em>Moon</em> is the perfect companion to <em>Black Mirror</em></h2><div class="mx-auto mt-8 w-full max-w-4xl aspect-video">
    <div id="video-container-01M15N5GJS79JFBPDTXNMEKSAH"></div>
</div>


<p>This mind-bending sci-fi movie starts off grim and just gets grimmer, pivoting off the same paranoia and distrust of technology as <em>Black Mirror</em>. Sam Bell (Sam Rockwell) is the sole inhabitant of a largely automated helium-3 mining facility on the dark side of the Moon. With only the AI named GERTY (played by Kevin Spacey) for company, Sam believes he&rsquo;s nearing the end of a three-year contract and will soon return to his family on Earth. When he discovers his own badly-injured body at a crashed rover site outside the facility, however, his reality starts dissolving as he realizes the truth: He&rsquo;s ultimately just an asset owned by a corporation, and he&rsquo;s never going home.</p><p>What makes <em>Moon</em> the perfect film for <em>Black Mirror</em> fans is the way it both explores the dark side of a scientific breakthrough (easy, robust cloning) and also just plain messes with your head. Sam&rsquo;s initial contentment gives way to puzzlement, and then to angry horror as he figures out what&rsquo;s going on. The story exposes how technology that <em>should </em>improve our lives can be leveraged against us to rob us of agency and exploit us in ways we can&rsquo;t currently imagine.</p><p>At the same time, like the best <em>Black Mirror</em> episodes (&ldquo;San Junipero,&rdquo; for example), <em>Moon</em> ultimately pivots to an ending that simultaneously surprises and reinforces how resilient and unstoppable the human spirit really is. Technology, the film argues, might be nightmarish when misused, but being human means we will always find a way to wriggle free of its grasp and find something close to justice. Add in a typically amazing performance from Rockwell, and you&rsquo;ve got the perfect movie to watch after you&rsquo;ve exhausted your supply of <em>Black Mirror</em> episodes.
