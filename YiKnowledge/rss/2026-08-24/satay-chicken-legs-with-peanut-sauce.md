<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-satay-chicken-legs-with-peanut-sauce">Satay Chicken Legs with Peanut Sauce</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I feel like chicken legs is a cut that is often overlooked, and I don&rsquo;t know why. You get the juicy meat of thighs, the fun of drumsticks, and they&rsquo;re good value. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So here&rsquo;s a recipe to try to get you excited about using drumsticks! I&rsquo;m giving them a <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/thai-chicken-satay-peanut-sauce/" id="34977" type="post">satay</a> makeover &ndash; marinated in Thai flavours, roasted until golden, then served with a creamy peanut dipping sauce that you can dunk the entire drumstick into.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Big flavour, little spend!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-ingredients-in-satay-chicken-legs-with-peanut-sauce">Ingredients in Satay Chicken Legs with Peanut Sauce</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This has the same ingredients as <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/thai-chicken-satay-peanut-sauce/" id="34977" type="post">Thai Chicken Satay Skewers</a>, but the cooking method and quantities for the marinade and sauce have been adapted to suit chicken legs. Also, I intentionally made the sauce thinner so it&rsquo;s suitable for dunking the entire drumstick in! </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-left" id="h-1-chicken-and-satay-marinade">1. Chicken and satay marinade</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Marinating is optional. You will still get plenty of tasty flavour in the chicken even without marinating time.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202166" height="857" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Satay-Drumsticks-Ingredients-1.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Drumsticks &ndash;</strong> You&rsquo;ll need 10 large drumsticks, totally around 1.5kg / 3lb. If yours are smaller, use more, or larger, use less. Just make sure they will all fit on the tray with a little space between them.</p><p><strong>Other chicken cuts &ndash; </strong>Wings and boneless thighs can be used and cooked in the oven. Breast is better done on the stove. See recipe card notes for directions.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Small can coconut milk &ndash; </strong>Here in Australia, some brands of coconut milk come in a handy small 270ml/90oz can which turns out to be just the right amount to make the satay marinade for the chicken plus a good amount of sauce (enough for chicken dunking and rice dousing). If you can only get standard 400ml/14oz cans, that&rsquo;s totally fine, just measure out what you need. You&rsquo;ll have 1/2 cup left over &ndash; use it to make the peanut sauce for <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/gado-gado-indonesian-salad-with-peanut-sauce/" id="47286" type="post">Gado Gado</a>!</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Thai red curry paste</strong>&nbsp;&ndash; The secret ingredient in Thai satay, for both the marinade and peanut sauce! Maesri is my preferred brand (photo <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/uploads/2019/03/best-Thai-red-curry-paste.jpg">here</a>). Authentic flavour, cheap and even carried at regular shops here in Australia (<a href="https://www.coles.com.au/product/maesri-red-curry-paste-114g-1323800?cid=col_cpc_Generic%7CColesSupermarkets%7CPLA%7CPantry%7CAustralia%7CBroad&amp;s_kwcid=AL!12693!3!683434678444!!!g!1769519071423!&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=20838080074&amp;gbraid=0AAAAADzlvJeUqzcqnwynsWxNADgC4nR60&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwtKrUBhAhEiwAr77ZovfoyM8VQpZDfUDn4REBmNsI1-5uWi8bEMFbkoV2wemUks0iIzcXXhoClpQQAvD_BwE">Coles</a>, <a href="https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/45458?utm_source=google&amp;utm_medium=cpc&amp;utm_campaign=WW-0001&amp;cq_net=g&amp;cq_src=GOOGLE&amp;cq_cmp=SUPC-0001_Always%20On_Shopping__Catch%20All_Search_Conversion_8458_815925_FY26_L3_Buy_National-AU&amp;cq_med=11055829892&amp;cq_plac=&amp;cq_term=PRODUCT_GROUP&amp;ds_adt=pla&amp;cq_plt=gp&amp;cq_gclid=CjwKCAjwtKrUBhAhEiwAr77Zok_OS5Jwnm2e2X1pcz7EGhwvO0fx0ZtTuSZvVLmUR8Jey97hWvwBShoCXGcQAvD_BwE&amp;ds_de=c&amp;ds_pc=online&amp;ds_cr=462157525380&amp;ds_tid=pla-297612067635&amp;ds_locphys=9071807&amp;ds_pid=45458&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds&amp;cmpid=smsm:ds:GOOGLE:SUPC-0001_Always%20On_Shopping__Catch%20All_Search_Conversion_8458_815925_FY26_L3_Buy_National-AU:PRODUCT_GROUP&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=11055829892&amp;gbraid=0AAAAACoj6t502kBhmsy2X_lbnfgWkfqnd&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwtKrUBhAhEiwAr77Zok_OS5Jwnm2e2X1pcz7EGhwvO0fx0ZtTuSZvVLmUR8Jey97hWvwBShoCXGcQAvD_BwE">Woolies</a>, <a href="https://www.harrisfarm.com.au/collections/maesri-curry-paste-range/products/maesri-red-curry-paste-10476?_pos=2&amp;_fid=3350a9112&amp;_ss=c">Harris Farms</a>). But any will do here as we only use a little. <em>Tip: If using Maesri cans which can&rsquo;t be resealed, freeze leftovers flattened in a ziplock bag so you can easily break off what you need.</em></p></li>



<li><p><strong>Curry powder</strong> &ndash; This is a key ingredient for a proper satay flavour. Just use regular curry powder form grocery stores, like Clives or India or Keens. Mild, not hot (unless you want it spicy!).</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Oil &ndash; </strong>This is loosen the marinade a bit and also to help it brown, avoiding the need to spray the chicken with oil before baking. Use liquid coconut oil if you have it, else any plain flavoured oil.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Salt and white sugar &ndash; </strong>For seasoning.</p></li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-left" id="h-2-peanut-sauce">2. peanut sauce</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And here&rsquo;s what you need for the peanut sauce. As mentioned above, it&rsquo;s the same ingredients as the peanut sauce for everybody&rsquo;s favourite Thai Chicken Skewers, except thinner so it&rsquo;s more suitable for dunking.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202167" height="857" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Satay-Drumsticks-Ingredients-2.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Coconut milk</strong> &ndash; The rest of the 270ml / 9oz can, else measure out 3/4 cup.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Natural peanut butter &ndash;</strong>&nbsp;The type with no added oil or sugar. Best for stronger peanut flavour and a thinner sauce for dipping. Peanut butter spreads like Bega or Skippy also work, but the sauce will be thicker and milder.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Thai red curry paste &ndash; </strong>See notes in the marinade ingredients section above. We only need 1 tablespoon for the peanut sauce. Don&rsquo;t skip it! It&rsquo;s a key flavouring.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Milk</strong> &ndash; This is what I use to thin the sauce. Chicken stock can also be used. Water can be a fall-back, though I&rsquo;d start with 1/2 cup (concerned about flavour dilution).</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Apple cider vinegar &ndash; </strong>This is for a touch of tang, to balance out the richness. It doesn&rsquo;t make it sour (we only use 1 tablespoon).</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Dark soy sauce &ndash; </strong>Just 1 teaspoon gives the sauce the right satay-brown colour. It doesn&rsquo;t add flavour though, so substitute light/all-purpose soy sauce or 1/4 teaspoon extra salt, though expect the sauce to be paler in colour.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Salt &ndash; </strong>To season the sauce. </p></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-make-satay-chicken-legs-with-peanut-sauce">How to make Satay Chicken Legs with Peanut Sauce</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">No skewering, no chopping &ndash; just some quick slashes to get that tasty satay paste right into the flesh!</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202162" height="331" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Satay-Drumsticks-with-peanut-sauce-1.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><p><strong>Slash</strong> &ndash; Make 3 slashes into the flesh of each drumstick. Do this on the meaty side, not the side that the bone runs closer to. Make the slashes about 5 mm / 1/5&Prime; deep, in the position pictured in the photo above, taking care not to cut all the way through to the bone (that&rsquo;s a bit deep and will open up too much).</p><p>This will help the satay marinade flavour penetrate into the flesh. It&rsquo;s especially important not to skip it if you don&rsquo;t have time to marinate.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202163" height="334" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Satay-Drumsticks-with-peanut-sauce-2.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="2">
<li><p><strong>Satay marinade &ndash; </strong>Mix the ingredients together using a rubber spatula in a bowl large enough to toss the drumsticks.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Toss</strong> the drumsticks in the marinade using the rubber spatula until they are all coated. <strong>Rub</strong> the marinade into the slashes to get the flavour right into the meat! </p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202164" height="335" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Satay-Drumsticks-with-peanut-sauce-3.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="4">
<li><p><strong>Marinate (if you can) &ndash; </strong>Leave the legs to marinade for 3 hours to overnight if you can. 3 hours is enough to get the flavour infusing into the flesh. </p><p>But even if you don&rsquo;t marinate, this is still worth making (I don&rsquo;t always say that about recipes!) because of the slashes, the flavour in the marinade is quite strong and, of course, because you will be dunking the drumsticks into the tasty peanut sauce.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Bake</strong> on a rack set on a tray for 35 minutes, then flick to the grill/broiler on high for a few minutes to get some nice charry bits on the chicken. Don&rsquo;t turn (this is why we use the rack), leaving it undisturbed allows the surface to colour better.</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="How to make Satay Drumsticks with Peanut Sauce" class="wp-image-202165" height="331" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/How-to-make-Satay-Drumsticks-with-peanut-sauce-4.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="6">
<li><p><strong>Peanut sauce &ndash; </strong>Make the sauce while the legs are baking. Just put everything in a medium saucepan, whisk to combine, then let it simmer on medium low for 5 minutes. Don&rsquo;t let it bubble wildly, it should just simmer. Whisk regularly so the base doesn&rsquo;t catch. The sauce should be on the runny side, like maple syrup consistency. Not so thick that you feel like you&rsquo;re dunking the leg into a tub of peanut butter. It should be thin enough so it drips off, but thick enough that it clings generously.</p></li>



<li><p><strong>Serve &ndash; </strong>Pile the legs onto a platter with the sauce on the side. Use a vessel deep enough that you can dunk the whole drumstick in. Sprinkle everything with peanuts, coriander and chilli, if you want a splash of colour. Then dig in!</p></li>
</ol>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Satay chicken legs with peanut sauce" class="wp-image-202152" height="1500" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Satay-chicken-legs-with-peanut-sauce-7.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Satay chicken legs with peanut sauce" class="wp-image-202153" height="1500" src="https://www.recipetineats.com/tachyon/2026/08/Satay-chicken-legs-with-peanut-sauce-0.jpg" width="1200" /></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-matters-of-serving">Matters of serving</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Serve these drumsticks with steamed <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/how-to-cook-jasmine-rice/" id="47533" type="post">jasmine rice</a> on the side &ndash; there&rsquo;s plenty of peanut sauce to drizzle over.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And I&rsquo;ve just added plain tomato and cucumber chunks to get greens into this meal. No dressing, nothing. Very typical of how many dishes are served in Thailand &ndash; simple and fresh, to balance out strong flavoured mains. Though if you want something more substantial, <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/asian-slaw/" id="2991" type="post">Asian Slaw</a> or <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/smashed-cucumber-salad/" id="40623" type="post">Smashed Cucumbers</a> are two that I always reach for. Else any leafy greens or steamed broccoli with <a href="https://www.recipetineats.com/asian-sesame-dressing/" id="18635" type="post">Asian Sesame Dressing</a> (literally takes 90 seconds to make this, and very handy to keep in the fridge as it lasts for weeks and weeks).</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And there you go. Dinner sorted! <strong><em>&ndash; Nagi x</em></strong></p>

<div class="wp-block-rte-accordion-item accordion-item"><h3 class="accordion-item__title wp-block-heading"><button class="accordion-item__button"><span>Can this be made ahead? YES!</span></button></h3><div class="accordion-item__content" hidden="hidden" id="ee83b02d-6be9-4bdd-ac82-fa9dc700bc33">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yes! The chicken can be prepared then marinated overnight in the fridge, or you can freeze it raw (with the satay marinade on it). Then thaw and cook on demand. The peanut sauce can be cooked and kept in the fridge for 4 &ndash; 5 days, or frozen for 3 months. This is a good way to make this if you are cooking just for one or two people, rather than cooking the whole batch and eating leftovers night after night. 🙂</p>

<div class="wprm-recipe-notes-container wprm-block-text-normal" id="recipe-202035-notes"><h3 class="wprm-recipe-header wprm-recipe-notes-header wprm-block-text-bold wprm-align-left wprm-header-decoration-none">Notes</h3><div class="wprm-recipe-notes"><span style="display: block;"><strong>1. Curry powder</strong> &ndash; Any curry powder carried at regular grocery stores is fine, no need to hunt down a special one.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>2. Thai Red Curry Paste</strong> &ndash; Maesri is my preferred brand (photo here), widely available in Australia (Coles, Woolies, Harris Farms). Most authentic flavour and cheapest. However, for this recipe, I&rsquo;m pretty relaxed about the curry paste, use what you&rsquo;ve got!</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>3. Natural peanut butter</strong> is 100% peanuts with no oil, stabilisers or sugar added. It&rsquo;s runnier and has a stronger peanut flavour. If you use a spread like Bega, Skippy, the peanut sauce won&rsquo;t have as strong a peanut flavour and will be thicker.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>4. Dark soy sauce</strong> &ndash; Main purpose is to darken the sauce to make it the proper satay sauce colour we all know and love! Without, it&rsquo;s paler. If you don&rsquo;t have any, that&rsquo;s ok, just use any soy sauce you have but expect a paler sauce.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>5. Skipping the marinade</strong> &ndash; It&rsquo;s ok, the marinade has a lot of flavour and you rubbed it well into the slashes (right? 🙂 ), the legs will still be very flavourful. Plus, peanut sauce!</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>6. Sauce adjustments &ndash;</strong> Simmer longer if loose, add more milk if too thick. Don&rsquo;t let it get too thick otherwise it will coat the chicken too thickly when you dunk it in!</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>7. Other chicken cuts &ndash;&nbsp;</strong>Wings would be great here, same weight, same temp and time, &nbsp;no slashing. For boneless thighs, also don&rsquo;t slash. Bake 20 minutes per temp in the recipe then finish under the grill/broiler for some colour. For breast, split in half horizontally, coat in satay marinade then pan fry, don&rsquo;t bake.</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>8. Gluten-free &ndash;&nbsp;</strong>Use tamari instead of dark soy (sauce will be a bit paler) and make sure the curry paste you use is gluten-free (Maesri is but I haven&rsquo;t checked other brands closely)</span><div class="wprm-spacer"></div>
<span style="display: block;"><strong>Leftovers </strong>will keep 3 &ndash; 4 days in the fridge (sauce and chicken). Not the best for freezing, though the peanut sauce can be frozen for 3 months.
