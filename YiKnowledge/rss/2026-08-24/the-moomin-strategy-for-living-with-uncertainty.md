<h3>&#8220;All things are so very uncertain, and that’s exactly what makes me feel reassured.&#8221;</h3>
<hr />
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img class="cover" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a><strong>Tove Jansson</strong> (August 9, 1914&ndash;June 27, 2001) is among the most imaginative, important, and influential children&#8217;s book creators of all time, an artist and writer of unparalleled creative vision and great sensitivity to life&#8217;s ineffable nuances. A recipient of the prestigious Hans Christian Andersen Award, she had the courage to turn down Walt Disney and build her own creative empire. From her beloved <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/03/26/tove-janssen-moomin-mymble-little-my/">Moomins</a> characters to her spectacular vintage illustrations for <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/07/07/best-illustrations-alice-in-wonderland/"><em>Alice in Wonderland</em></a> and <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/06/13/vintage-hobbit-illustrations/"><em>The Hobbit</em></a>, her stories exude the metaphorical magic of Milne&#8217;s <em>Winnie-the-Pooh</em> tales, the fanciful whimsy of Baum&#8217;s Oz world, the contemplative introspection of Saint-Exupéry&#8217;s <em>The Little Prince</em>, and the enchanting symbolism of Carroll&#8217;s Wonderland. Philip Pullman has aptly called her &#8220;a genius of a very subtle kind&#8221; and Neil Gaiman considers her work &#8220;a surrealist masterpiece.&#8221;</p>
<figure class="wp-caption alignnone"><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/tovejansson1967.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Tove Jansson in 1967 (photograph by Hans Gedda)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Jansson&#8217;s singular sensibility springs from her own unusual life. Born to an artistic and rather eccentric family from Finland&#8217;s Swedish-speaking minority, young Tove was raised by wildly creative parents &#8212; her father was one of Finland&#8217;s greatest sculptors and her mother designed books and postage stamps, illustrated book jackets, and created punchy political cartoons. Jansson completed her formal training in art and graphic design in various institutions across Sweden, Finland, and France, but the origin of her iconic Moomin characters was rooted in an affectionate family joke rather than in her formal training &#8212; while studying in Stockholm in her late teens and living with relatives there, Jansson would regularly sneak into the kitchen for treats; her uncle would tease her that a “Moomintroll” lived in the kitchen pantry, ready to breathe cold air down stealthy snackers&#8217; necks.</p>
<figure class="wp-caption alignnone"><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/tovejansson_selfportrait.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Tove Jansson: self-portrait © Moomin Characters<img alt="™" class="wp-smiley" src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/2122.png" style="height: 1em;" /></figcaption></figure>
<p>Moominvalley&#8217;s main protagonist, Moomintroll, is thus a self-portrait of sorts, but perhaps Jansson&#8217;s most interesting character is also the one based on the most intimate part of her life. Too-ticky, the sage of Moominvalley who solves even the most existential of problems with equal parts practicality and wisdom, was inspired by the love of Jansson&#8217;s life &#8212; the great Finnish sculptor and graphic arts pioneer Tuulikki &#8220;Tooti&#8221; Pietilä, <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2020/06/12/tove-jansson-letters-tuulikki-pietila/">the love of Tove Jansson&#8217;s life</a>. The two women met at the Helsinki Artist League&#8217;s holiday party in the 1950s and remained together until Jansson&#8217;s death decades later, collaborating on a lifetime of creative projects &#8212; all at a time when queer couples were straddling the impossible line between <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/06/19/the-invisibles-sebastien-lifshitz/">anguishing invisibility</a> and <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/10/edith-windsor-debbie-millman-time/">dangerous visibility</a>.</p>
<figure class="wp-caption alignnone"><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/tovetuulikki.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Jansson and Pietilä crafting characters for the television adaptation of the Moomin series.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although Too-ticky, clad in her signature red-and-white sweater, appears in a number of the Moomin books, her spirit blossoms most vibrantly in the 1957 gem <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><strong><em>Moominland Midwinter</em></strong></a> (<a href="http://www.worldcat.org/title/moominland-midwinter/oclc/25411844&#038;referer=brief_results" target="_blank"><em>public library</em></a>), where &#8220;her common sense often restores order in the valley.&#8221; More than mere common sense, however, Too-ticky&#8217;s laconic sagacity and aphoristic reflections are full of invaluable wisdom on life.</p>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter6.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>The book tells the story of Moomintroll who, unlike his family that hibernated from November to April every year, wakes up early and decides to stay up through the harsh Scandinavian winter. He grows angry at the sun&#8217;s absence, angry at the raging blizzards, angry at those who seem able to enjoy rather than resent the season of snow and ice. It is a tale of learning to live with <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/08/05/a-guide-for-the-perplexed-schumacher/">the vital discomfort of uncertainty</a>, to <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/08/04/field-guide-to-getting-lost-rebecca-solnit/">get lost in order to find oneself</a>, to surrender to the rhythms of life rather than agonizing in resistance.</p>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter1.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Lost in the forest, Moomintroll comes upon a warm light emanating from a cozy hole someone had dug for shelter &#8212; &#8220;someone who lay looking up at the serene winter sky and whistling very softly to herself.&#8221; It is, of course, Too-ticky. When Moomintroll inquires about the song she is whistling, she replies, <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/05/13/whitman-illuminated-song-of-myself-allen-crawford/">Whitman-like</a>, with a wonderfully metaphorical answer:</p>
<blockquote><p>It&#8217;s a song of myself&#8230; The refrain is about the things one can’t understand. I’m thinking about the aurora borealis. You can’t tell if it really does exist or if it just looks like existing. All things are so very uncertain, and that’s exactly what makes me feel reassured.</p></blockquote>
<p>This theme of uncertainty and of finding joy in questioning reality is a recurring one for Too-ticky. Echoing the first of Bertrand Russell&#8217;s <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/05/02/a-liberal-decalogue-bertrand-russell/">ten famous commandments of teaching, learning, and life</a> &#8212; <em>&#8220;Do not feel absolutely certain of anything.&#8221;</em> &#8212; she offers comforting solidarity in Moomintroll&#8217;s lament that he doesn&#8217;t understand the snow:</p>
<blockquote><p>I don&#8217;t either&#8230; You believe it’s cold, but if you build yourself a snowhouse it’s warm. You think it’s white, but at times it looks pink, and another time it’s blue. It can be softer than anything, and then again harder than stone. Nothing is certain.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter3.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>In many ways, Too-ticky&#8217;s wisdom seems almost Zen Buddhist in nature. In addition to championing the ability to be at peace with uncertainty, she also advocates a minimalist approach to material possessions &#8212; when Moomintroll discovers, distraught and indignant, that someone is secretly smuggling things out of his sleeping family&#8217;s house, Too-ticky responds:</p>
<blockquote><p>That’s nice, isn’t it? You’ve got too many things about you. As well as things you remember, and things you’re dreaming about.</p></blockquote>
<p>Too-ticky is also a sage of the <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/04/30/rachel-sussman-interview/">&#8220;slow churn&#8221;</a> and wise champion of the idea that <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/10/23/7-lessons-from-7-years/">&#8220;anything worthwhile takes a long time.&#8221;</a> (Janssen would certainly know &#8212; she wrote her first Moomin book, <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moomins-Great-Flood-Tove-Jansson/dp/1908745134/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><em>The Moomins and the Great Flood</em></a>, in 1939 and published it in 1945, but it was not a success; her first critical acclaim arrived in 1946, nearly a decade after she had created the Moomins, with the publication of <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Comet-Moominland-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312608888/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><em>Comet in Moominland</em></a>.) When Moomintroll grows angry and impatient with the sun&#8217;s refusal to rise, Too-ticky reminds him that <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/09/26/alan-watts-hurrying-timing">hurrying is a toxic way of trying to resist the present</a>:</p>
<blockquote><p>Don’t be in such a hurry&#8230; Soon now. Sit down and wait.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter12.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>When the sun does appear, it flits across the horizon for a fleeting moment, only to set back down. Moomintroll is even more frustrated, but Too-ticky assures him that the sun, like <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/05/23/the-long-game/">the myth of the overnight success</a>, follows an incremental rise to brilliance:</p>
<blockquote><p>He’ll return tomorrow&#8230; And then he’ll be a tiny bit bigger, about like a piece of cheese rind. Take it easy.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter11.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>The story is also a gentle primer on evolution. When Moomintroll, against Too-ticky&#8217;s instruction, opens her secret cabinet and finds a strange creature living there, he tells her it was &#8220;only a sort of old rat,&#8221; but she corrects him:</p>
<blockquote><p>That was no rat. It was a troll. A troll of the kind you were yourself before you became a Moomin. That was how you looked a thousand years ago.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter4.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Moomintroll is so unsettled by the notion that he is related to a mere rat &#8212; an elegant allegory for why some people are drawn to such defensive fancies as Young Earth creationism &#8212; he storms into the attic to look for an old family album. Janssen writes:</p>
<blockquote><p>Page after page of dignified Moomins, most often reproduced standing in front of porcelain stoves, or on fret-worked verandahs. Not a single one of them resembled the cupboard troll. “Must be a mistake,” Moomintroll thought. “He can’t be any relation of mine.&#8221;</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter5.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Slowly, Moomintroll makes peace with Too-ticky&#8217;s knowledge:</p>
<blockquote><p>He went down and looked at his sleeping Pappa. Only the nose bore some resemblance to the troll’s. But possibly, a thousand years ago.</p></blockquote>
<p>There is almost a cosmology element to this undercurrent &#8212; a reminder that, however discomfiting this too may be to most humans, we are indeed <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/02/04/accidental-universe-alan-lightman/">a cosmic accident</a>. Janssen traces the evolution of Moomintroll&#8217;s understanding:</p>
<blockquote><p>Suddenly he felt very proud of having an ancestor. And it cheered him no little to think that Little My [Moomintroll&#8217;s sister] had no pedigree at all, but rather had come into the world by chance.</p></blockquote>
<p>But perhaps her most profound wisdom deals with our quintessential <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/09/17/sherwin-nuland-how-we-die/">struggle to make peace with death</a>, which stems from an inability to recognize the <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2010/11/11/wabi-sabi/">comforting interconnectedness of life</a>. When the Lady of the Cold &#8212; the beautiful but formidable priestess of the Great Cold, capable of turning into an icicle any fool so bewitched as to look straight into her eyes &#8212; freezes the cheerful little squirrel Moomintroll had befriended, Too-ticky sighs:</p>
<blockquote><p>It’s very hard to tell if people take any pleasure in their tails when they’re dead.</p></blockquote>
<p>Death, too, is part of nature&#8217;s <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2010/11/11/wabi-sabi/">necessary cycles of growth and decay</a>. When Moomintroll and Little My remonstrate the very mention of death, Too-ticky responds:</p>
<blockquote><p>When one’s dead, then one’s dead. This squirrel will become earth all in his time. And later on still there’ll grow trees from him, with new squirrels skipping about in them. Do you think that’s so very sad?</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter7.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Too-ticky&#8217;s greatest gift, it appears, is a certain quality of presence &#8212; the kind she cultivated in &#8220;her own private winter world that had followed its own strange rules year after year&#8221; &#8212; that allows her to feel one with the world. It is from that standpoint that, when spring finally arrives, she responds to Moomintroll&#8217;s accusation that she hadn&#8217;t comforted him during the long winter by offering assurance that spring will come, but instead focused on what the world had to offer right there and then. Too-ticky&#8217;s answer, emanating a kind of Emerson-like ideal of <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/08/18/werner-herzog-guide-for-the-perplexed-cronin/">self-reliance</a>, rings with extraordinary, if uncomfortable, poignancy:</p>
<blockquote><p>One has to discover everything for oneself. And get over it all alone.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter10.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Moomintroll imbibes Too-ticky&#8217;s existential lesson. Soon, when his friend the Snork Maiden comes across &#8220;the first brave nose-tip of a crocus&#8221; shyly trying to push through snow, she suggests they put a glass over it to protect it from the frost at night. But Moomintroll objects:</p>
<blockquote><p>No, don’t do that. Let it fight it out. I believe it’s going to do still better if things aren’t so easy.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/moominlandmidwinter13.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Decades before the groundbreaking research on <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/03/28/angela-duckworth-grit-success/">why cultivating grit is the greatest key to success</a>, Jansson made the same point with great subtlety and wisdom.</p>
<p><a href="http://www.amazon.com/Moominland-Midwinter-Moomins-Tove-Jansson/dp/0312625413/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><strong><em>Moominland Midwinter</em></strong></a> is an immeasurably wonderful read in its entirety, as are all of Jansson&#8217;s Moomin books. For another taste, see <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/03/26/tove-janssen-moomin-mymble-little-my/">my favorite one</a>.</p>
