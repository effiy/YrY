<p>The <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/apple-new-airtag" target="_blank"><u>biggest upgrade</u></a> you&rsquo;ll find over the first-generation AirTag is Apple&rsquo;s newer Ultra Wideband chip, which the company says gives Precision Finding up to 50% more range than before. That means your iPhone can start pointing you toward a nearby AirTag from farther away, using on-screen arrows, haptic feedback, and sound as you get closer. Apple has also made the built-in speaker 50% louder, which can be especially helpful once Precision Finding has guided you into the right area&mdash;a stronger sound makes the AirTag easier to pinpoint when it is buried under clothes, tucked inside a bag, or wedged between couch cushions.</p><p>Apple Watch owners get a nice upgrade too, as Precision Finding now works with the Apple Watch Series 9 and newer and the Apple Watch Ultra 2 and newer, allowing you to track down a nearby AirTag directly from your wrist without reaching for your iPhone. Once your trackers are set up, you can get even more out of them with a few lesser-known tricks, which we cover in our <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/hacks-every-airtag-user-should-know" target="_blank"><u>AirTag hacks</u></a> guide.</p><p>That said, the rest of the experience is largely unchanged&mdash;it still uses a replaceable CR2032 battery, carries the same IP67 water- and dust-resistance rating, and keeps the same dimensions, so holders and keychains should continue to fit. Owners of the original AirTag probably will not find enough here to <a href="https://lifehacker.com/tech/your-iphone-might-not-be-fully-compatible-with-apples-new-airtag" target="_blank"><u>justify an upgrade</u></a>, but for first-time buyers, this $79.99 four-pack is an affordable way to get the newer model without spending much more than older AirTag multipacks have typically cost.</p><hr /><div class=" relative flex justify-center py-16 md:left-1/2 md:w-[780px] md:max-w-max md:-translate-x-1/2">
