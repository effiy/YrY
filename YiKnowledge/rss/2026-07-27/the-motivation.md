<p><img alt="The Motivation" class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual" height="535" src="https://randsinrepose.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/motivation-1024x535.png" width="1024" /></p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Why do I write? I write because I like the mental state created by writing. It calms and quiets me, but that is not why I write.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Why do I write? I write because I am curious and like to understand the world. Writing forces me to understand a thing, a person, a situation. I don&#8217;t think many humans do this, but I am addicted to it. But that is not why I write.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Why do I write? I write because I like helping. People are often the worst, but I have a desire to help. This internal contradiction is confusing. Humans who approach me looking for Rands, but find Lopp, are disappointed and confused because Rands helped them and Lopp apparently couldn&#8217;t care less? Introvert. But helping is not why I write.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Why do I write? I deeply believe it is important for humans to build stuff. I think this is a primary reason we are on the planet, and I think that the ways a human can build are as varied as the humans who do the building. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Failure to build is abhorrent to me. Much of the anger on the planet right now is from humans who aren&#8217;t building, believing they are owed a free lunch because they showed up, or they know better, or some other poorly informed worldview they acquired without effort, without curiosity&#8230; just because they exist in their particular circumstances.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Writing is how I build. <strong>That&#8217;s why I write</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You will never do 150% of a thing if you do not have a version of these motivations. 15% effort means you have not found your reasons yet.</p>
