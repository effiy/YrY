<h3><strong>Reviews &amp; Mini-Views</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>Emio &#8211; The Smiling Man: Famicom Detective Club ($49.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328360" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/emiothesmilingmanfamicomdetectiveclub.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>It seems like the latest trend is making sequels to decades-dormant franchises. Video games, always trying to be like Hollywood. Nintendo has rather unpredictably decided to resurrect<em> Famicom Detective Club</em>, a series mainly known in the West through the &#8220;blink and you&#8217;ll miss it" remake of the first two games that came out on Switch a couple of years back. I feel like there may be a connection here. At any rate, for the first time this millennium we have a brand new<em> Famicom Detective Club</em> adventure! That&#8217;s nifty.</p>
<p>The challenge with bringing back an old brand is in deciding how faithful to the original the new game should be. The risk of being too faithful is that the game could be seen as old-fashioned or out of date, but change too much and there&#8217;s a chance fans could be completely turned off. <em>Emio &#8211; The Smiling Man: Famicom Detective Club</em> leans more toward maintaining the style of the recent remakes, themselves hewing quite closely to the originals. It&#8217;s an odd blend, to be sure. The visuals are as good as anything you would see in similar games from modern times, and the story certainly pushes a little further than what the Nintendo of the 90s would have done even in Japan. But the gameplay feels very old-school, and I think that is a determining factor in whether or not you enjoy this game.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328737" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/emio2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>A student is found dead with a paper bag over his head that has a smiling face on it. This digs up some old unsolved murders from eighteen years ago that featured a similar calling card, which raises all manner of questions. Then there is the urban legend about Emio, a killer who promises to give his victims an eternal smile. Has a previous killer resurfaced after all this time? Is it a copycat? Is there really any such thing as Emio? The cops are stumped, so it&#8217;s time to call in the Utsugi Detective Agency! Using the powers of going around to places and badgering people, you&#8217;ll do what the law enforcement was powerless to: find the truth!</p>
<p>Basically, you&#8217;re searching around every scene for clues, talking to all of the people you meet (sometimes you need to press the issue and ask multiple times to get what you want), and trying to find the connections you&#8217;ll need to solve the case. Think of the sections of <em>Ace Attorney</em> outside of court and you&#8217;re not too far off the mark. Depending on how well you take to this kind of gameplay, you mind find parts of the game to be annoying or a slog. There are certainly bits I feel could have been handled more smoothly, and it feels like specific chains of logic needed just a little more signposting. Well, this is a Cat Hair Mustache genre, and I suppose by that standards <em>Emio</em> isn&#8217;t doing anything particularly egregious.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328738" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/emio3.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>And you know what? That&#8217;s where I&#8217;m going to leave it. I have some criticisms of the story, but I enjoyed it on the whole. It was engaging, twisty, and well-written. Some of the plot beats didn&#8217;t hit me the way it seems they hit others, but I can&#8217;t really talk about that in detail without ruining the story. And I really don&#8217;t want to do that, because this is a tale that is best experienced fresh. I liked more than I didn&#8217;t, and when it picks up it really picks up.</p>
<p><em>Emio &#8211; The Smiling Man: Famicom Detective Club</em> is not the sort of thing we typically see from Nintendo, but if there was any rust on the team it assuredly doesn&#8217;t show. It&#8217;s perhaps a little too adherent to the original games in its mechanics, and while the plot is mostly excellent there are some times where the pace falls off or things resolve in ways I didn&#8217;t find as satisfying as I would have ideally hoped for. Still, these are minor complaints for what is otherwise a thoroughly enjoyable mystery adventure. Welcome back, <em>Detective Club</em>. Don&#8217;t stay away so long this time.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Splintered Fate ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-326258" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/teenagemutantninjaturtlessplinteredfate.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>We&#8217;re starting to get a nice little set of <em>TMNT</em> games on the Switch, aren&#8217;t we? You can enjoy the Konami classics in the <em>Cowabunga Collection</em>, get an incredible modern take on the arcade beat &#8217;em up formula with<em> Shredder&#8217;s Revenge</em>, bring the modern arcade home in <em>Wrath of the Mutants</em>, and now satisfy your urge for something with a little more home console flavoring in <em>Splintered Fate</em>. There are a couple more on the way, too. We&#8217;re swimming in Turtles! <em>With</em> Turtles? Turtles, they&#8217;re everywhere. So how is this one?</p>
<p>Not too shabby, actually. I mean, if you&#8217;ve played this on Apple Arcade then you know the deal, and if you&#8217;re reading this site then the odds are somewhat decent you have. But just in case, let&#8217;s go over the broad pitch. Basically, imagine taking a <em>TMNT</em>-style beat &#8217;em up and blending it with <em>Hades</em>. Alright, that pretty much does it. You can go it alone in the usual roguelite style, or play with up to four players via local or online multiplayer. Mikhail and I did the online multiplayer thing together and it worked rather well, so that&#8217;s a good way to go. The game is fine when you&#8217;re on your own, but adding more people predictably improves the experience. That&#8217;s <em>TMNT</em> for you.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328736" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/tmntsplinteredfate2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Anyway, some kind of shenanigans occur with Shredder and a mysterious power. Splinter is in trouble, and the Turtles have to try to figure out how to save him. When all you have is a katana, every problem looks like a Foot Soldier. Slice, dice, and/or bludgeon whatever enemies come in front of you, dash tactically to avoid attacks, pick up some perks that you can enjoy in your current run, and collect another currency to score some permanent upgrades. If you die, it&#8217;s back to the lair to try again. Roguelite beat &#8217;em up. But with the Turtles, so it&#8217;s automatically better than it would be without them. It isn&#8217;t particularly inspired, but it does its job well enough.</p>
<p>I wouldn&#8217;t call <em>Splintered Fate</em> a must-have in general, but <em>TMNT</em> fans will probably enjoy this twist on the usual fare. The multiplayer is well-implemented, and it&#8217;s good to see that important <em>TMNT</em> element wasn&#8217;t passed over despite this being the kind of game that is usually played solo. Those without an affinity for the Turtles can find better roguelite games to play on the Switch, but with this being such an intensely competitive genre on the platform it&#8217;s to the credit of <em>Splintered Fate</em> that it can manage to keep pace at all. Not bad.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 3.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Nour: Play With Your Food ($9.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328677" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nour-play-with-your-food-switch-review-main.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>When <em>Nour: Play With Your Food</em> originally released on PC and PS5, I was a bit surprised to see it not also released on Switch and mobile. It felt like a perfect fit for touchscreens as an experimental food art experience. I played it on PC and liked it quite a bit, but this isn&#8217;t a traditional game for everyone. If you enjoy playful sandbox-like experiences and love food, you will likely find a lot to love in <em>Nour: Play With Your Food</em>, but the Switch version is a bit lacking.</p>
<p>If you&#8217;re new to <em>Nour: Play With Your Food</em>, it has you playing with specific food types across different stages with interesting music and a lot of over the top nonsense (compliment). It feels like a combination of an interactive app made for those who love food and art equally. Initially, you only get access to the basics, but I found myself surprised at how much the developers added to let you literally play with your food as the name suggests. This is when I realized why it might not be as easy to play on a touchscreen.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328681" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/nour-play-with-your-food-switch-review-.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>I am disappointed in the lack of touchscreen support on Switch though. Aside from that, <em>Nour: Play With Your Food</em> was a bit of a heavy game on Steam Deck as well, and you can see the cutbacks on Switch to have it run decently. The biggest issue is the load times being long both docked and handheld on Switch.</p>
<p><em>Nour: Play With Your Food</em> is worth experiencing if you love food, art, and interactive apps. While the Switch version isn&#8217;t the best way to experience it, Nour still feels best on a portable, and I hope it does well enough to get more DLC or even a physical release. We don&#8217;t see many releases like this in gaming, but games like <em>Nour</em> and <em>Townscaper</em> are the perfect compliment to more-involved RPGs and story-driven games. <em>-Mikhail Madnani</em></p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 3.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Fate/stay night REMASTERED ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328682" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/fate-stay-night-remastered-switch-review-main.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> launched about a month ago on Nintendo Switch and Steam worldwide. I was hoping to have it covered sooner, but the month has been really busy and<em> Fate/stay night</em> itself is very long. Before covering the game itself, I want to take you on a bit of a journey. Many years ago, a close friend convinced me to watch<em> Fate/Zero</em> as one of the first few anime I watched from start to finish. Back then, I wanted to experience more from the universe, but there wasn&#8217;t really something interesting I could officially play in English. I ended up importing the PS Vita version of<em> Fate/stay night Realta Nua</em> just to own it, and since then have basically been playing whatever <em>Fate</em>-related release or Type Moon property we see in the West. This includes <em>Fate/EXTELLA, Fate/Samurai Remnant</em>, watching<em> Fate/stay night</em>, and even trying out <em>Fate/Grand Order</em> for a bit.</p>
<p>All of that led to me being very glad that Aniplex brought over <em>Witch on the Holy Night</em> and <em>Tsukihime</em>&#8216;s remake recently, but there was the one game I was hoping we&#8217;d see released in English somehow. I didn&#8217;t bother with fan translations, and kept hoping we&#8217;d see<em> Fate/stay night</em> in English. Here we are. It still feels unreal that this is in English and on Switch. But is it worth your time and money? Well I can save you some time and say absolutely on both fronts with a few caveats.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328684" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/fate-stay-night-remastered-switch-review-3.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> is a remaster of the 2004-released Type Moon visual novel covering the story of Emiya Shirou, the Holy Grail war, and more. This is basically the best entry point into the Fate universe, and I know there are many who have only experienced it through anime and other games so far, so this is now a chance to see the origin of the series as it was meant to be. <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> even with its few quality of life features is still a 55+ hour experience at least, and that makes the low asking price shocking. I get that this is a remaster of an older game, but the volume of text and work done really makes it one of the best value releases on the eShop this year.</p>
<p>If you already played the original versions of <em>Fate/stay night</em> in Japanese, <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> does a lot to improve the experience. Obviously it adds English, but the 16:9 support is welcome. It also feels like a lot more work went into this than I initially expected. I&#8217;ve been playing a bit of the PS Vita release to compare, and the developers of this remaster have done a great job at making <em>Fate/stay night</em> look good on modern displays, but don&#8217;t expect the gorgeousness of <em>Tsukihime</em>&#8216;s recent remake.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328667" height="2160" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/fate-stay-night-remastered-switch-vs-steam-deck-review-.jpg" width="3840" /></p>
<p>While I love <em>Fate/stay night</em> itself, I&#8217;m glad that the developers actually put in touchscreen support on Nintendo Switch. I played most of <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> on my Switch Lite (which is sadly busted now) and then synced it to my Switch OLED to play at home and also docked. It feels perfect on Nintendo&#8217;s hybrid system. Hopefully it gets more platform releases in the future like iOS and PS5 so more can experience it.</p>
<p>Speaking of more platforms, I also played a good amount of <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> on Steam Deck. It works perfectly out of the box. If you were wondering where to play <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> assuming you owned a Steam Deck or Switch, get it wherever you want but make sure you play it. It is that good.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328683" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/fate-stay-night-remastered-switch-review-4.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>There&#8217;s nothing really holding <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> back right now aside from no physical release on Switch, and I hope it does well enough to get one in the future to go on my shelf with <em>Witch on the Holy Night</em> and <em>Tsukihime</em>.</p>
<p><em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> is basically an essential for visual novel fans and I still can&#8217;t believe it is not only available in English, but also on both Switch and Steam. The low asking price makes it an even easier recommendation. While it may not look as good as <em>Tsukihime</em>&#8216;s recent remake, <em>Fate/stay night REMASTERED</em> is still worth your time, and I&#8217;m glad I finally got to play it in English after all these years of owning the Japanese PS Vita release. <em>-Mikhail Madnani</em></p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>TOKYO CHRONOS &amp; ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos TWIN PACK ($49.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328672" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/TOKYO-CHRONOS-ALTDEUS-Beyond-Chronos-TWIN-PACK-switch-review-main.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p>As someone who didn&#8217;t bother with VR outside of borrowing a PS VR headset on PS4 Pro years ago, I know I&#8217;ve ended up missing out on some great games. When looking at what my friends who own current VR headsets talk about, in addition to games like <em>Thumper</em> or <em>Beat Saber</em>, I used to see rumblings of <em>TOKYO CHRONOS</em> and <em>ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos</em> mentioned. Both were praised for their stories and also for being full experiences in VR. I never got to experience the stories until <em>TOKYO CHRONOS &amp; ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos TWIN PACK</em> hit Switch through Izanagi Games just over a month ago.</p>
<p>When you first boot up <em>TOKYO CHRONOS &amp; ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos TWIN PACK</em> on Switch, you get to choose which game you want to play. The first game has you experiencing the story of high school friends in an alternate version of Shibuya, lost memories, killers, and more. If you&#8217;ve played many visual novels or adventure games, the narrative here will feel very predictable in parts, but it isn&#8217;t bad. It has good visuals and I feel like trying it out in VR after seeing how it feels on Switch just for the novelty of a full visual novel in VR.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328674" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/TOKYO-CHRONOS-ALTDEUS-Beyond-Chronos-TWIN-PACK-switch-review-3.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos</em> on the other hand is a lot better with its production, music, writing, voice acting, and characters in general. It also goes beyond being a pure visual novel in parts, and I love when developers do this. Punching above your weight in a visual novel usually makes for a more memorable experience when coupled with important story beats. <em>ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos</em> is the standout of the two, but I think the pack is definitely worth buying if you&#8217;re ok with the controls and movement in the demo.</p>
<p>Aside from some aspects of the stories not hitting as hard, the Switch version has some performance issues when it comes to camera movement. These aren&#8217;t a dealbreaker, but it is distracting having that happen during first person and other encounters. The rest of the Switch features more than make up for it though with touchscreen support (my favorite feature in adventure games) and good rumble.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328675" height="759" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/TOKYO-CHRONOS-ALTDEUS-Beyond-Chronos-TWIN-PACK-switch-review-2.jpg" width="1350" /></p>
<p><em>TOKYO CHRONOS &amp; ALTDEUS: Beyond Chronos TWIN PACK</em> is a great experience on Nintendo Switch with its touch controls and rumble support added to make it even more immersive. I&#8217;m glad I got to finally experience these stories since I never owned a VR headset, and I hope to see more from this team in the future. If you enjoy sci-fi stories, I urge you to download the demo at least to see how this one feels on Switch. <em>-Mikhail Madnani</em></p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
