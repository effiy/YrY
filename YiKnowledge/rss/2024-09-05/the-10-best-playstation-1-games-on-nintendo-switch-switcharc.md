<p>Well here it is, friends. This will be the last of my retro game eShop lists, mostly because I am running out of retro consoles with a good variety of games to choose from. I&#8217;ve left one of the best for last, though: the PlayStation. Sony&#8217;s first whack at the ol&#8217; console market succeeded beyond any expectations, and it&#8217;s little wonder it built a library so full of great games that we still see re-releases to this day. Sure, these games gave Nintendo a black eye a quarter of a century ago, but it&#8217;s all smiles now for everyone who can enjoy them on their platforms of choice. We&#8217;ve got ten of our favorites here, in no particular order of course. On with the PlaySta-Show!</p>
<h3><strong>Klonoa: The Door to Phantomile &#8211; Klonoa Phantasy Reverie Series ($39.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-294825" height="770" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/klonoaphantasyreverieseries.jpg" width="1368" /></p>
<p><em>Klonoa</em> is a really great game that probably didn&#8217;t get as much attention as it deserved, but got enough that it didn&#8217;t go totally unnoticed. Probably one of the more successful 2.5D platformers on the console, even. You play as a little floppy-eared cat-creature who is navigating the world of dreams to stop a dangerous threat. Vibrant visuals, snappy gameplay, interesting bosses, and a surprisingly impactful story for what it is. The second game, originally released on PlayStation 2, isn&#8217;t quite as good as the first, but you have to get them as a set anyway.</p>
<h3><strong>Final Fantasy VII ($15.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-250515" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/finalfantasy7.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>This is one of the big ones, isn&#8217;t it? The Japanese RPG that finally woke the wider Western market up to the genre, Square Enix&#8217;s biggest success ever, and one of the main instruments of the PlayStation&#8217;s rocket to the top of the business. Sure, there is that remake of <em>Final Fantasy VII</em> out there, but I hope by now that people realize what that really is. If you want to play the original <em>Final Fantasy VII</em> story, you&#8217;ll have to risk getting your eye poked out on some sharp polygons. Still a very good game, and it&#8217;s not hard to see why so many people took to it the way they did.</p>
<h3><strong>Metal Gear Solid &#8211; Master Collection Version ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328782" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/metalgearsolidhd.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Another very big name from the PlayStation, <em>Metal Gear Solid</em> dragged a franchise that was more or less dormant into the spotlight of a much bigger stage. The <em>Metal Gear Solid</em> series got a lot sillier over time, and perhaps started gazing into its own belly button a little too deeply. But it&#8217;s hard to argue with this first game, which feels less like a bizarre trip through Kojima&#8217;s philosophical musings and more like an episode of<em> GI Joe</em>. It doesn&#8217;t hurt that it&#8217;s a very fun game to play, either. And hey, if you enjoy it enough, both of the PlayStation 2 follow-ups are available on Switch too.</p>
<h3><strong>G-Darius HD ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-285462" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/gdariushd2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Alright, let&#8217;s venture into the weeds again just a little bit.<em> G-Darius</em> took Taito&#8217;s classic shoot &#8217;em up series into 3D, and it&#8217;s amazing just how well it made that transition. Sure, those chunky rough-textured polygons haven&#8217;t aged as well as the sprites in the 2D games, but there&#8217;s a real charm to them. The vivid colors, enjoyable enemy-catching mechanic, and creative bosses make for one heck of a solid shooter.</p>
<h3><strong>Chrono Cross: The Radical Dreamers Edition ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-291470" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/chronocrosstheradicaldreamers.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I could almost fill this entire list with Square Enix games, but I&#8217;m going to leave it with this one and <em>Final Fantasy VII</em> just to give other games a chance. <em>Chrono Cross</em> had an impossible job: follow up one of the most well-liked Japanese RPGs ever made. And no, it couldn&#8217;t quite manage that task. People today do not speak of <em>Chrono Cross</em> the way they do<em> Chrono Trigger</em>, and that&#8217;s fair. But pull it away from that comparison and you&#8217;ll find a really clever and gorgeous RPG with a huge cast of (admittedly poorly developed in the main) characters to recruit and play with. Also, one of the best video game soundtracks of all time. Feel free to @ me, I&#8217;m right.</p>
<h3><strong>Mega Man X4 &#8211; Mega Man X Legacy Collection ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328783" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/megamanx4.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I love just about every <em>Mega Man</em> game to some extent, but I am well aware that my vision is clouded by bias stemming from a severe case of childhood nostalgia. As such, when it comes to recommending games in the series with clearer eyes, there are only a few games in each series I feel good about putting in the hands of non-fans. With the <em>Mega Man X</em> series, it&#8217;s just two games: <em>Mega Man X</em> and <em>Mega Man X4</em>. While I enjoyed the games between them, <em>X4</em> just feels like it has itself put together better than most of its siblings. This was a short-lived slice of balance, because things went off the rails again with the next game. But hey, don&#8217;t take my word for it &#8211; buy the <em>Legacy Collections</em> and see for yourself!</p>
<h3><strong>Tomba! Special Edition ($19.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-327411" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/tomba-2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>You know, there were a lot of games published by Sony as first-party releases that it ended up not owning. I always assumed <em>Tomba</em> was as much a first-party property as<em> Crash Bandicoot</em> was, but&#8230; oh wait, I guess it <em>was</em>. Anyway, <em>Tomba!</em> is a really interesting platformer. It incorporates a lot of adventure game elements, but the action is on point too. Just don&#8217;t forget that that creator of<em> Tomba!</em> is the same guy behind <em>Ghosts &#8216;n Goblins</em>. It might seem breezy at first, but it&#8217;s going to challenge you as it goes on. Very neat game, and I&#8217;m glad it&#8217;s available again.</p>
<h3><strong>Grandia &#8211; Grandia HD Collection ($39.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-257162" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/grandiahd3.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Okay, technically <em>Grandia</em> was a SEGA Saturn game first, but the PlayStation port was clearly used as the basis of this HD release so I&#8217;m putting it here. <em>Grandia</em> comes from many of the same people who made <em>Lunar</em>, and it channels much of its spirit. In a time period where many RPGs were taking heavy cues from <em>Evangelion</em>, <em>Grandia</em> dared to be a bright and cheerful adventure. It also has a very satisfying combat system that builds on what Game Arts was doing with the <em>Lunar</em> games in strong ways. The other game in this set isn&#8217;t too shabby either.</p>
<h3><strong>Tomb Raider &#8211; Tomb Raider I-III Remastered Starring Lara Croft ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-318890" height="450" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/tombraider1-3remastered1.jpg" width="800" /></p>
<p>Another true icon of the PlayStation era, Lara Croft had five adventures on the PlayStation by the time the console reached its end. The quality varied from game to game, and some of them were stronger at some things and weaker at others. If you asked me to pick the best one, I think I&#8217;d have to go with the original. It was more focused on actual raiding of tombs and less on action, and I think that was putting the best foot forward as far as the Core Design period of the franchise went. But this is another chance to make up your own mind, because you get the first three games here whether you like it or not.</p>
<h3><strong>moon ($18.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-271501" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/moon2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>Let&#8217;s finish up with a deeper cut. Originally only released in Japan, moon was something of a deconstruction of the contemporary RPG. Indeed, its creators refer to it as an anti-RPG. In practice, it&#8217;s more of an adventure game than anything else, and it is very&#8230; <em>punk</em>? Can I call it punk? There are parts of the game that aren&#8217;t very fun at all, but it&#8217;s also not trying to be fun all the time. There&#8217;s an interesting message to this game if you see it through, and I&#8217;m glad we finally got to see this come out in English.</p>
