<p><img alt="The Apple Upgrade Situation" class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual" height="535" src="https://randsinrepose.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/the-apple-upgrade-situation-1024x535.png" width="1024" /></p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The final measure of any financial negotiation is the feeling of whether or not you got a &#8220;deal&#8221;.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That prior sentence&#8230; makes no sense.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I wrote &#8220;measure,&#8221; and then six words later I wrote &#8220;feeling&#8221;. A feeling is never a measure, but yet, when you hand over your hard-earned money to buy a thing, you&#8217;ll feel better because you believe the value received is higher than the dollars paid.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Too abstract. Here&#8217;s a good example:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I have felt since it was announced that the <a href="https://www.apple.com/shop/iphone/iphone-upgrade-program">Apple iPhone Upgrade Program</a> has not just been a deal, but a steal. The specifics:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>You apply for a loan for a full-price iPhone at 0%.</li>



<li>If approved, your payments are split over 24 months.</li>



<li>Pay for 24 months, and the phone is yours.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That was just the deal; the steal was that it was low effort every single year to get a new phone. I&#8217;d re-up for a new phone, and Apple would forgive the remaining payments of the loan because I&#8217;d signed up for another 24 months. Oh, and bonus, AppleCare was included, which was a total steal because I hate iPhone cases (iPhones are designed to be felt) and, uh, also I have been known to drop my phone.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Felt fair. The Apple bet was that users would embrace the original plan, were going to re-up regularly, and Apple would refurb the phone on a strong used market. That bet <a href="https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-upgrade-launches-in-the-united-states/">ended on a Tuesday</a>. Apple retired the Upgrade Program and replaced it with <a href="https://www.apple.com/shop/apple-upgrade">Apple Upgrade</a>: a Klarna-financed lease covering iPhones, Watches, iPads, and Macs. The loan became a lease. And it feels different.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Follow the AppleCare Money</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At first glance, the new plan looks cheaper. The original plan: one year of iPhone 17 Pro (256GB, $1,099 sticker) was ~$57/month. 12 payments, trade-in, restart: <strong>~$684 for the year</strong>. The new one, same phone: $45.99/month on the 12-month lease: <strong>$552 for the year</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But wait! The first and most important change to the new plan is that AppleCare is <strong>no longer included</strong>. Adding it back to this phone at $13.99/month: <strong>$720 for the year</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But hold it! You can pay for AppleCare annually at $139.99, which brings the year to $692 — <strong>eight dollars from the original plan</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But wait once more! You have <a href="https://www.apple.com/applecare/">AppleCare One</a>? Well, good news, your new iPhone can ride along in a $5.99/month slot, bringing us to <strong>$624 for the year</strong>. In fact, and illuminatingly so, the only way to get the good price on iPhone-plus-AppleCare is to get three pieces of hardware on the AppleCare One plan, which means having three pieces of hardware.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This might sound reasonable until you think about the number of times I&#8217;ve never had the opportunity to drop my Mac Studio. For all Mac hardware in general, there was that one time Rands Jr. somehow cracked her MacBook screen. That one time — in a couple of decades.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">AppleCare is insurance. Like all insurance, you pay for protection from expensive, but hopefully unlikely disasters. In the event of the unlikely, you don&#8217;t pay. My issue with this approach is it appears Apple is removing AppleCare from the upgrade plan and herding folks towards AppleCare One because margins on insurance are sweet — like 2x the margins on hardware. I already pay Apple insurance. It&#8217;s not called insurance; it&#8217;s called &#8220;incredibly high quality hardware.&#8221; I gleefully pay Apple a premium not just because of its cutting-edge design, but also exemplary quality. With Dell, I pay for the insurance because the motherboard on my fancy Alienware gaming PC has already failed once.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">It Still Feels Like a Deal, Not a Steal</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When I buy a ticket at United, just before I click purchase, there is a must-acknowledge box I am required to read. It is a light-fear-inducing reminder that &#8220;Stuff changes when you are flying. You never know, but&#8230; maybe plop down a couple of hundred dollars because, well, you never know and we know better so maybe yeah, pay us money, and we&#8217;ll make it ok.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Never in the history of ever have I done this. Yes, in the middle of winter when travel plans are truly up in the air, I think about it, but mostly&#8230; gross. This is a business trying to eke out high-margin dollars just before the sale when you are most worried about the value of your purchase.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is genuine value in AppleCare, and I&#8217;ve considered AppleCare One because the Rands house has a lot of hardware, but the more I stare at the new plan, the more I feel a little gross.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I&#8217;ll stick with the plan for the phone along with additional AppleCare (not AppleCare One) because, remember, it&#8217;s whether or not I feel I got a deal. The primary intangible is that Apple is quite good at operations. The process of re-upping for the new phone has historically been trivial.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>After the new phone has been announced, click a couple of things in the Store app, and you&#8217;re done.</li>



<li>New phone arrives promptly along with a pre-labeled return box for your old phone.</li>



<li>Drop that box off at FedEx. And done.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">New phone sitting at the door. Almost like magic. Almost.</p>
