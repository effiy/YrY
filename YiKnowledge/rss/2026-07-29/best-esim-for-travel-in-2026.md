<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is an eSIM, and why do you need one?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>An eSIM is the modern version of a SIM card.</strong> This is the device that connects your phone to the local phone network and allows you to use the internet when out and about, without connecting to wifi.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, eSIMs are different from SIM card because instead of a little plastic card you insert into your phone, you simply download them digitally.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">No store visit necessary &#8212; AND you can do it from home before your trip!</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>One thing to know is that while almost all current smartphones support eSIMs, some older models do not.</strong> The iPhone XS and later, the Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, the Google Pixel 3 and later all accept eSIMs.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Older and budget Android phones are the ones that tend to be incompatible, so double-check before you buy.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Are eSIMs really necessary for travel?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Yes, they are, because eSIMs help you stay safe.</strong> I&#8217;ve been teaching women how to travel the world safely for more than 16 years now, and one of my top tips is to get an eSIM.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Having internet access on your phone helps you stay safe. Here&#8217;s how:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>eSIMs allow you to summon a ride when you need one.</strong> Whether it&#8217;s Uber in the US, Grab in Southeast Asia, or Yandex in Central Asia, rideshare and taxi apps are a critical way to get out of a situation quickly and safely.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>eSIMs make it easier to navigate by map. </strong>Ever tried finding your way around the winding streets of Venice or Lisbon without a map? An eSIM gives you that little blue dot on Google Maps that shows exactly where you are.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>eSIMs help you communicate. </strong>Google Translate is a vital tool when you don&#8217;t speak the local language. Not only can you type into it, but you can use the listen function to translate conversations, and the image tool to translate signs and menus.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>eSIMs keep you in contact. </strong>Whether you need to text your partner at home with an update or contact your hotel to modify your booking, or even book a reservation at a restaurant, eSIMs make it far easier to connect to the world.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>eSIMs keep you from missing important information.</strong> A lot of airports have inaccessible or limited wifi &#8212; if your flight has a last-minute schedule change or gate change, you&#8217;ll know about it.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why can&#8217;t I just use my own phone plan while traveling?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Using your international roaming plan on your home phone while traveling &#8212; especially if you&#8217;re American &#8212; is often grotesquely expensive.</strong> American phone plans tend to be much more expensive than other countries to begin with, and they charge even higher rates when using them internationally.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I have so many friends who got back from their trip and nearly keeled over when their phone bill charged them $200, $400, or even more. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even I once paid $80 to use my home phone on a weeklong stay in Antigua in 2019, because I didn&#8217;t want to go to a phone store and I didn&#8217;t know eSIMs existed. Oh, and an eSIM would have been SO much cheaper!</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What matters when choosing an eSIM?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There are several factors that go into choosing an eSIM.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>What destinations do you need the eSIM to cover? </strong>If you&#8217;re visiting a single country, it&#8217;s fairly straightforward &#8212; but if you&#8217;re visiting multiple countries in Europe or Southeast Asia, for example, it&#8217;s much easier to get a multi-destination regional eSIM.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For example, if you&#8217;re traveling from the US to Czech Republic, Austria, and Hungary, get a Europe eSIM that covers all three countries.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Global eSIMs do exist, covering (most of) the world, but they tend to be expensive and I find them unnecessary for most trips. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For example, if you&#8217;re traveling from the UK to Australia and doing a two-day layover in Dubai on the way there, just get a two-day eSIM for the UAE and a longer eSIM for Australia rather than a global eSIM.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>How much data does the eSIM include? </strong>I can tell you first-hand that it&#8217;s awful to lose data when you really need it &#8212; and it can often be a hassle to top-up. Unless you work in tech, it can be hard to guess just how much data you need.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some eSIMs, like Holafly, have unlimited data. Unlimited data is often more expensive than a fixed amount of data, but I find that never having to worry about your data running out is worth its weight in gold. Especially if you watch or upload a lot of videos.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>If your data isn&#8217;t unlimited, how do you top up?</strong> Back in the day in Thailand, I went to 7/11s to top up my data. These days, most eSIMs let you do it online, but double-check whether you can do it without having to access wifi.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Does your eSIM provide support? </strong>Some eSIM companies provide 24/7 support in English via chat or WhatsApp; others only provide support in the local language, only at certain hours, only by email, or not at all.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Do you need a local phone number, or just data?</strong> If you&#8217;re spending your trip making and receiving lots of local calls, like someone on a business trip working with lots of local companies, having a local phone number can be worth it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For me, I find having a local number unnecessary. The world outside the US makes most of their calls and texts on WhatsApp. On the rare occasion that I need to call an actual phone number, I use Google Voice.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Frequently Asked Questions About Travel eSims</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Are eSIMs safe?</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yes, eSIMs are as safe as regular SIM cards. They are held to the same standard telecom security protocols as the SIM card you use at home.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But one big difference is that they can&#8217;t be physically removed or stolen the way a plastic SIM can.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As always, be sure to buy an eSIM from a reputable company, not some rando on the street.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Does an eSIM drain my phone&#8217;s battery?</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">eSIMs do NOT drain your phone&#8217;s battery more than a regular SIM card! This is a common misconception.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Can I use an eSIM and keep my regular number?</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In almost all cases, yes. Most phones support two active SIM cards at once (one physical and one eSIM, or two eSIMs), so you can keep your home phone number active for texts.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just keep in mind that you might be charged more for receiving those texts when out of the country. Double-check with your home phone company to be sure.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What if my phone isn&#8217;t eSIM compatible?</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You&#8217;ll need to get a physical SIM card or use a pocket wifi device for your trip. If financially feasible, I&#8217;d recommend upgrading your phone in the near future, as this likely won&#8217;t be changing.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Is it cheaper to use an eSIM than my phone&#8217;s international roaming plan?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It is almost always significantly cheaper to use an eSIM rather than your phone&#8217;s international roaming plan. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I have friends who have received bills for $200 and up after using their home plan while traveling.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Do eSIMs work in remote areas?</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">eSIMs use the phone networks of different countries, so where there is phone coverage, there is eSIM coverage.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For the most part, I&#8217;ve found that eSIMs work well in the places where tourists go. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On my Uzbekistan trip, for example, my Holafly eSIM worked great in Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara, and Khiva, but didn&#8217;t work on the train in the middle of nowhere.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You know they call it Airplane Mode for a reason, right? You shouldn&#8217;t be using your phone on an airplane! And even if you did, it wouldn&#8217;t work when high up in the air.</p>
