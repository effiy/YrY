<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-is-marry-me-chicken">What is marry me chicken?</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-"></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;Marry Me Chicken&#8221; is a viral chicken dish that was so good, it initiated a marriage proposal, quickly becoming the ultimate go-to romantic dinner. The dish typically consists of pan-seared chicken with a rich, cream sauce made with:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Chicken stock</li>



<li>Heavy cream</li>



<li>Sun-dried tomatoes</li>



<li>Parmesan</li>



<li>Basil</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-is-marry-me-chicken-and-tuscan-chicken-the-same-thing">Is marry me chicken and tuscan chicken the same thing?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These two dishes are actually very similar, including a rich sun-dried tomato cream sauce, but they also have a few key differences.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Heat.</strong> Marry Me Chicken includes red pepper flakes for a kick of heat.</li>



<li><strong>Basil.</strong> Tuscan Chicken typically includes spinach, whereas Marry Me Chicken omits the greens and adds basil instead.</li>
</ul>
</div></div>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="" class="wp-image-49759" height="1140" src="https://damndelicious.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/250409_DD_Marry-Me-Chicken_591-760x1140.jpg" width="760" /></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-make-marry-me-chicken">How to make marry me chicken</h2>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Prep the chicken.</strong> Season the chicken with salt and pepper. If using chicken breasts, pound the chicken to 1/2-inch thickness. </li>



<li><strong>Cook the chicken.</strong> Sear the chicken until a nice golden-brown crust forms, letting it cook undisturbed for 4-5 minutes per side. Cook until the chicken reaches an internal temperature of 165°F. Set aside.</li>



<li><strong>Cook the aromatics.</strong> Cook the tomato paste, garlic, oregano, thyme, and red pepper flakes&nbsp;until fragrant.</li>



<li><strong>Add the flour.</strong> Stir in the flour until browned &#8211; this will serve as a premium thickening agent for the cream sauce. Cooking the flour also eliminates that raw flour taste.</li>



<li><strong>Deglaze the pan.</strong> Stir in the wine, deglazing the pan by scraping up all of the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the Dutch oven. Chicken stock can be substituted for the wine.</li>



<li><strong>Make the sauce.</strong> Add the chicken stock, heavy cream, and sun-dried tomatoes. Heavy cream will result in that luxurious creaminess whereas half-and-half (or whole milk) will be lighter but watery.</li>



<li><strong>Simmer.</strong> Simmer until slightly thickened, adding additional stock if the mixture is too thick.</li>



<li><strong>Add the finishing touches.</strong> Add the Parmesan, basil and cooked chicken.</li>



<li><strong>Serve.</strong> Garnish with parsley and serve over pasta, <a href="https://damndelicious.net/2022/11/11/how-to-make-perfect-mashed-potatoes/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">mashed potatoes</a> or rice with <a href="https://damndelicious.net/2017/12/05/no-knead-rosemary-bread/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">crusty bread</a>.</li>
</ol>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading faq-title" id="h-title-frequently-asked-questions">Marry Me Chicken: Frequently Asked Questions</h2>



<div class="schema-faq wp-block-yoast-faq-block"><div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1780186800322"><strong class="schema-faq-question">Why is it called &#8220;Marry Me Chicken&#8221;?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">The chicken dish is just so good, it will prompt anyone who eats it to propose!</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1715620917648"><strong class="schema-faq-question">Can I use chicken breasts?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">Yes, chicken breasts can absolutely be used here, but chicken thighs have more dark meat and a higher fat content which will yield juicier, more flavorful chicken (and are much more forgiving when overcooked!). If using chicken breasts, gently pound the chicken to 1/2-inch thickness for even cooking without drying out.</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1780187498326"><strong class="schema-faq-question">Can I use half-and-half for the heavy cream?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">Heavy cream is the gold standard due to its high fat content, yielding that luxurious, rich, and velvety texture. Half-and-half can certainly be used but may result in a thin sauce.</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1780186674988"><strong class="schema-faq-question">Why is my sauce too thin?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">Heavy cream will thicken as it simmers. If the sauce is too thin, let it simmer for longer until the desired consistency is reached. If the sauce is too thick, add a splash of chicken stock.</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1780186732317"><strong class="schema-faq-question">What can I serve this with?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">This can be served with anything that can soak up the sauce &#8211; pasta, mashed potatoes, rice, and crusty bread are all solid options.</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1715620969548"><strong class="schema-faq-question"><strong>How can I store leftovers?</strong></strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">Leftovers can be stored in an airtight container in the fridge for 3-4 days, reheating in the oven in a <a href="https://amzn.to/3Un35LW" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">baking dish</a> with a splash of stock or water (covered in foil) at 325°F until warmed through, about 15-20 minutes.</p> </div> <div class="schema-faq-section" id="faq-question-1780186548039"><strong class="schema-faq-question">Can I freeze this?</strong> <p class="schema-faq-answer">Freezing is not recommended as cream sauces tend to separate and become grainy when thawed.</p> </div> </div>

<div class="wprm-recipe-notes-container wprm-block-text-normal" id="recipe-49762-notes"><h3 class="wprm-recipe-header wprm-recipe-notes-header wprm-block-text-bold wprm-align-left wprm-header-decoration-none">Notes</h3><div class="wprm-recipe-notes"><ul>
<li><strong>Tomato paste adds so much flavor.</strong> Tomato paste is a key ingredient in Marry Me Chicken, adding body and richness in small amounts without adding too much liquid to the dish. Opt for <a href="https://amzn.to/40cbQv8" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">tomato paste in a tube</a>, using up only what you need at a time without having to open up a whole can.</li>
<li><strong>Choose a wine you will drink.</strong> A dry white wine (pinot grigio or sauvignon blanc) is great here – it does not have to be anything fancy or overpriced. Just something you don’t mind sipping on since you will have an open bottle.</li>
<li><strong>Deglaze with wine.</strong> This is key to scraping up all the browned, caramelized bits stuck to the bottom of the pan (called fond) after searing the chicken. This is where all the flavor is!</li>
<li><strong>Use heavy cream.</strong> Half-and-half (or whole milk) can be used, if needed, but heavy cream will yield the ultimate level of creaminess, whereas lower-fat dairy products (half-and-half or milk) will be a little more watery and prone to curdling.</li>
<li><strong>Use <a href="https://amzn.to/49rhvTx" rel="noopener" target="_blank">dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes</a>.</strong> These will be soft and brightly colored without being overly greasy (also great for snacking!).</li>
<li><strong>Freshly grated Parmesan goes a long way.</strong> Pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking agents that can make the sauce gritty. Grate fresh Parmesan (using a <a href="https://amzn.to/3YtqWM6" rel="noopener" target="_blank">microplane</a>) for a smooth, velvety consistency.</li>
<li><strong>Let it simmer and reduce.</strong> Let the sauce simmer gently until it coats the back of a spoon.</li>
</ul></div></div>
