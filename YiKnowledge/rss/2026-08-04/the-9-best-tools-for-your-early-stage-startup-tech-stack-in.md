<p>Building a startup used to be about nailing the fundamentals. Those still matter, but it&#8217;s never been easier to get them done. What separates startups now is the software they choose to build on, and how much of it is powered by AI.</p>
<p>The highest-impact software today has AI built into the core of the product, and it takes real work off your plate. These tools change what a small team can do. A three-person company can A/B test at scale, resolve support conversations without hiring in support, and identify what&#8217;s working on shoe-string budgets.</p>
<p>But there are more of these tools on the market than any early-stage team can realistically evaluate, or <a href="https://fin.ai/startup-pack">afford on a startup budget</a>.</p>
<p>This guide covers how to choose tools that are going to have the biggest impact for your startup, and the nine we recommend starting with.</p>
<h2 id="best-practices-for-choosing-the-right-tools-for-your-startup-tech-stack">Best practices for choosing the right tools for your startup tech stack</h2>
<p>Your earliest hires shape the company more than almost any decision you&#8217;ll make. Your tech should make them faster, not busier. Three principles help keep it that way:</p>
<h3>1. Choose tools that scale with you</h3>
<p>Be deliberate about picking tools that can serve your business needs right now and can adapt as you grow. Otherwise, you’ll face a painful migration the moment you scale. Before you commit, ask whether you can add capability as your needs change, onboard new teammates without friction, and keep pricing manageable as usage climbs.</p>
<p>This matters a lot with AI tools, where usage and cost can climb fast. Certain tools offer credits and free tiers early, so you can try them out and then graduate to paid plans once the return on investment is clear.</p>
<p>Full disclosure: we built the <a href="https://fin.ai/startup-pack">Fin Startup Pack</a> to help early-stage companies make use of these types of offers across 55+ partners.</p>
<h3>2. Make sure your tools integrate with each other</h3>
<p>Small teams lose more time to context-switching than almost anything else, and a tool that doesn&#8217;t talk to the rest of your stack creates manual work that compounds with every new hire. Before adopting anything, check how it connects to your core systems, and whether those integrations are built in or something you&#8217;ll have to build yourself.</p>
<p>When tools like Claude Code and MCPs make it possible to connect almost any two systems, there&#8217;s less excuse than ever for them to work in isolation.</p>
<h3>3. Focus on the problem</h3>
<p>A tool is only as good as the problem it solves. Define the problem, then pick the tool. And after you&#8217;ve rolled something out, be honest about whether it&#8217;s genuinely saving time or just adding another thing to manage.</p>
<h2 id="the-9-tools-wed-start-with">The 9 tools we’d start with</h2>
<h3>1. Stripe for payments</h3>
<p>Payments, billing, and the plumbing beneath them are the kind of thing you want to get right once and stop thinking about. Stripe handles payments, subscriptions, and even company formation, and scales from your first charge to global billing without you switching systems later.</p>
<h3>2. Slack for team productivity</h3>
<p>When your team is small, everything happens in one conversation. Add a few people and context scatters across DMs, threads, and whoever was in the meeting. Slack keeps those conversations, tools, and decisions in one place, so things don&#8217;t get lost and you&#8217;re not living in email.</p>
<h3>3. Granola for AI meeting notes</h3>
<p>Founders spend their days in meetings, and when notes are patchy or skipped entirely, decisions get lost. Granola records and transcribes those meetings and turns them into clear, shareable notes, so founders aren&#8217;t choosing between being present and getting actionable summaries.</p>
<h3>4. Fin Customer Agent (and the Intercom helpdesk) for the customer experience</h3>
<p>As soon as customers discover your product, questions will arrive, ranging from sales to support. Answering them one by one is exactly the work that pulls founders off building.</p>
<p>Fin is an AI-powered Customer Agent with dedicated roles for customer service and sales. It can resolve up to 76% of support conversations on its own, and can even qualify prospects, so your team can focus on building your company. It works natively inside the Intercom helpdesk and also connects to Salesforce, HubSpot, Freshdesk, and others.</p>
<h3>5. Linear for issue tracking and planning</h3>
<p>Great execution starts with an aligned team. Linear brings issue tracking, sprints, planning, and product roadmaps together, providing a clear workstream for engineers and a way for founders to stay in the know.</p>
<h3>6. Datadog for analytics and observability</h3>
<p>Without monitoring, you find out about a bug from an angry customer instead of a dashboard, and trust is hard to win back this early. Datadog watches logs, metrics, and traces in one place, which is a day-one concern now that AI systems are in production.</p>
<h3>7. MongoDB for your database</h3>
<p>Most startups need a database that can evolve with the product. MongoDB’s flexible schema allows iteration without heavy data modeling, and Atlas manages the hosting and scaling.</p>
<h3>8. Rippling for payroll, HR, and IT</h3>
<p>Hiring your first employee brings compliance and contract questions most founders aren&#8217;t equipped to answer alone – and getting them wrong is a legal risk, not just an admin headache. Rippling unifies payroll, benefits, devices, and apps in one platform.</p>
<h3>9. Apollo for outbound sales and CRM</h3>
<p>Running a real sales motion out of a spreadsheet or someone&#8217;s inbox means deals can fall through the cracks. Apollo combines a contact database, prospecting, and a lightweight CRM with data enrichment, so an early team can find the right people to reach and track deals.</p>
