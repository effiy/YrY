<h3><strong>Marvel vs. Capcom Fighting Collection: Arcade Classics ($49.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328915" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/marvelvscapcomheader.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>As a fan of Marvel, Capcom, and fighting games back in the 1990s, Capcom&#8217;s line of fighters based on Marvel&#8217;s characters were like a dream come true. Starting off with the excellent <em>X-Men: Children of the Atom</em>, these games just kept getting bigger and better. Moving on to the wider Marvel Universe with <em>Marvel Super Heroes</em>, then the at-the-time unbelievable crossovers between Marvel and<em> Street Fighter</em>, on to the over the top Marvel vs. Capcom, and then the wildly over-the-top in all the right ways <em>Marvel vs. Capcom 2</em>, Capcom kept raising the stakes. That wasn&#8217;t the end of the series, but it takes us up to the end of what is covered in <em>Marvel vs. Capcom Fighting Collection: Arcade Classics</em>. Oh, and you get Capcom&#8217;s excellent <em>Punisher</em> belt scrolling beat &#8217;em up as some extra mustard. A fantastic set of great games.</p>
<p>This collection appears to have been handled by whoever did the<em> Capcom Fighting Collection</em>, and in most respects it has similar features and extras. That unfortunately extends to only having a single save state across the entire collection, shared by all seven games. That was annoying enough in a collection full of fighting games, but it&#8217;s even worse with a beat &#8217;em up in here where you might want to, you know, save your progress independently of whatever you&#8217;re up to in the fighters. Oh well. Everything else here is as you would want it. Lots of options like visual filters and gameplay options, great extras including an extensive array of art and a music player, and rollback online multiplayer. New to this set is NAOMI hardware emulation, and whoever Capcom had on the job did it well. <em>Marvel vs. Capcom 2</em> looks and plays great.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328916" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/marvelvscapcom1.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I&#8217;m not going to knock it for this, but I do want to say that I wish some of the home versions were included. The PlayStation EX versions of the tag-team based games are different enough that it would be neat to have them in here, and the Dreamcast version of <em>Marvel vs. Capcom 2</em> has a lot of fun extras that make it a superior choice for home play for the solo player. I also wouldn&#8217;t have minded if Capcom had tucked its two Super NES Marvel games in here, even if they aren&#8217;t the greatest of games. Well, the collection&#8217;s name says <em>Arcade Classics</em>, and unlike with Blizzard it seems the word is being properly applied here.</p>
<p>Marvel fans and fighting fans alike have a reason to rejoice with this fine collection. The games are superb, they&#8217;ve been treated with care, and you get a good suite of extras and options. Only having a single save state shared between the games is a serious bummer, but otherwise I can&#8217;t find much to pick on here.<em> Marvel vs. Capcom Fighting Collection: Arcade Classics</em> is another must-have compilation from the folks at Capcom, and it plays great on the Switch.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4.5/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Yars Rising ($29.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328917" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/yarsrising.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I&#8217;ll admit that I was pretty skeptical of this game from its announcement. I like <em>Yars&#8217; Revenge</em> a lot. One of my favorite 2600 games. So when I read that WayForward had been tapped to make a Metroidvania-style <em>Yars</em> game starring a young hacker with a bare midriff code-named Yar, I felt like I had accidentally surfed over to a parody site. Just the perfect storm of &#8220;why", you know? So do I have to eat those words? Yes and no. First and foremost, this is a good game. WayForward does a solid job with this kind of thing, and so it did here. Looks and sounds good, plays well, and the map layouts are fine enough. In true WayForward fashion, the bosses tend to drag on too long, but it&#8217;s not a deal-breaker.</p>
<p>Commendations must also be given to WayForward for doing its best to handle a bit of a tough ask in trying to connect this game to an ancient single-screen shooter. You get to play<em> Yars&#8217; Revenge</em>-style sequences fairly often, the abilities you earn are evocative of the original game, and it connects to the relatively fleshed-out lore as well as it can. It still feels like a massive stretch, but I suppose Atari has little choice but to try to make long passes like this one. Its classic library can only be <em>Recharged</em> so much, after all. It just feels like a game that is torn between two audiences with very little overlap, and I&#8217;m not sure if that was the right call versus doing something wholly original.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328918" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/yarsrising2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>Still, while the argument can rage on about whether or not this made sense conceptually, there&#8217;s no doubt the game itself is enjoyable. I don&#8217;t think the finest in the genre have much to worry about, but if you&#8217;re looking for a Metroidvania game to sink your teeth into over the course of a weekend or so, you won&#8217;t have a bad time with<em> Yars Rising</em>. And who knows? Maybe they follow up on this once or twice and it all feels natural.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Rugrats: Adventures in Gameland ($24.99)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328919" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/rugratsadventureingameland.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>I&#8217;m a few years too old to have much nostalgia for <em>Rugrats</em>, though not so old that I didn&#8217;t watch it with my younger siblings from time to time. Like, I know the names of the main characters and the theme song. Don&#8217;t ask me about the movies or the grown-up versions, and certainly don&#8217;t ask me to recall any specific episodes. I know the <em>Rugrats</em>, but I have no special warm and fuzzies for the brand. With that in mind, I didn&#8217;t really know what to expect from <em>Rugrats: Adventures in Gameland</em>. I heard someone say it was like <em>Bonk</em>, and that does fit Tommy&#8217;s physical build if nothing else. Well, only one way to know for sure. I started up the game, picked Tommy, and went into the tutorial stage.</p>
<p>First thing to hit me was the crisp visuals. Crisper than the show, if my memory serves. The next thing to hit me was the awkward placement of the controls. Luckily, there&#8217;s an option for that. The music was the Rugrats theme, so that all checks out. There were some Reptar coins to collect, and some simple puzzles and enemies to deal with. Alright, no problem. Platformer with some exploration in the levels, a tried and tested formula. Not very <em>Bonk</em>-ish, but that was never promised.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328920" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/rugratsadventures2.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>At a certain point Tommy had taken a few hits, so I decided to swap over to Chuckie to enjoy his full life meter. I then noticed that he had a very familiar jump. A very high but somewhat hard to control jump. Surely they didn&#8217;t? I swapped over to Phil, who had a low jump, then to Lil who could&#8230; float. They did. They really did! Folks, this is a <em>Super Mario Bros. 2</em> (USA)-inspired game! Sure enough, the enemies I had just been stomping on could also be picked up and thrown. There were also blocks I sometimes had to pick up and pile up to reach higher places. Slightly non-linear stages with lots of verticality! Stages where you have to dig in sand, and you know Phil is the expert digger of the bunch. Awesome.</p>
<p>I mean, there are some homages to other platformers in here, but the main gameplay evokes what is probably one of the higher-selling classic games that never gets riffed on. Not bad, not bad at all. The boss battles are even interesting and enjoyable. After playing for a while, I even noticed I could swap the visuals and soundtrack between the spiffy modern versions and NES-level 8-bit renditions. It plays well either way, and both styles have their merits. Oh, and you can use a filter. There if you want it. But yes, creative and fun. Inspired by a game I love quite a lot. Uses its license well. You can play multiplayer! Aside from the control issue, the only gripe I have is that it&#8217;s a bit too short and simple.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328921" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/rugratsadventures3.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p><em>Rugrats: Adventures in Gameland</em> is a better game than I expected it to be. It&#8217;s a quality platformer in the style of the Western<em> Super Mario Bros. 2</em>, with some extra elements and features to keep it from being too close to its source. The Rugrats license has been implemented well, though I did find myself wishing it had voice acting for the cut scenes. A little on the short and breezy side to be sure, but worth a play for fans of platformers and the <em>Rugrats</em> alike.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 4/5</strong></p>
