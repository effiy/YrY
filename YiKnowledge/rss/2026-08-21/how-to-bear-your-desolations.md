<h3>&#8220;You may have to break your heart, but it isn’t nothing to know even one moment alive.&#8221;</h3>
<hr />
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/How-Love-World-Poems-Gratitude/dp/1635863864/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img alt="How to Bear Your Desolations" class="cover with-border alignright size-medium" height="450" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/howtolovetheworld.jpg?fit=320%2C450&amp;ssl=1" width="320" /></a></p><p>The morning after a relationship of depth and significance long bending under the weight of its own complexity had finally broken with an exhausted thud, I opened <a href="https://themarginalian.org/tag/ceramics">the kiln</a> to discover a month&#8217;s worth of pottery shattered &#8212; two pieces had exploded, the shrapnel ruining the rest. All that centering, all that glazing, all the hours of pressing letterforms into the wet clay &#8212; all of it in shiny shards. And meanwhile spring was breaking outside and a little girl in bright blue rain boots was jumping in a puddle, smashing the reflections of the clouds with savage joy. </p>
<p>And I thought, this is all there is: breaking, breaking apart, breaking open. </p>
<p>Breaking alive. </p>

<p>It is not an easy assignment, being alive. Coming awake from the stupor of near-living that lulls us through our days, awake to the knowledge that on the other side of the neighborhood ICE trucks are handcuffing people and on the other side of the planet children are dying in gunfire, while outside the first birds of spring are singing and everywhere people are falling in love and in some faraway mountain village a shepherd is singing under a thousand stars. And somehow, somehow, all of it has to cohere into a single world in which we, in all our incohesion, must live this single life. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/tag/ellen-bass/">Ellen Bass</a> reckons with all of this in her splendid poem &#8220;Any Common Desolation,&#8221; originally published in The Academy of American Poets&#8217; <a href="https://poets.org/poem-a-day" target="_blank">poem-a-day</a> newsletter and later included in James Crews&#8217;s lifeline of an anthology <a href="https://www.amazon.com/How-Love-World-Poems-Gratitude/dp/1635863864/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><strong><em>How to Love the World: Poems of Gratitude and Hope</em></strong></a> (<a href="https://search.worldcat.org/title/1159045177" target="_blank"><em>public library</em></a>), shared here with Ellen&#8217;s blessing. </p>
<p></p>
<blockquote><p><strong>ANY COMMON DESOLATION<br />
<em>by Ellen Bass</em></p>
<p>can be enough to make you look up<br />
at the yellowed leaves of the apple tree, the few<br />
that survived the rains and frost, shot<br />
with late afternoon sun. They glow a deep<br />
orange-gold against a blue so sheer, a single bird<br />
would rip it like silk. You may have to break<br />
your heart, but it isn’t nothing<br />
to know even one moment alive. The sound<br />
of an oar in an oarlock or a ruminant<br />
animal tearing grass. The smell of grated ginger.<br />
The ruby neon of the liquor store sign.<br />
Warm socks. You remember your mother,<br />
her precision a ceremony, as she gathered<br />
the white cotton, slipped it over your toes,<br />
drew up the heel, turned the cuff. A breath<br />
can uncoil as you walk across your own muddy yard,<br />
the big dipper pouring night down over you, and everything<br />
you dread, all you can’t bear, dissolves<br />
and, like a needle slipped into your vein &#8212;<br />
that sudden rush of the world.</p></blockquote>
<p>Complement with Henry James on <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2022/10/17/henry-james-the-beast-in-the-jungle/">how to stop waiting and start living</a> and Hermann Hesse on <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/07/06/hermann-hesse-wonder-butterflies/">how to be more alive</a>, then revisit Ellen&#8217;s magnificent poem <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/11/28/how-to-apologize-ellen-bass/">&#8220;How to Apologize.&#8221;</a> And if you are looking to break your poetry open, I couldn&#8217;t recommend her <a href="https://www.ellenbass.com/events/living-room-craft-talk-series/">Living Room Craft Talks</a> more heartily.</p>
