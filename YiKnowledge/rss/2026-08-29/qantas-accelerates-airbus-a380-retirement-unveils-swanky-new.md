<div class="rssBody"><div><p>Qantas is accelerating plans to retire the world&#x27;s biggest passenger plane.</p><p>This week, the Australian flag carrier announced it would begin phasing out its Airbus A380s in 2028. That&#x27;s four years earlier than the airline had originally planned.</p><p>It&#x27;s also the latest reminder that the superjumbo won&#x27;t be around forever.</p><p>Qantas&#x27; plans to sunset its four-engine, double-decker plane come as the airline is set to get an influx of new long-haul aircraft in the coming years.</p><h2 class="rssH2">Bidding farewell to the superjumbo</h2><p>In 2023, the Sydney-based carrier revealed an order for 24 new twin-aisle jets: 12 Airbus A350s and 12 Boeing 787 Dreamliners.</p><p>At the time, Qantas said those planes would eventually replace its aging Airbus A330 and A380 fleets.</p><p>The airline had expected to start bidding farewell to its A380s in 2032.</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2024/12/20241217_Qantas-A380-First-LAX-SYD_MMoffitt-38.jpg" /><figcaption>MATT MOFFITT/THE POINTS GUY</figcaption></figure><p>But with 17 new planes expected to join its fleet this year and another 31 slated for delivery in 2027, Qantas executives this week announced they&#x27;d begin the retirement process in 2028. It&#x27;ll likely take years before each of its A380s is grounded for good.</p><h2 class="rssH2">Airlines turning away from the A380</h2><p>While the A380 was an aviation marvel when it debuted in the early 21st century, global carriers have increasingly moved away from the superjumbo.</p><p>Its four engines and massive weight make it an expensive plane to operate — and a potential money-loser if too many seats are empty. And there are a lot of seats to fill.</p><p>That has made the jet a tough sell for carriers as new, more fuel-efficient planes have hit the market. Not to mention, <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/news/summer-airfare-surge-inflation-tips-booking-flights/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=397649&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">fuel prices have surged</a> in 2026.</p><p><strong>Qantas: <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/news/qantas-airways-project-sunrise-nonstop-flights-sydney-new-york-london/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=397649&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">London will get first &#x27;Project Sunrise&#x27; flights, followed by New York</a></strong></p><h3>Which airlines still fly the Airbus A380?</h3><p>Aside from Qantas, nine other airlines currently fly the huge plane. Most notably, Emirates flies more A380s than any other carrier (by far).</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2026/06/image00075.jpeg" /><figcaption>Emirates Airbus A380 with NBA livery. SEAN CUDAHY/THE POINTS GUY</figcaption></figure><p>Airbus built its last A380 in 2021.</p><h2 class="rssH2">How to still fly Qantas on the Airbus A380</h2><p>Qantas has 10 A380s currently in service.</p><p>Its 485-seat aircraft features a <a href="https://thepointsguy.com/airline/reviews/qantas-first-class-a380/?utm_campaign=the-points-guy&amp;utm_content=397649&amp;utm_medium=syndication&amp;utm_source=The+Points+Guy" target="_blank">true international first-class cabin</a>, which is increasingly rare on global carriers. It also has 70 business-class pods.</p><figure class="rssFigure"><img alt="" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2024/12/20241217_Qantas-A380-First-LAX-SYD_MMoffitt-57.jpg" /><figcaption>MATT MOFFITT/THE POINTS GUY</figcaption></figure><p>U.S. travelers can experience the jet on three routes to Australia, still:</p><ul><li>Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) to Sydney Airport (SYD)</li><li>LAX to Melbourne Airport (MEL)</li><li>Dallas Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) to SYD</li></ul><h2 class="rssH2">New business-class suites coming to the Boeing 787</h2><p>While Qantas&#x27; older planes are slated for retirement, the airline this week revealed a luxe new offering for its incoming aircraft.</p><p>The carrier announced a next-generation business-class suite for its Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners.</p><p>The suites feature sliding privacy doors — a first for Qantas&#x27; 787 fleet. Business-class flyers will get 80-inch beds and wider footwells, which should be an upgrade over the existing product.</p><p>Plus, there&#x27;s a 19-inch entertainment screen.</p><figure class="rssGallery"><img alt="787 business suite" src="https://runway-media-production.global.ssl.fastly.net/us/originals/2026/08/787-Business-Suite.jpg" /><figcaption>QANTAS</figcaption></figure><p>Qantas is calling this an &quot;evolution&quot; over the seats it&#x27;s flying up front right now.
