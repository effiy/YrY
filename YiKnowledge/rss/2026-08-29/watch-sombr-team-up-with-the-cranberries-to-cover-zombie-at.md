<p><img alt="Sombr joined by Noel and Mike Hogan, 2026" class="attachment-full size-full wp-post-image" height="1270" src="https://www.nme.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/live-the-cranberr-esEP.jpg" width="2000" /></p><p><a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/sombr">Sombr</a> teamed up with <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/the-cranberries">The Cranberries</a> to cover &#8216;Zombie&#8217; at Electric Picnic in Ireland last night &#8211; check out footage below.</p>

<li><strong>READ MORE: <a href="https://www.nme.com/news/music/sombr-brits-2026-interview-verve-oasis-uk-music-jack-whitehall-3932360">Sombr at the BRITs 2026: “I love America, but I think music from the UK beats them”</a></strong></li>

<p>The three-day event kicked off last night (August 28) at Stradbally Hall in County Laois, where the US pop artist took to the stage with a flamboyant entrance that saw him arrive backstage in a Mercedes, and later attempt to split the G.</p>
<p>He later surprised fans by bringing out Noel and Mike Hogan of The Cranberries to perform the 1994 classic &#8216;Zombie&#8217; &#8211; and you can find footage of the moment below.</p>
<p>It’s the second time he&#8217;s done a Cranberries crossover. Back in March, the singer played the final night of his ‘The Late Nights &amp; Young Romance Tour’ at Dublin’s 3Arena, <a href="https://www.nme.com/news/music/watch-sombr-try-to-split-the-g-and-perform-linger-with-the-cranberries-on-stage-in-dublin-3935235">and brought out Mike and Noel to mark the occasion</a>.</p>
<p>They joined him on stage for a rendition of their 1993 classic hit, ‘Linger’, prompting fans to joke that the American singer had &#8220;earned his Irish passport&#8221;.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.nme.com/news/music/sombr-brits-2026-interview-verve-oasis-uk-music-jack-whitehall-3932360">The singer spoke to <em>NME</em> at the BRITs 2026</a>, where he was <a href="https://www.nme.com/news/music/brit-awards-2026-nominees-full-list-3924663">nominated for International Artist and International Song (‘Undressed’)</a>. Sombr said he felt “honoured” to be at the awards, and was “thankful for the British people for accepting” his debut album “with open arms”.</p>
<p>“I just love it here,” he told us. “It’s the music that comes from the UK [that clicks with me]. In my opinion, I love America, but I think acts from the UK just beat them, in terms of iconic acts.”</p>
<p>He cited the likes of <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/the-beatles">The Beatles</a>, <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/the-rolling-stones">The Rolling Stones</a>, <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/david-bowie">David Bowie</a> and <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/elton-john">Elton John</a>, as well as some legendary Manchester bands like <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/oasis">Oasis</a> and <a href="https://www.nme.com/artists/the-stone-roses">The Stone Roses</a>.</p>
