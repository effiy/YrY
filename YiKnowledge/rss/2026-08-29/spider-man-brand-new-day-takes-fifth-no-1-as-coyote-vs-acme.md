<p>Sony/Marvel Studios&#8217; &#8220;Spider-Man: Brand New Day&#8221; has completed its sweep of the month of August, holding on to the No. 1 spot for every day of the month save for last Friday&#8217;s opening day of &#8220;Insidious: Out of the Further&#8221; and earning its fifth No. 1 frame this weekend with an estimated $22 million total. </p>



<p>With an estimated domestic total of $891 million, the Marvel Studios film is just $43 million away from passing the unadjusted domestic total of &#8220;Star Wars: The Force Awakens&#8221; to take the all time North American record. </p>



<p>Meanwhile, Ketchup Entertainment/Warner Animation Group&#8217;s &#8220;Coyote vs. Acme&#8221; has finally hit theaters after nearly being shelved by Warner Bros. for a tax writeoff. Released at 3,573 locations, the film earned $5.2 million on its opening day and is estimated for a $13-14 million opening weekend as it contends with the seventh weekend of Universal&#8217;s &#8220;The Odyssey&#8221; for the No. 2 spot. </p>



<p>Ketchup paid a hefty sum of $50 million to acquire &#8220;Coyote vs. Acme,&#8221; but the film&#8217;s notoriety among animation lovers for its behind-the-scenes turbulence has made it the indie distributor&#8217;s largest opening weekend ever, more than quadruple the previous record of $3.1 million set by fellow &#8220;Looney Tunes&#8221; acquisition &#8220;The Day the Earth Blew Up&#8221; in March 2025. </p>



<p>The film is also getting rave reviews from both critics and audiences, earning Rotten Tomatoes scores of 94% critics and 97% audience and becoming the first &#8220;Looney Tunes&#8221; theatrical feature ever to earn an A on CinemaScore. In fact, only two other Warner Bros.-produced animated features have ever earned that CinemaScore grade: &#8220;The Iron Giant&#8221; in 1998 and &#8220;The Lego Movie&#8221; in 2014.</p>



<p>It&#8217;s practically unheard of for a family film with word-of-mouth this strong to come out at the end of the summer, but that is where &#8220;Coyote vs. Acme&#8221; finds itself. As kids head back to school, we will see if families discover this title in the weeks ahead. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s far less likely that an audience will build for 20th Century&#8217;s &#8220;The Dog Stars,&#8221; which is bombing fast amid poor reviews with an estimated $7 million opening weekend after making just $2.8 million from 3,330 locations. Ridley Scott&#8217;s latest film starring Jacob Elordi and Josh Brolin has left critics unimpressed with a 39% Rotten Tomatoes score while audience scores stand at 70% and a C+ on CinemaScore. </p>
