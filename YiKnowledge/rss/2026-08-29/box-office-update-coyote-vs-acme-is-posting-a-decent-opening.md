<p>Considering the terrible summer Warner Bros. is having, it’s pretty clear they made a mistake axing <em>Coyote vs. Acme</em> from their catalogue, with the small distributor Ketchup Entertainment leading it to a decent opening in the mid-teens. According to <a href="https://deadline.com/2026/08/box-office-coyote-vs-acme-dog-stars-spider-man-1237061934/">Deadline’s numbers</a>, it’s looking at an opening in the $12-14 million range. While hardly blockbuster box office numbers, one must remember that Ketchup has had to go the grassroots direction with its marketing of the film, so that’s a pretty impressive opening. Had WB given it one of their high-end campaigns, it could have opened to twice that number, which would have let it outperform one of their many flops from this summer, which included <em>Supergirl</em>, <em>Evil Dead Burn</em>, and <em>The End of Oak Street</em>.</p>



<p>Alas, instead they decided to make it a tax write-off, and had the fans and Ketchup not intervened, it would have been lost in legal limbo forever (similar to the fate that awaited the still-unseen&nbsp;<em>Batgirl</em>&nbsp;movie). It’s worth noting that&nbsp;<em>Coyote vs. Acme</em>&nbsp;also scored a great A CinemaScore rating.</p>



<p>However, the weekend’s other big opener, Ridley Scott’s <em>The Dog Stars</em>, is on track for an abysmal $8 million opening (<a href="https://www.joblo.com/the-dog-stars-wont-outswing-spider-man/">pretty much what we predicted earlier this week</a>), which is horrible for a movie that cost over $100 million. Despite a buzzy cast that includes <em>Euphoria</em>’s Jacob Elordi, Margaret Qualley, and Josh Brolin, only faithful Ridley Scott fans are seeking this one out, and it’s earned a flat-out terrible C+ CinemaScore. There’s nothing saving this from being one of the year’s biggest flops.</p>



<p>Another low-key release, Roadside Attractions’&nbsp;<em>Buddy</em>, about a killer, Barney-esque children’s TV mascot, is overperforming, with it on track for a $4-5 million weekend. As for&nbsp;<em>Spider-Man: Brand New Day</em>, it will easily top the weekend box office with about $19 million, while Christopher Nolan’s&nbsp;<em>The Odyssey</em>&nbsp;is still riding high with $14.5 million.&nbsp;<em>Insidious: Out of the Further</em>&nbsp;is proving to be front-loaded, with it looking at a 64% drop to $9 million, but that’s not unexpected for a PG-13 horror flick.</p>
