<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2024/09/10110709/MixCollage-10-Sep-2024-11-05-AM-7630.jpg" /><p>The packed history of superhero cinema in the 21st century is full of a lot of near-misses: Actors rumored to play roles, or who got different jobs than the ones they were supposedly getting, or—in extreme cases like <a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAEScQHrOzAVpvlwe8nuNlomHSmgpl6hcflZODV6jCvxOS8mMOV4bmkywfsvvUp5YTyO3UfKzC5tQpzLK-pf2QfzvTLHRlHB0kjVQitTV453kY30ITPhikvcpHN-jNrKtw0HB1rXneMbPxguxsj3uvPqzvnP">Mahershala Ali&#8217;s Blade movie</a>—getting announced in a big, flashy press conference to be making a movie that then ultimately evaporates. Still, the collection of actors who&#8217;ve actually been put in an iconic comic book part, showed up on set, filmed their scenes, and <em>then&nbsp;</em>got cut from the canon is pretty small—which might explain why Shailene Woodley is still pretty bummed about it, a decade after the fact.</p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p><a href="https://variety.com/2026/film/news/shailene-woodley-amazing-spider-man-2-mary-jane-cut-a-year-after-filming-1236846453/">Per <em>Variety</em></a>, the <em>Divergent&nbsp;</em>series star was talking at a SAG-AFTRA event when her brief (bordering on non-existent) tenure in the Andrew Garfield&nbsp;<em>Spider-Man&nbsp;</em>movies came up. Woodley was famously cast as Mary Jane Watson in <em><a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESkQEB6zswFb8AQojmfw17BVUhBTiQidoaqEaj3WKLiZdicztKGoft6lTeUwxJORlyMogEMH6i1oBzJUWOeyJBmbzkDrSnO4IOqK_cAXLBb3FgaO2ksmabNK7KdXOvclwlfCH0mBUBv1V8X_edArW_r0z6PetZwoIyF6Cq2myaZOYrhZP5Zp_QxtZkksdWpLkciQPd">The Amazing Spider-Man 2</a>&nbsp;</em>back in 2013, including filming scenes with Garfield. But the character was ultimately cut from the movie, to better focus on the relationship between Garfield&#8217;s Peter Parker and Emma Stone&#8217;s Gwen Stacy. (Given how that film ends, and how generally well-received Stone&#8217;s performance in those movies was, we suspect producers may have made the right call; Woodley would have been setting herself up for some pretty peak 2010s internet dogpiling.) Still, she sounds pretty miffed about it, even to this day: &#8220;That was a fun phone call,&#8221; she said of getting the news—a year after filming was finished— that she just straight-up wasn&#8217;t in the movie anymore, adding that she&#8217;d been especially excited because &#8220;Spider-Man’s my favorite superhero. Mary Jane was my favorite character growing up. So, when I got it, I was like, ‘This is so exciting.'&#8221;&nbsp;</p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p>At the time, Woodley was told they&#8217;d save her debut for a straighter introduction in <em>The Amazing Spider-Man 3</em>, which, of course, wound up not happening. (The deal Sony and Marvel put together in 2015 to get Spider-Man into the MCU killed the franchise, although Garfield reprised his role as The Saddest Peter in 2021&#8217;s&nbsp;<em>Spider-Man: No Way Home</em>.) As for Woodley, she admits there could have been other reasons her Mary Jane was cut. Noting that producers made public statements that shoe-horning a second love interest into <em>Amazing 2&nbsp;</em>proved extremely unwieldy, she added, &#8220;Well, that’s what I was told, you know. But again, who knows what the truth is. I could have been terrible and they’re like, ‘We can’t do this.&#8217;”</p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
