<img src="https://img.pastemagazine.com/wp-content/avuploads/2026/08/29133441/days-of-thunder-cruise-hathaway-feature.jpg" /><p>Tom Cruise has been <a href="https://www.avclub.com/tom-cruise-days-of-thunder-sequel">publicly mulling</a> the possibility of making a sequel to 1990 NASCAR flick&nbsp;<em>Days Of Thunder&nbsp;</em>for a few years now, confident that audiences would give the same reception they gave to&nbsp;<em>Top Gun: Maverick&nbsp;</em>to a sequel to his <em>other&nbsp;</em>Tony Scott-directed technical epic. Now—after what&#8217;s <a href="https://www.avclub.com/days-of-thunder-update-director-tom-cruise">reportedly been about two years of negotiations between all parties</a>—Paramount has announced that it&#8217;s officially putting the pedal to the bit of the floor where the pedal goes, announcing a new film (apparently still just called&nbsp;<em>Days Of Thunder</em>) starring Cruise and Anne Hathaway, and aimed at a June 2, 2028 release.</p><!-- RevContent --> <div id="revcontent-hidden"> <!-- revisit --><!-- admarker --> 
<p>Given how much emphasis always gets put on Cruise&#8217;s movie star status—to the point that <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pd4Olzi0HEc">a recent trailer</a> for his upcoming Alejandro G. Iñárritu film&nbsp;<em><a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESgAEB6zswFeJpVgdU1mmGPRyesLas3MyomtK3loFaSrzTeYdUotHSs75rd8RCXhck6xhR8pjo98BE5YFzY0ORrDManr-olujRriQnviYwpBEFB_PNMAWvxpaDmRDyM9g57yVmFE_lDVtRtV0wO5-4h4qlCeiWVMDMYsxbiZudv9yVyQ">Digger</a>&nbsp;</em>amounted, almost literally, to two straight minutes of &#8220;Hey, you like Tom Cruise, right?&#8221;—it&#8217;s actually a little surprising that he&#8217;s sharing billing in the movie&#8217;s big press announcement. Hathaway <em>is</em> having a pretty productive summer, though, having starred in both <em><a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYQHrOzAVmtaFjn9p3u_8cNrlbJLJENA9fJ4ZYYanX1S8TuTAa901wlI8OP7huoYXhIUE_QQ-cPRyFEpzTBCAvIPtADUlN1y7or_IT3YfeMsAibH3AaWw-MkuJpfLnfvYG0Q">The Odyssey</a>&nbsp;</em>and&nbsp;<a href="https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESawHrOzAVstBYMsEekRC5pjCnWbUsHxCzGRsQhzhpbK84gwAEzljBN79qDvIrZhKnLlebxDHaKiZGFEjimiPI5kzwFCrRGHhsByimqWwmt0Yw38vLIbB2NNDvMWUTQt7OsEq5nS31iXlusm9i"><em>The End Of Oak Street</em></a>,&nbsp; so someone clearly thinks she has enough star power at this point to balance out Cruise. (Our alternative theory, meanwhile, is that they just needed someone else to stand in the press photo to obscure a visual reminder, printed on the car shown, that Cruise&#8217;s character name in this franchise is &#8220;Cole Trickle&#8221;—which has always sounded to us like a urinary disease that afflicts people who&#8217;ve spent too long down in a mine.)</p><!-- inlinecontent_1 --> </p>
<p>No plot details for the sequel have been announced, although we did get character descriptions: The 64-year-old Cruise will, of course, be returning as his once-hotshot NASCAR driver, while Hathaway will play &#8220;a team owner and engineer.&#8221; Jonathan Levine, whose resumé is mostly filled with comedies like <em>50/50&nbsp;</em>and&nbsp;<em>Long Shot</em>, will direct; it&#8217;s an interesting choice, given that nothing in Levine&#8217;s filmography suggests the kind of technical rigor required to keep Cruise alive on a film set full of fast-moving traffic that you just <em>know </em>he&#8217;s going to want to run out and play in.</p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p><!-- inlinecontent_2 --> </p>
