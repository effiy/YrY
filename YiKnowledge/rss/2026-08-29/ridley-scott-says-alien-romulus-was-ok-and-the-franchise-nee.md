<p><em>Alien: Romulus</em> was a major critical and commercial success, with many calling it <a href="https://www.joblo.com/alien-movies-ranked-from-worst-to-best/">one of the best of the franchise</a>, but it appears Ridley Scott wasn&#8217;t blown away. During a recent interview with <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DclolS2DtTT/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&amp;igsi=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">AlloCiné</a>, the iconic director said the 2024 sequel was merely &#8220;OK.&#8221; In fact, he added that he believes the franchise needs some help, and he&#8217;s the one to get it back on track.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-ridley-scott-on-alien-romulus">Ridley Scott on <em>Alien: Romulus</em></h2>



<p>&#8220;Actually, I suddenly went back and got involved,&#8221; Scott told the French outlet. &#8220;The one that just came out [2024’s <em>Alien: Romulus</em>] was OK. I think it needs some help. So, I’ve gone back in.&#8220;</p>



<p>Set between the events of <em>Alien</em> and <em>Aliens</em>, <em>Alien: Romulus</em> follows &#8220;a group of young people on a distant world who find themselves in a confrontation with the most terrifying life form in the universe.&#8221; Director Fede Álvarez <a href="https://www.joblo.com/alien-romulus-sequel-fede-alvarez-not-directing/">wasn&#8217;t going to return for the sequel</a>, but he did co-write the script. However, finding a new director has proven to be difficult, and there were even rumours that the project had been cancelled, which 20th Century Studios President Steve Asbell <a href="https://www.joblo.com/alien-romulus-2-cancelled/">denied</a>.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-does-ridley-scott-have-planned">What Does Ridley Scott Have Planned?</h2>



<p>However, it doesn&#8217;t sound like Scott is interested in picking up the pieces of <em>Alien: Romulus</em>. Instead, he wants to return to David (Michael Fassbender), the unhinged android who got up to trouble in <em>Prometheus</em> and <em>Alien: Covenant</em>.</p>



<p>&#8220;I’ve already got a footprint for the next <em>Alien</em>,&#8221; he said. &#8220;We did <em>Prometheus</em>, and then <em>Alien: Covenant</em>. So, I’m picking up where we left off in <em>Covenant</em> with Micheal Fassbender, who now thinks he’s Ozymandias.&#8220;</p>



<p>Scott continued, &#8220;I like the fact that AI has gone off the rails. If we go AI, the moment we discover it’s off the rails is too late. It could switch every digital thing off in the world like that. You’d have fucking chaos. It’s that kind of stuff that’s scary.&#8220;</p>



<p>If I&#8217;m being perfectly honest, I <em>would</em> like to see the final stage of David&#8217;s story, but I also want to see what <em>other</em> directors can do with the franchise. It was a unique element of the <em>Alien</em> movies. After Ridley Scott set the stage with the first movie, James Cameron, David Fincher, and Jean-Pierre Jeunet put their own spin on the universe, and it worked beautifully; well, some better than others, but they each felt distinctly different. And if the franchise really is going to move forward, it can&#8217;t just be the Ridley Scott show.</p>



<p>Was <em>Alien: Romulus</em> just okay? Do you want Ridley Scott to continue David&#8217;s story with the next <em>Alien</em> movie?</p>
