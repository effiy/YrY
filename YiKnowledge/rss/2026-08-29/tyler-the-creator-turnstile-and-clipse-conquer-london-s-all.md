<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	<a href="https://www.billboard.com/artist/tyler-the-creator">Tyler, The Creator</a> opened his All Points East headline set in London by expressing gratitude that Theresa May is no longer Prime Minister — with a few expletives thrown in. Beef between the pair goes way back: May famously <a href="https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/tyler-the-creator-says-hes-been-banned-from-the-uk-for-3-5-years-6678408/">barred the L.A. native from the U.K.</a> back in 2015 for allegedly “posing a threat to public order.”</p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	A lot has changed in the decade-plus since, not least the fact that Tyler is a rather different person to who he might have been deemed by some back then. His music is just as different too.</p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	Tyler’s career has, in fact, been marked by constant reinvention. Ever since his early, button-pushing days in rap troupe Odd Future, his music and artistry has blossomed to mercurial levels, weaving between the sun-kissed neo-soul of <em>Flower Boy</em> and iconoclastic pop-funk of <em>IGOR</em> to the high-energy experimentation of 2025’s <em>Don&#8217;t Tap the Glass</em>.</p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	Fittingly, the bill for the first of his two nights headlining Victoria Park on Friday (Aug. 28) followed that same genre-blurring instinct, a line-up reflecting not just the variation in Tyler’s own sound, but in his fans’ tastes too.</p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	On the rap front, Vince Staples brought potent, sardonic West Coast hip-hop, alongside <a href="https://www.billboard.com/artist/clipse/">Clipse</a>’s laser-sharp technicality, Sexyy Red’s club-ready Southern bangers, the Spanish drill of BB Trickz and fakemink repping the U.K. underground. </p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	For the more melodic crowd, there was Rex Orange County — a key <em>Flower Boy</em> collaborator making a rare recent U.K. festival appearance — along with Mariah the Scientist&#8217;s atmospheric R&amp;B, Houston dream-pop act Love Spells, the introspective tones of Memphis singer Yebba and supermodel*&#8217;s lo-fi shoegaze, each echoing a different soulful corner of Tyler’s catalogue. </p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	<a href="https://www.billboard.com/artist/turnstile/">Turnstile</a>, meanwhile, held down the harsher end, as did Melbourne’s Ecca Vandal, Sydney’s Shady Nasty and the Brockhampton-approved Jean Dawson — hardcore and rock acts all mirroring Tyler’s own push-and-pull between hard and melodic sounds.</p>



<p class="paragraph larva // lrv-u-margin-lr-auto  lrv-a-font-body-m   ">
	As Tyler kicked off his first day at All Points East, these were the seven best moments.</p>
