<p>We may earn a commission from links on this page.</p><p>Look, I love my <a href="https://zdcs.link/aDpr5W?pageview_type=RSS&amp;template=content&amp;module=content_body&amp;element=offer&amp;item=text-link&amp;element_label=AirPods&amp;object_type=content&amp;short_url=aDpr5W&amp;u=https%3A%2F%2Flifehacker.com%2Ffeed%2Frss" rel="sponsored" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">AirPods</a>. I wouldn't really opt for a different pair of earbuds, considering how entrenched I am in the Apple ecosystem. While the audio quality and feature set keep me hooked, there is one enormous flaw lurking under the surface: once the batteries go, the AirPods go, too.</p><p>AirPods are far from alone here. Many wireless earbuds do not give users the option to replace the batteries. That includes whether you want to replace the batteries yourself, or have a repair shop handle the task. The way these devices are designed, battery replacement borders on simply impossible, since companies often solder batteries in place. And seeing as all lithium-ion batteries degrade over time, eventually, your earbuds won't be able to hold a usable charge. Once that day comes, you'll likely buy a new pair, spending potentially hundreds of dollars once again, and adding another device to the e-waste pile. </p><p>It doesn't have to be this way. Earbud manufacturers are choosing to design their products without serviceable batteries. We know this because some companies out there actually sell earbuds that let you replace the batteries. <p>Fairphone's whole shtick is repairability. The company's product line&mdash;including its Fairphone smartphone, Fairbuds XL headphones, and Fairbuds earbuds&mdash;is designed to be modular and serviceable, skipping glue and convoluted manufacturing with easily accessible screws. That's not because companies are seeing the light, or even because there's a profit to be made from including replaceable batteries in their earbuds. Instead, new regulations (<a href="https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20230609IPR96210/making-batteries-more-sustainable-more-durable-and-better-performing" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="open in a new window">particularly from the EU</a>) are forcing companies to make their devices less consumable. In this case, devices will likely start shipping with more readily replaceable batteries, so that the entire product doesn't need to go once the batteries die. </p>
