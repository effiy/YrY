<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I was sad last fall when I read and recommended Jaya Saxena&#8217;s <a href="https://www.eater.com/food-culture/911138/foodie-history-american-food-culture?src=longreads" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">final story</a> for <em>Eater</em>. Saxena is a food writer the <em>Longreads</em> editors have <a href="https://longreads.com/tag/jaya-saxena/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">followed</a> for years, and a journalist I&#8217;ve always counted on for food writing that both informs and entertains; she goes deep on aspects of <a href="https://www.eater.com/23991259/not-too-sweet-asian-dessert-memes?src=longreads" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">cuisine</a>, <a href="https://www.eater.com/23737639/benihana-be-the-chef-onion-volcano-shrimp-tails-performance-anxiety?src=longreads" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">dining</a>, the <a href="https://www.eater.com/22866863/future-of-food-drone-delivery-apps-ghost-kitchens?src=longreads" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">restaurant industry</a>, and <a href="https://www.eater.com/2019/12/3/20974732/authentic-food-definition-yelp?src=longreads" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">food culture</a> in a way that&#8217;s relatable and unpretentious. (She&#8217;s also the series editor of <em><a href="https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-best-american-food-and-travel-writing-2025-bryant-terryjaya-saxena?variant=43747534012450" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Best American</a> <a href="https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-best-american-food-and-travel-writing-2024-padma-lakshmijaya-saxena" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Food and Travel Writing</a></em> anthology; you can <a href="https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-best-american-food-and-travel-writing-2026-jose-andresjaya-saxena?variant=44759076732962" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">preorder the 2026 edition</a> now.) So I was glad to hear, earlier this year, that Saxena and other former <em>Eater</em> writers were cooking up something new.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Where did you grow up?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I was born and raised in New York City, and aside from a brief stint in New Orleans for college, I’m still here. Except now I’m over in Queens instead of my hometown of Manhattan.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What places feel like home?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Aside from my actual apartment and the one I grew up in: Various NYC beaches, the backyard of my local bar, a punk show, Ray’s Pizza &amp; Bagel Cafe on St. Mark’s Place, and one specific rock in Central Park.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Other than family members, who or what has shaped you the most?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For high school I went to a Quaker school in Manhattan, and though I don’t consider myself religious in any way, Quaker values began shaping my future politics then. It’s where I first started learning about the value of consensus-based community decision making, and given that this was in the early 2000s, anti-war and anti-violence politics. I even remember a teacher making the case that cars should be banned in Manhattan. It was a big influence on my politics and value systems.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What is your favorite time of day?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">To work, right after I wake up, around 8 a.m., when my mind hasn’t filled with the noise of the day. Otherwise, early evening when it’s still light out, and everything is just beginning.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What are you <em>really</em> good at?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Untangling necklaces.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s the best gift you’ve ever received?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One year my wife bought me a Cameo of the cow from <a href="https://a24films.com/films/first-cow" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><em>First Cow</em></a>, whose handler fed her a treat in my honor. The video is one of my most prized possessions.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Describe your favorite meal.</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Of course as a food writer this is tripping me up, because are we talking about a single great meal I’ve experienced, or a dish I could eat every day? If it’s the former, it’s the time my wife and I walked into a random seafood counter in Valencia, Spain, and spent the night eating sardines in garlic oil and sparkling wine from ceramic cups. If it’s the latter, my mom’s mac and cheese.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Sound or silence? (And if sound, what sound?)</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I’m a city kid, so too much silence scares me. I need the background noise of people on the street to get anything done.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Where do you do your best thinking?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the shower or walking around listening to music.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What journey—physical, creative, intellectual, or otherwise—has meant the most to you?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 2018, my wife and I traveled with my dad to his hometown in India. It was the first time he had been back since before I was born, and being able to experience that with him was so important. We also ate absolutely the best kebab I’ve ever had in my life. Maybe I should have put that as my favorite meal.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Where do you like to read?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On the subway.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s the last rabbit hole you disappeared into?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">My friend recently introduced me to the case of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Judy_Smith" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Judy Smith</a>, a woman who went missing during a trip to Philadelphia and was discovered murdered in the woods outside Asheville, North Carolina. I have now spent a lot of time reading conspiracies about what happened to her.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Name three publications you enjoy reading these days.</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em><a href="https://defector.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Defector</a></em> has fantastic culture coverage, I love every issue <em><a href="https://cakezine.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Cake Zine</a></em> puts out, and some of the best money I’ve spent recently is on the monthly print issue of <em><a href="https://membership.theonion.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Onion</a></em>.&nbsp;</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s one longread that you can’t stop thinking about?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This piece in <em>The New Yorker</em> by Lauren Collins <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2025/06/09/how-a-hazelnut-spread-became-a-sticking-point-in-franco-algerian-relations" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">about El Mordjene</a>, an Algerian hazelnut spread that white French people are being very racist about. It’s just such a good exploration of how something innocuous becomes the center of a culture war. I was also thrilled to find the spread at an Egyptian grocer in my neighborhood, and sorry white French people, it <em>is</em> better than Nutella.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What was the last book you read?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I had somehow never read <em>Interview With the Vampire</em> by Anne Rice, but after watching the TV series my friends and I decided to read it together.&nbsp;</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What piece of nonfiction are you most proud of writing?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“<a href="https://www.eater.com/24162871/food-makes-you-gay" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Food That Makes You Gay</a>,” for <em>Eater</em>. I had editors that really trusted me to follow my instincts, and I managed to thread together some things I had an inkling were related. I’m also just glad I could get a full throated defense of bodily autonomy and the right to change one’s body in a piece that started out talking about straight men being afraid to eat bananas in public.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s your most reliable way to get creatively unstuck?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Walk away from the computer. I just need to do something else, anything else, for a while.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Who’s a writer you turn to when you need some inspiration?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Reading just about anything will definitely help me, but I love Helen Rosner’s food writing. And I tend to listen to a lot of music when I need inspiration, so as far as lyricists go, probably Aimee Mann, Fiona Apple, Joni Mitchell, Prince, and Paul Simon.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What words do you overuse?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I write pretty conversationally, so I sprinkle in a lot of “just” and “of course” and “perhaps” that I usually have to go back and delete.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s your favorite guilty pleasure?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am a firm believer that there is no such thing as a “guilty” pleasure (unless it’s like, continuing to read <em>Harry Potter</em> after J.K. Rowling has made it clear that doing so supports her transphobic work). No one should feel guilty about doing something that brings them joy. But in terms of indulgences that make little sense to others, I love a Hershey’s Cookies &#8216;N&#8217; Creme bar.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What superpower would you like to have?&nbsp;</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Generously, the power to instantly heal others. Selfishly, the power to have three cocktails and sleep through the night.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What animal or nonhuman being do you most identify with?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Any time I see a photo of a magpie standing on its nest made of shiny, pretty things, that’s me.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>If you have a free solitary hour in your day, what do you typically do?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Unfortunately, I’m probably looking on my phone. I have a very dumb “project” where I am trying to train my Instagram algorithm to show me the absolute worst videos out there. But I am trying to get better about doing things like taking a walk to the river, or practicing a new hobby (my latest one is trying to learn some card tricks).</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What five items would you place in a time capsule?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A copy of <em>The God of Small Things</em> by Arundhati Roy, a bootleg Knicks champion shirt, a fortune teller fish, a bottle of 4711 cologne, and a DVD of <em>Velvet Goldmine</em>.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What does your writing space look like?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You should know for this photo I am standing directly against my bed, which my cats sit on all day and scream at me from. You should also know half the time I’m not even here, and instead writing with my laptop on the couch.</p>
