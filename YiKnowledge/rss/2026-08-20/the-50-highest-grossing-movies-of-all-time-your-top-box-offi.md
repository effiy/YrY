<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After&nbsp;<em>Avengers: Endgame</em> and&nbsp;<em>Avatar</em> duked it out for bragging rights as the all-time highest-grossing movie in 2019, the world was hit by the COVID-19 pandemic, which effectively shuttered theaters (some of them never to reopen), and this list seemed safe for the time being. But in late 2021, <em>Spider-Man: No Way Home</em> blew everyone away and nostalgia-bombed its way into the top 10.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just a few months later, the decades-in-the-making sequel&nbsp;<em>Top Gun: Maverick</em> also shocked the world by swooping past the $1 billion mark, and another half year later,&nbsp;<em>Avatar: The Way of Water</em> proved James Cameron is the box office GOAT by shooting all the way up to the No. 3 spot. And with the addition of global hits <em>Inside Out 2</em> in 2024 and <em>Zootopia 2</em> in 2024, Disney now lays claim to a whopping eight of the top 10 highest-grossing films of all time (nine, if you count its joint ownership of <em>Titanic</em> with Paramount).</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 2023, <em>The Super Mario Bros. Movie</em> surprised everyone by jolting into the top half of this list in just its first six weeks of release, ultimately landing at No. 16, and most were ready to call it a year. Then Barbenheimer fever hit, and before we knew it, we had a new champion that was not only the highest-grossing film ever solo-directed by a woman, but also the highest-grossing film in Warner Bros. studio history. 2025 was another big year, with three films — including James Cameron&#8217;s third <em>Avatar</em> movie, <em>Fire and Ash</em> — entering the top 20, and in 2026, after <em>Toy Story 5</em> cracked the top 50, Christopher Nolan&#8217;s <em>The Odyssey</em> and <em>Spider-Man: Brand New Day</em> joined in quick succession, with the latter becoming the second-fastest film ever to reach the $1 billion milestone — it&#8217;s already sitting at No. 12 all time — and both still in theaters and climbing as of the latest update.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For the list below, we&#8217;ve included global box office performance, as well as domestic numbers and release dates. We included dollars earned in re-releases, and in each of our descriptions, we look at where the film stood record-wise at the time of its run and dive into things like critical and audience reception.&nbsp;<strong>We&#8217;ll be here to track the progress of new blockbusters and regularly update this list of top box office performers. So keep your eyes here, and check in with our <a href="https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/weekend-box-office/">weekly weekend box office wrap-ups</a>.</strong></p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $2.924 billion worldwide / $785.2 million domestic (including re-releases)<br />Release Date: December 18, 2009</strong><br /><br />The world had to wait some 12 years for James Cameron to follow up the biggest film of all time with what would become <em>the new biggest film of all time</em>. Nobody believed he was going to surpass <em>Titanic</em>’s numbers with this tale of an alien planet and the paraplegic Marine who teams up with its inhabitants in the battle for Unobtanium. But he did. At the peak of a 3-D reemergence, aided by the filmmaker’s usual technological gamesmanship (and higher ticket prices), <strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/avatar"><em>Avatar</em>&#8216;</a></strong>s seven straight weekends at No. 1 led to over $595 million at the North American box office, ultimately becoming the highest domestic earner ever and the only film ever to earn $2 billion outside of the U.S. and Canada, making it the world&#8217;s highest grosser at the time. Nearly 10 years later, <em>Avengers: Endgame</em> snatched the crown, but with an&nbsp;<em>Avatar</em> sequel on the way in 2021, the now Disney-owned-and-rebranded 20th Century Studios decided to re-release the film in China and pushed it back into pole position.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $2.799 billion worldwide / $858.4 million domestic (including re-releases)<br />Release Date: April 26, 2019</strong><br /><br />As the journey that began in 2008 with <em>Iron Man&nbsp;</em>was coming to an end, audiences who had been holding their breath after perhaps the biggest cliffhanger since <em>The Empire Strikes Back&nbsp;</em>could not wait to buy their tickets&nbsp;— and did they ever <em>buy those tickets&nbsp;</em>when they finally could. Opening weekend for <em><strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/avengers_endgame">Avengers: Endgame</a></strong> </em>in April 2019&nbsp;surpassed <em>Infinity War</em>’s year-long record by nearly $100 million. In just eight days, the film had grossed a half billion domestically. One by one, the records fell, leading many to speculate when, not if, <em>Avatar</em>&#8216;s previous record ($2.787 billion) would fall. But in the era of the modern blockbuster, even a record-breaker can be front-loaded and spend only three weeks atop the charts. After just six weeks of release,&nbsp;<em>Endgame</em>&nbsp;was&nbsp;about&nbsp;$73 million away from dethroning&nbsp;<em>Avatar</em> — substantial ground to make up. but Marvel and Disney re-released the film on June 28, 2019 with new goodies over its end credits. Over the weekend of July 19, 2019 — its 13th week of release — <em>Endgame</em>&nbsp;squeaked ahead&#8230; at least for a while. With a 2021 re-release in China,&nbsp;<em>Avatar</em> did ultimately take back the crown, but it doesn&#8217;t diminish the incredible achievement of&nbsp;<em>Endgame</em>.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $2.33 billion worldwide / $688.5 million domestic<br />Release Date: December 16, 2022</strong><br /><br />For 13 years, the arguments about whether or not James Cameron’s <em>Avatar</em> maintained a cultural footprint strong enough to draw in the crowds again only increased. The counter-argument of “Don’t bet against the king” turned out to be the right one. Despite having just the 38th-highest opening weekend at the time, <em><strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/avatar_the_way_of_water">Avatar: The Way of Water</a></strong></em> benefited around the world from word of mouth that another theatrical event was upon us. Spending seven weeks atop the box office (the same as its predecessor) and 15 in the top 10 (one more than the original), the sequel to the biggest film of all time erased all doubts. Once again, Cameron made the most expensive film ever and came out profitable on the other side, securing his place to have three of the four biggest spectacles ever made.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $2.27 billion worldwide / $23.3 million domestic (including re-releases)<br />Release Date: Jan. 29, 2025 (China), Feb. 14, 2025 (US)</strong><br /><br />The biggest international release of all-time is not <em>Avatar</em> or <em>The Avengers</em> or even <em>Star Wars</em>. It belongs to an animated film about a young boy and his demon-fighting powers. The original 2019 film <em>Ne Zha</em> was barely a blip in the states ($3.69 million) but amassed over $719 million in China (or over 99% of its global gross). <em><strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/ne_zha_ii">Ne Zha II</a></strong></em> then broke all of China’s box office records besting <em>The Force Awakens</em> ($936.7 million) in just 11 days. It passed <em>Inside Out 2</em> to become the highest-grossing animated film ever in only three weeks and became the first animated and non-American film to gross $2 billion.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $2.265 billion worldwide / $674.4 million domestic (including re-releases)<br />Release Date: December 19, 1997</strong><br /><br />James Cameron makes expensive movies. <em>The Abyss</em>, <em>Terminator 2</em>, and <em>True Lies</em> were all the most expensive movies of their time upon release. In 1997, Cameron blew out the budget again, and this time there was worry he may have gone too far. Though delayed from July until December,&nbsp;<em><strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/titanic">Titanic</a></strong></em> nevertheless became a global phenomenon the likes of which the box office had never seen at the time. After 15 straight weeks at No. 1, 14 Oscar nominations, and ultimately 11 statuettes, <em>Titanic</em>, its stars, and its signature song were ingrained in the hearts and tear ducts of the world, and the movie would hold the all-time box office record for 12 years — until Cameron would eclipse himself once again with <em>Avatar</em>.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Box Office: $1.066 billion worldwide / $423.3 million domestic<br />Release Date: July 7, 2006</strong><br /><br />Everyone mocked the concept of Disney turning one of their classic rides into a feature-length film. Well, some $300 million and an Oscar nomination for Johnny Depp’s portrayal of Captain Jack Sparrow later, we were looking at a franchise with a modicum of respect. At least, for a little while. Critics went from disdain for the concept to disdain for its epic length and earnestness in the span of just two films, with the original movie&#8217;s score of 79% dropping to 53% on the Tomatometer for the sequel. But audiences went the other direction, giving <em><strong><a href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/pirates_of_the_caribbean_dead_mans_chest">Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man&#8217;s Chest</a></strong></em>&nbsp;a 38.6% boost in domestic&nbsp;earnings and an 84.2% boost internationally. It was Disney’s first $100-plus million opening ($135 million to be precise), and the studio has had dozens more since then. From 2006 until <em>Toy Story 3</em> was released in 2010, <em>Dead Man&#8217;s Chest&nbsp;</em>was the highest-grossing domestic release in Disney’s history.</p>
