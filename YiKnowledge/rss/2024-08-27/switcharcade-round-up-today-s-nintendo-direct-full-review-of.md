<h3><strong>Reviews &amp; Mini-Views</strong></h3>
<h3><strong>EGGCONSOLE Star Trader PC-8801mkIIsr ($6.49)</strong></h3>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328024" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/eggconsolestartraderpc8801mkiisr.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p>It always comes down to two questions with these non-translated <em>EGGCONSOLE</em> releases. First, how is the game itself? Second, can it be enjoyed without understanding Japanese? <em>Star Trader</em> is an interesting game, though not what I would call a <em>great</em> one. Falcom fused a Japanese-style adventure game format with some side-scrolling shoot &#8217;em up stages, and it doesn&#8217;t do either of those things amazingly well. The adventure game side at least has some nice art, and it&#8217;s interesting to see a shoot &#8217;em up try to tell a story in this way. You&#8217;re mostly talking to people and picking up quests, the successful completion of which will earn you money you can use to upgrade your ship. This is important, because you&#8217;ll need to keep that cash coming in to stay on top of the action segments.</p>
<p>As for the shoot &#8217;em up parts&#8230; well, the PC-8801 wasn&#8217;t very good at scrolling the screen smoothly. So what you get here is a very choppy experience, and one that I&#8217;m not sure would even be all that great if things were moving at a normal clip. I&#8217;m not sure which part of the game is meant to serve the other, but in the end Star Trader is more interesting than it is good. Which naturally brings us to the second question. As you might expect, the adventure game segments are text-heavy and require some informed input from the player to achieve the best outcomes. If you can&#8217;t understand them, you&#8217;re not only missing out on half of the game, but you&#8217;re likely hobbling yourself for the other half because you&#8217;ll fail to earn enough credits to keep your ship up to date. You can brute force this one better than some <em>EGGCONSOLE</em> releases, but it&#8217;s not going to be a good time.</p>
<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328254" height="720" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/eggconsolestartrader-2.jpg" width="1280" /></p>
<p><em>Star Trader</em> is an interesting piece of gaming history, showing a developer working outside of the lane we usually associate them with. Unfortunately, what joy could be had from poking at this curio is severely dampened by the fact that there is a ton of Japanese text in here that most Western players won&#8217;t be able to read. You might still get something out of messing around with it, but it&#8217;s hard to recommend it with much vigor.</p>
<p><strong>SwitchArcade Score: 3/5</strong></p>

<p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-328255" height="562" src="//cdn.toucharcade.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/cryptcustodian.jpg" width="1000" /></p>
<p>A top-down action-adventure game about a recently-deceased cat named Pluto who, after a bad meeting with the Afterlife Guardian, is banished from the palace of the afterlife and sentenced to eternally clean. Dang. Explore, fight enemies with your broom, meet weird characters, battle bosses, expand your abilities, and so on. You know how this kind of game works. You know what? This is pretty good. If you enjoy this genre, I&#8217;d say you should give it a go.</p>
