## Action recommendations

1. **Five dimensions**: requirements + evaluation + SLA + commercial + legal; none missing
2. **Anchored to business problem**: ask the business question before writing the RFP; not sloganeering
3. **Requirements**: functional + non-functional + integration + data; do not omit
4. **Evaluation rubric**: quantified scoring + weights + judges; not by gut feel
5. **SLA**: availability + latency + support + security; not vague
6. **Commercial**: pricing + billing + renewal + exit; do not omit
7. **Legal**: data ownership + audit + cross-border + termination; do not omit
8. **MVP option**: must tag mandatory vs optional; don't mix
9. **Don't lock-in**: keep requirements open; allow vendors to propose alternatives
10. **No bias**: evaluation rubric is public; no black box
11. **Proof**: POC + references + cases; not empty
12. **Budget**: TCO + hidden costs; not unit price
13. **Link with vendor eval**: RFP + evaluation co-built
14. **Link with vendor relationship**: RFP + relationship co-built
15. **Link with vendor security admission**: RFP + security co-built
16. **Link with budget**: RFP + budget co-built
17. **Link with build vs buy**: RFP + decision co-built
18. **Link with tech selection**: RFP + selection co-built
19. **Toolchain**: RFP template + evaluation form + contract template
20. **Publicly queryable**: RFP-related parties can look it up; not hidden
21. **Periodic review**: evolution updates; not one-shot
22. **First principles**: why RFP is necessary; worst consequence of not doing it
23. **Inversion thinking**: how much can direct procurement solve; if solvable, don't introduce an RFP
24. **Second-order thinking**: second-order consequences after the RFP (lock-in / cost / compliance / extensibility) 
25. **Occam**: RFP the simpler the better; cut redundant steps
