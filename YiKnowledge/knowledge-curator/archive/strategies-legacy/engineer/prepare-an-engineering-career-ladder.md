# I want to build an engineering career ladder

> **As an** engineer, **I want to** prepare an engineering career ladder, **so that** launch is safe. 

## Summary

- Career ladder = contract; not stacked titles
- Five dimensions: capability + impact + autonomy + scope + governance
- Publicly queryable; not vague
- Periodic review; not one-shot
- Dual track: management + technical
- Not only output; look at impact + scope
- Link with mentor + 1on1 + retrospective
- Link with compensation + equity + promotion
- New hires read first; not verbal
- First principles / inversion / second-order / Occam

## Action recommendations

1. **Five dimensions**: capability + impact + autonomy + scope + governance; no missing dimension
2. **Publicly queryable**: everyone can view and look up; not vague
3. **Periodic review**: half-year / yearly review; not one-shot
4. **Dual track**: management + technical; do not force management
5. **Not only output**: impact + scope + governance; not only PR count
6. **Link with mentor**: mentor guides ladder growth
7. **Link with 1on1**: 1on1 tracks ladder progress
8. **Link with retrospective**: retrospective runs ladder retrospective
9. **Link with compensation + equity + promotion**: ladder drives promotion
10. **New hires read first**: not verbal; clear expectations
11. **Per-level rubric**: clear behavioral indicators; not vague
12. **Cross-level contrast**: clear differences between adjacent levels; no confusion
13. **Cross-department alignment**: same ladder across departments; no drift
14. **Promotion process**: nomination + review + decision; no black box
15. **First principles**: why a ladder is necessary; worst consequence of not writing it
16. **Inversion thinking**: how much can be solved with 1on1 + documentation; if solvable, do not introduce a ladder
17. **Second-order thinking**: second-order consequences after the ladder (growth consistency / recruiting / trust / retention)
18. **Occam**: simpler ladder is better; cut redundant levels
