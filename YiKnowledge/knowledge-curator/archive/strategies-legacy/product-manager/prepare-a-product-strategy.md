## Action recommendations

1. **Five dimensions**: vision + market + differentiation + path + measurement; no missing dimension
2. **Business-value driven**: prioritize by business growth + market share + profit + user value; not sloganeering
3. **Vision**: product vision + user value + long-term goal + business goal; do not omit
4. **Market**: target market + user segmentation + competitors + industry trends + opportunity window; do not omit
5. **Differentiation**: core moat + unique value + blue ocean / red ocean + Porter's five forces + business model canvas; do not omit
6. **Path**: now / next / later + second curve + roadmap + milestones + priorities; do not omit
7. **Measurement**: north-star + business metrics + user metrics + financial metrics + retrospective; do not omit
8. **Not one-shot**: progress gradually from vision -> market -> differentiation -> path -> measurement; no skipping
9. **Not report-ized**: reports are only the start; not the end
10. **Not sloganeering**: every principle must have landing evidence; not vague
11. **Versioned**: strategy has versions; evolution is traceable
12. **Link with product-roadmap**: strategy + roadmap co-built
13. **Link with product-launch-checklist**: strategy + launch co-built
14. **Link with product-analytics**: strategy + measurement co-built
15. **Link with go-to-market**: strategy + GTM co-built
16. **Link with technical-vision**: strategy + tech co-built
17. **Link with pitch**: strategy + fundraising co-built
18. **Toolchain**: product-strategy-framework / blue-ocean-strategy / porter-five-forces / business-model-canvas / now-next-later-roadmap / second-curve / OKR / North-Star-Metric
19. **Publicly queryable**: everyone can look up the strategy; not hidden
20. **Periodic review**: evolution updates; not one-shot
21. **First principles**: why must have a product strategy; worst consequence of not doing it
22. **Inversion thinking**: how much can be solved by intuition alone; if solvable, do not introduce a heavy strategy
23. **Second-order thinking**: second-order consequences after the strategy (cost / risk / growth / business)
24. **Occam**: the simpler the strategy the better; cut redundant steps
