<p>There is a lot of excitement and some trepidation among <em>The X-Files</em> fans about <a href="https://deadline.com/2026/02/ryan-coogler-x-files-reboot-hulu-pilot-order-daneille-deadwyler-1236733363/">Ryan Coogler's reboot</a> for Hulu, currently in production. While we're waiting, Hulu just released Chris Carter's revamped (and R-rated) director's cut of the 2008 standalone spinoff film <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X-Files:_I_Want_to_Believe"><em>The X-Files: I Want to Believe</em></a>, distinguishable from its predecessor with a slightly different title: <em>The X-Files: I Want to Believe–Vrach Frankenshteyn</em>.</p>
<p><strong>(Minor spoilers below but no major reveals.)</strong></p>
<p><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X-Files:_I_Want_to_Believe"><em>I Want to Believe</em></a> received mixed reviews at its release and didn’t exactly light up the box office, but it still grossed $68 million against its $30 million budget. By 2015, the project led to the return of <em>The X-Files</em> as a limited-run TV series, with the six-episode season 10 debuting in 2016. Mulder and Scully were reunited 14 years after the events of season 9 as the FBI reopened the X-Files under Assistant Director <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Skinner">Walter Skinner</a> (Mitch Pileggi). The response was strong enough to warrant a follow-up ten-episode season in 2018, focusing on Mulder and Scully’s search for their missing son, William.</p><p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/culture/2026/08/r-rated-directors-cut-of-x-files-film-restores-chris-carters-original-horror-vision/">Read full article</a></p>
