<p>This week, Ars <a href="https://www.youtube.com/live/IxS_FIhhH28">hosted a live discussion</a> with Ross Scott about the Stop Killing Games movement and <a href="https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/05/bill-to-keep-online-games-playable-clears-key-hurdle-in-california/">its ongoing efforts</a> to ensure that games remain playable even after the original publisher shuts down the online servers undergirding them.</p>
<p>Scott talked with Ars Technica Senior Gaming Editor Kyle Orland, saying said he felt the situation around long-term game access was already "intolerable a decade and a half ago" and that he "kept hoping someone else would kick this off, [someone] more experienced or knowledgeable, but it felt like nothing was happening." Scott said he felt making the effort to organize a movement against the practice was like "[drawing] the short straw on this, essentially."</p>
