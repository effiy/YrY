# First Month Goals — 30-60-90 Day Plan

> **As a** new hire, **I want to** have a structured 30-60-90 day plan with clear milestones, **so that** I know what success looks like at each stage and can calibrate my progress.

> The 30-60-90 plan is a conversation starter with your manager, not a rigid contract. It should be reviewed and adjusted at each milestone. The goal is alignment on expectations, not micromanagement.

## Summary

- First 30 days: Learn — understand the product, codebase, team, and processes. Ship small changes. Build relationships.
- Days 31-60: Contribute — own a small feature end-to-end. Participate in design discussions. Review others' code.
- Days 61-90: Lead — own a medium feature independently. Propose improvements. Mentor the next new hire.
- Each phase has a checkpoint conversation with your manager to calibrate progress and adjust goals
- Key principle: The plan measures learning velocity, not output volume. A new hire who deeply understands one system is more valuable than one who shallowly touches ten.

## Core viewpoints

### 1. The 30-60-90 plan is a learning scaffold, not a performance contract

The plan exists to give you a clear path to productivity, not to create a pass/fail test. If you're ahead of the plan, great — accelerate. If you're behind, the plan helps identify the bottleneck (unclear documentation? complex codebase? not enough support?). Adjust the plan, not the judgment.

### 2. Depth before breadth in the first 90 days

It's tempting to touch every system to show engagement. Resist. Deep understanding of one system (you can explain its architecture, data flow, and failure modes) is more valuable than shallow familiarity with ten. Choose one system to go deep on in your first 90 days.

### 3. Relationships are output, not input

Meeting people is not a checklist item to complete. The goal is to build working relationships: you know who to ask about authentication, who understands the deployment pipeline, who can explain the business logic. These relationships are the scaffolding for your future productivity.

### 4. The plan should be visible to your manager and buddy

Share your 30-60-90 plan as a living document. Update it weekly. This gives your manager and buddy visibility into your progress and makes it easy for them to help. Hidden plans help no one.

## Key info

### 30-Day Milestones (Learn)

**Product & Domain:**
- [ ] Can explain the product's core value proposition to a stranger
- [ ] Understand the primary user personas and their workflows
- [ ] Can navigate the product as a user for all core features

**Technical:**
- [ ] Can set up the full development environment from scratch
- [ ] Understand the high-level architecture of all major projects
- [ ] Deep-dive into one project: can trace a request from entry to response
- [ ] Have shipped and merged 5+ PRs (mix of bug fixes, small features, docs)

**Team & Process:**
- [ ] Know every team member's name and role
- [ ] Understand the team's development workflow (branching, PR, review, deploy)
- [ ] Have attended all recurring team meetings at least twice
- [ ] Have a weekly 1:1 cadence with your manager

### 60-Day Milestones (Contribute)

**Technical:**
- [ ] Have owned a small feature end-to-end (design → implementation → testing → deployment)
- [ ] Have participated in 2+ technical design discussions
- [ ] Have reviewed 10+ PRs from teammates with substantive feedback
- [ ] Understand the testing strategy and can write tests independently
- [ ] Can debug production issues using logs and monitoring tools

**Team & Process:**
- [ ] Have identified and proposed 2+ process or tooling improvements
- [ ] Have documented something that was previously tribal knowledge
- [ ] Are participating in on-call rotation (if applicable) or shadowing it

### 90-Day Milestones (Lead)

**Technical:**
- [ ] Have owned a medium feature end-to-end independently
- [ ] Have proposed and implemented a technical improvement (performance, reliability, DX)
- [ ] Can review PRs in at least 2 different codebases with confidence
- [ ] Understand the technical debt landscape and have opinions on priorities

**Team & Process:**
- [ ] Are mentoring the next new hire (buddy role)
- [ ] Have led at least one team meeting or technical discussion
- [ ] Have a clear understanding of the next 6 months of technical priorities
- [ ] Have written or significantly improved one piece of team documentation

### Checkpoint conversations

| Milestone | When | Agenda |
|---|---|---|
| 30-day check-in | End of week 4 | What's working? What's confusing? Are goals on track? Adjust 60-day plan. |
| 60-day check-in | End of week 8 | Are you contributing independently? What skills need development? Adjust 90-day plan. |
| 90-day review | End of week 12 | Probation review (if applicable). Set 6-month goals. Transition from onboarding to regular performance cycle. |

## Action recommendations

1. **Write your 30-60-90 plan in week 1**: Share with your manager and buddy. Make it a living document you update weekly.
2. **Schedule checkpoint conversations now**: Book 30/60/90-day check-ins with your manager in week 1. Don't let them slip.
3. **Track your contributions**: Keep a running list of PRs merged, features shipped, docs improved, and people met. This is your evidence at the 90-day review.
4. **Be honest about struggles**: If you're behind on a milestone, say so early. The plan is a diagnostic tool, not a judgment.
5. **Celebrate milestones**: Each phase completed is a real achievement. Acknowledge it.

## Anti-patterns

- **No plan at all**: Winging it for 90 days and hoping for the best. Ambiguity breeds anxiety.
- **Too many goals**: A 30-60-90 plan with 30 items per phase. Pick 5-7 key milestones per phase.
- **Output-only goals**: Only tracking PRs and features shipped. Include learning goals (understand X system, build relationship with Y team).
- **Set and forget**: Writing the plan in week 1 and never updating it. The plan should evolve as you learn.
- **Comparing to other new hires**: Everyone onboard at different speeds based on prior experience, domain knowledge, and team complexity. Your plan is yours.
