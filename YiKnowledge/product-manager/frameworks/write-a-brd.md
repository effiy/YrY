## Core viewpoints

- **A BRD is a business case, not a product spec. It answers "should we invest?" not "how do we build?"** The audience for a BRD is the executive who controls the budget, not the engineer who writes the code. Every section of the BRD should serve the purpose of answering the executive's implicit question: "why should I allocate resources to this instead of the 10 other proposals on my desk?" If the BRD does not answer that question in the first page, it has failed.

- **The BRD's risk section is the most important section and the one most teams treat as boilerplate.** A risk register that says "technical risk: the technology may not work" is a placeholder, not an analysis. The risk section should be specific: "the third-party API we depend on has a documented 99.5% SLA, which means 3.6 hours of downtime per month. Our mitigation is a cached fallback that serves stale data during outages." Specific risks with specific mitigations build executive confidence; generic risks erode it.

- **Business domain classification is not just a taxonomy — it is a constraint on scope.** When a BRD touches 3 business domains (e.g., after-sales, finance, data), the complexity is not linear — it is multiplicative. Each domain boundary is a potential source of conflicting terminology, incompatible data models, and misaligned stakeholder expectations. A BRD that spans multiple domains must explicitly address the boundary integration strategy, not just list the domains.

- **The BRD's scenario section is the reality check that separates feasible proposals from fantasy.** A BRD that describes a feature in the abstract ("the system will automatically generate reports") is a wish. A BRD that walks through a concrete scenario ("the service manager opens the tablet at 8:30 AM, sees 12 pending repair orders, and the system generates a prioritized work plan") is a proposal that can be evaluated. Scenarios force the author to confront the messy details of real-world usage.

- **A BRD generated by AI is a first draft, not a final document.** The YiAi BRD Agent can produce a structurally complete BRD in minutes, but it cannot validate the business assumptions, interview the stakeholders, or verify the domain terminology. The PM's role shifts from author to editor: the AI generates the scaffold, and the PM fills in the evidence, validates the assumptions, and ensures the business case is grounded in reality. The AI accelerates the writing; the PM ensures the thinking.

## Key info

- **BRD structure template (10 sections)**: (1) Executive Summary — one page, answers "should we invest?", includes problem statement, proposed solution, expected ROI; (2) Business Context — industry background, market trends, competitive landscape; (3) Problem Statement — specific pain points, quantified impact (revenue loss, efficiency loss, customer dissatisfaction); (4) Proposed Solution — high-level description of the product/system, key differentiators; (5) Business Domains — which domains are involved (after-sales, finance, data, etc.), domain boundary integration strategy; (6) Scenarios — 3-5 concrete user scenarios walking through real-world usage; (7) Success Metrics — how will we measure success (OKRs, KPIs, target values); (8) Risk Register — specific risks with specific mitigations, confidence levels; (9) Resource Estimate — people, budget, timeline, dependencies; (10) Recommendation — go/no-go with rationale. The Yi-family BRD template follows this structure.
- **Business domain classification (9 domains)**: after-sales (repair, maintenance, warranty), data (analytics, reporting, data governance), finance (billing, payments, cost management), hr (recruiting, onboarding, performance), infra (cloud, networking, security), legal (compliance, contracts, IP), marketing (campaigns, leads, CRM), sales (pipeline, deals, forecasting), supply-chain (procurement, logistics, inventory). A BRD that spans multiple domains must include a domain boundary integration strategy addressing: terminology conflicts (same word means different things in different domains), data model differences, stakeholder alignment, and process integration.
- **BRD risk register template (5 categories)**: (1) Technical risk — "the third-party API has 99.5% SLA = 3.6 hours downtime/month, mitigation: cached fallback"; (2) Market risk — "competitor X launched a similar product in Q2, mitigation: differentiate on Y feature"; (3) Resource risk — "key engineer Z is on leave in Q3, mitigation: cross-train engineer W"; (4) Adoption risk — "target users are resistant to new tools, mitigation: phased rollout with champion users"; (5) Compliance risk — "data handling must comply with PIPL, mitigation: legal review by date X." Each risk must have: specific description, likelihood (high/medium/low), impact (high/medium/low), mitigation, and contingency (what if mitigation fails). The most common BRD failure: generic risks ("technical risk: the technology may not work") without specific mitigations.
- **BRD review and approval workflow**: (1) Author drafts BRD using YiAi BRD Agent (AI generates scaffold, PM fills in evidence); (2) Peer review — another PM reviews for clarity, completeness, and business case strength; (3) Domain expert review — domain experts (finance, legal, etc.) validate domain-specific assumptions; (4) Engineering review — tech lead reviews technical feasibility, resource estimates, and risk mitigations; (5) Executive review — decision-maker reviews for strategic alignment and resource allocation; (6) Approval — go/no-go decision with conditions. The review cycle should take 1-2 weeks; a BRD that takes >4 weeks to approve has too many stakeholders or unclear decision authority.
- **BRD → PRD transition**: The BRD answers "should we build this?" The PRD answers "how do we build this?" The transition from BRD to PRD should happen within 2 weeks of BRD approval. The BRD's scenarios become the PRD's user stories; the BRD's success metrics become the PRD's acceptance criteria; the BRD's risk register becomes the PRD's technical constraints. A BRD that is approved but never converted to a PRD is a BRD that was written for the approval, not for the build.
- **Yi-family BRD practices**: YiAi BRD Agent — generates BRD scaffold from user requirements, uses domain knowledge from YiKnowledge, produces 10-section BRD in minutes. Current BRD library: 20+ generated BRDs across 9 business domains. BRD generation prompt: role + domain knowledge + 3 few-shot examples + user requirements. The BRD Agent is the primary BRD creation tool; all BRDs are stored in `brd/brd-documents/` for reference and reuse.

## Action recommendations

1. **Structure**: background → business domain → scenario → targets → risks → terminology → references → interface → acceptance; write by template, do not drift.
2. **Business domain**: must be classified into one of the 10 domains; cross-domain must be explicitly declared (e.g., after-sales + data); see `brd/domains/`.
3. **Scenario-ize**: must give concrete scenarios (users + trigger + steps + result); do not write abstract functions; see [scn-unified-ticketing.md](./../../brd/README.md).
4. **Measurable targets**: every target must carry a number (recall / hours saved / conversion rate); see `brd/brd-objectives/` + [okr-design-summary.md](../../product-manager/frameworks/okr-design.md).
5. **Risk register**: technical / compliance / data / cost / launch risks must be registered + mitigation measures + owner; see `brd/brd-risks/`.
6. **Unified terminology**: cross-domain terminology must be checked against `brd/terminology/` to prevent conflicts; automotive / general terminology already exists.
7. **References**: countries / regulations / systems must be looked up in `brd/reference/`; cross-border business must fill in.
8. **Instance alignment**: before writing, first scan `brd/brd-documents/` 20+ already-generated BRDs to find similar cases.
9. **Generation prompt**: use [brd-generation-prompt.md](../../ai-engineer/methodology/prompts--brd-generation.md) to invoke the YiAi BRD Agent to generate a draft, then manually refine; see [adr-brd-agent-launch.md](../../tech-lead/decisions/yiai--brd-agent-launch.md).
10. **Review**: follow [requirement-review-process.md](../delivery/requirement-review.md); stakeholders include business owners + legal + security + engineering + design.
11. **Convert to PRD**: after BRD passes, convert to PRD on the engineering side; see [prd.md](../../knowledge-curator/templates/prd.md).

## Anti-patterns

- **The BRD as a solution in disguise.** When a BRD says "we need to build a real-time dashboard using React and WebSocket," it has already made technology decisions that should belong to the engineering team. The BRD should describe the business need: "the operations team needs to see inventory levels updated within 5 seconds of a change." The solution architecture is the engineering team's domain. A BRD that prescribes technology is a PRD wearing a BRD costume.

- **The BRD that confuses business goals with product features.** "Launch a mobile app" is not a business goal — it is a delivery vehicle. The business goal is "reduce customer wait time from 30 minutes to 10 minutes." The mobile app is one possible solution. When the BRD starts with the solution and works backward to justify it, the business case is a rationalization, not an analysis. The discipline: write the business goal first, then evaluate whether the proposed solution is the best way to achieve it.

- **Cross-domain BRD without terminology reconciliation.** When an after-sales BRD uses the term "ticket" to mean a repair order, and the finance domain uses "ticket" to mean a payment record, the BRD introduces ambiguity that will cause implementation errors. The BRD must include a terminology section that defines every domain-specific term, and cross-domain BRDs must explicitly reconcile conflicting definitions.

- **The BRD that ignores the "do nothing" baseline.** Every business case must compare the proposed investment against the alternative of doing nothing. If the cost of the problem is $50K per year and the proposed solution costs $200K, the BRD must explain why the $200K investment is justified despite the apparently unfavorable ROI. Perhaps the cost of the problem is growing, or the solution enables new revenue. Without the "do nothing" baseline, the BRD is advocacy, not analysis.

- **The BRD that treats stakeholder review as a signature exercise rather than a challenge process.** When stakeholders sign off on a BRD without reading it, they are approving the budget, not the analysis. The BRD review should be a structured challenge: the legal team questions the compliance assumptions, the finance team questions the ROI calculation, the operations team questions the feasibility of the timeline. If the BRD survives this gauntlet, the assumptions are robust. If stakeholders rubber-stamp it, the assumptions are untested.
